## I. General Checks

### Chapter Structure

- [ ] Opening has a hook rather than weather, scenery, or routine
- [ ] Middle advances the story: fast-paced genres need a visible event; slow-burn genres need at least a state change
- [ ] Situation changes—the world differs after the chapter
- [ ] Ending lands on the change rather than summarizing it

### Opening Check (First 300–500 Chinese Characters)

- [ ] Contains an attention-grabbing hook
- [ ] Does not begin with weather, scenery, or routine
- [ ] Introduces the protagonist quickly
- [ ] Makes the selling point or crisis visible
- [ ] Does not collide with the genre’s default opening formula. A hook does not guarantee differentiation; if the whole opening can move unchanged into any comparable book, it is homogeneous

### Chapter Advancement

- [ ] The chapter changes at least one of seven states: goal / risk / information / relationship / resources / identity / emotional alignment. Fast-paced Fanqie or wish-fulfillment fiction needs a visible event or payoff; only slow-burn or emotional fiction may pass with a small low-pressure change. Do not require a climax every chapter
- [ ] It is not padding. What state change would be lost if this chapter were deleted? Compress only if there is no answer
- [ ] Task bottlenecks matter: Does being blocked produce one of the state changes above?
- [ ] Strength of advancement fits the established genre, fulfillment pace, and comparables rather than a fixed universal character count

### Information Delivery

- [ ] No long block of setting exposition
- [ ] Information follows conflict and the premise is delivered through events
- [ ] Premise load remains controlled—no more than three new concepts per chapter

### Scene Check

- [ ] Scene has a goal—what does the character want?
- [ ] Scene has an obstacle—what blocks them?
- [ ] Scene has a change—what differs afterward?
- [ ] Characters do things rather than feel things
- [ ] No removable paragraphs

### Chapter Ending

- [ ] Lands on a change
- [ ] Contains at least one crisis, decision, discovery, or reversal
- [ ] Does not summarize
- [ ] Pulls the reader to the next page

### Language

- [ ] No empty lyrical passages
- [ ] No several consecutive paragraphs with the same emotion
- [ ] Dialogue fits character identity; different people speak differently
- [ ] Punctuation rhythm fits tone and voice: The text is neither reduced entirely to periods nor littered randomly with question and exclamation marks. Main text, including dialogue, contains no remaining `……`/`——`; hesitation or interruption has been replaced with action, short sentences, commas, or periods
- [ ] Emotion is grounded in action rather than “he felt sad”
- [ ] Outside title lines, no chapter or writing metadata. Do not use terms matching `第[一二三四五六七八九十百千万两0-9]+章|上一章|上章|前一章|本章|这一章|前文|后文|伏笔|细纲|读者`. When continuity is needed, use an event anchor the character can perceive or a relative time. Genuine in-story reading/discussion of “Chapter X” and actual reader-identity contexts are exceptions

### Serial Continuity

- [ ] No forgotten promises or foreshadowing
- [ ] No sudden dump of many new premise elements
- [ ] Foreshadowing advances
- [ ] The story engine is still running

### Filler Detection

These signals suggest possible filler:

- The entire chapter’s dialogue conveys no new information
- One emotion is described for more than three paragraphs
- Setting description exceeds 500 Chinese characters without advancing the plot
- A character recalls earlier events without a new perspective
- More than two consecutive chapters contain no conflict
- Procedural detail or a task bottleneck that can be deleted without loss = filler; compress or remove it

## II. Long-Form Checks

### Golden Three-Chapter Check

- [ ] Does the first 500 Chinese characters of Chapter 1 contain a hook?
- [ ] Does the protagonist appear in Chapter 1?
- [ ] Does Chapter 1 contain an event rather than pure setup?
- [ ] Does Chapter 2 escalate by deepening the conflict?
- [ ] Does Chapter 3 give a reason to continue?
- [ ] Do the first three chapters contain at least two payoffs?
- [ ] Is the world free of long expository blocks?
- [ ] Does every chapter end with a reason to continue? A weak hook or stage goal is enough for low-pressure or transition chapters; strong suspense is not required

### Pacing Check

- [ ] Have the most recent five chapters made clear progress?
- [ ] Does the interval between payoffs exceed 5,000 Chinese characters? Evaluate intervals in high-pressure and advancement chapters; exclude low-pressure, relationship, and information-organizing chapters
- [ ] Are there more than two consecutive chapters without conflict? Breathing chapters are normal; the red line is two consecutive chapters with pressure ≤1 or emotionally convergent adjacent chapters
- [ ] Does the volume vary chapter intensity rather than remaining uniform? Are low-pressure + transition chapters restrained, totaling no more than about 15%?

### Character Check

- [ ] Does protagonist behavior fit the characterization?
- [ ] Do supporting characters have presence?
- [ ] Does the villain’s stature fit the current stage?

### Five-Dimensional Scoring Standard

Score each dimension from 0–100 and select a refinement strategy from the results.

### Dimension 1: Core Consistency

Check whether crucial conflicts, actions, and motivations remain consistent.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Motivation changes abruptly without setup | critical | Add the event that triggers the change |
| Central conflict is inconsistent | high | Revise the conflict premise retrospectively |
| Crucial action contradicts characterization | high | Adjust the action or add an explanation |
| Secondary conflict is forgotten | medium | Resolve or de-emphasize it |

### Dimension 2: Surface Originality

Check whether sentence structure and wording are sufficiently original and avoid formulaic expression.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Copied wording creates an unnatural voice | medium | Rewrite only when genuine AI tone exists; retain normal source sentences |
| Heavy use of AI-marker words | high | Replace with concrete description |
| Repeated sentence pattern | medium | Vary the expression |
| Overly literary description—ornament piled up, formal register, chained metaphors | medium | Make it conversational or action-based. Action-based does not mean strings of three-to-five-word fragments; revised narration should still favor longer comma-linked sentences |

### Dimension 3: Format Consistency

Check whether paragraphs break naturally by dramatic unit or shot, whether subject/name rhythm is natural, and whether opening and ending formats align.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Mechanical breaks by character count or overly dense subjects | medium | Rearrange by dramatic unit: name at paragraph opening, pronoun or omission inside, name again at pivotal turns |
| Chapter length misses target | **high** | During writing/outline repair, return to the detailed outline and restore planned plot points before expanding. When removing AI tone from existing text, do not add plot |
| Inconsistent formatting for dialogue and description | low | Standardize formatting |

### Dimension 4: Readability

Check for verbosity, AI tone, vague summaries, and formulaic rhetoric.

| AI-Tone Trait | Identification | Fix |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Vague summary | “He finally understood”; “Everything was left unspoken” | Delete and replace with action |
| Formulaic rhetoric | “It was as if fate were playing a joke on him” | Delete or replace with concrete description |
| Stacked similes | Repeated “like / as if / seemingly / just as,” with every image explained through comparison | Retain only the few comparisons that serve the narrative; turn the rest into action, objects, sounds, or consequences |
| Emotion label | “He felt a wave of sadness” | Replace with behavior |
| Idle interiority | Internal monologue adds no information, repeats one emotion, or restates what readers know | Compress only the idle material; do not reduce monologue that adds information, a decision, or an emotional turn based on sentence count |

### Dimension 5: Logical Coherence

Check whether sentences and paragraphs flow and whether premises conflict.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Contradictory premise | critical | Find and unify the premise |
| Timeline error | high | Mark and correct the timeline |
| Inconsistent character information | high | Build and consult a character dossier |
| Broken causal chain | medium | Add a transition |

### Refinement Strategies

Select a strategy from the five-dimensional scores:

| Primary Problem | Strategy | Explanation |
|----------|------|------|
| Low core consistency | rewrite | Rewrite relevant passages around the central conflict |
| Excess length | compress | Remove material that does not advance plot |
| Meaningless bottleneck | compress | Preserve the change created by the bottleneck and compress the procedure |
| Strong AI tone | de_ai | Replace prohibited words and rewrite sentence patterns |
| Many small problems | polish | Refine language details |

### Bidirectional Review of Reader Contract and Endgame Reserves

First ask, “Is it free of reader turnoffs?” Then ask, “Is it compelling?” **Safe does not mean compelling.** Absence of a contract violation means only that the text avoids a hazard; emotional fulfillment must still be strong. Use three risk labels: contract-safe—consistent with the opening promise, protagonist retains key choice, and ownership of gains is clear; needs reinforcement—may dilute the spotlight or advance too quickly but can be repaired through setup, exchange, or cost; contract violation—the central selling point is confiscated or an irrelevant character takes ownership of anticipation, requiring an outline revision.

- [ ] Reader contract: Does the chapter/volume fulfill the opening promise, or hand the central selling point to an institution, supporting character, or accident? Risk: contract-safe / needs reinforcement / contract violation.
- [ ] Protagonist agency: Review causal authority + settlement authority, not page time. At every critical node, ask who decides why the event happens, who makes the irreplaceable choice, who bears or chooses the crucial consequence, and who receives the central gain/recognition/power. Supporting characters may perform local actions but cannot silently take ownership of anticipation.
- [ ] Anticipation debt: Is the promise repaid, delayed with interest, or swallowed by explanation, procedure, or worldbuilding?
- [ ] Endgame reserves: Does this unit use an endgame trump card that should not unlock yet—nemesis / truth / identity / special-advantage ceiling? Does it push a progression line near its ceiling with no next step? If both answers are no, approve it. Multiple gains from one victory and several lines rising together are strong design, not excessive speed.
- [ ] Ownership of fulfillment: Is ownership of anticipation settled according to the promise? Voluntary surrender becomes a red light only when “negative long-term net benefit + reduced control/causal authority” occur together. Is the strategic concession’s exchange for greater control, future gains, power, or information visible?
- [ ] Pacing connection: After a climax or fulfillment, does the story allow a brief low-pressure period and small visible gains/rewards instead of mechanically adding an immediate crisis? Do new elements pay transition debt without evading old promises?
- [ ] Genre-specific risk: In contract-fulfillment wish fulfillment or power fantasy, does the protagonist repeatedly create disasters through stupidity or avoidable incompetence and leave others to clean them up, damaging causal authority and settlement trust?
- [ ] Reverse review: If the “safe explanation” is removed, does the emotion become stronger? If the risky event remains, does it create stronger anticipation, cost, or payoff?
- [ ] Combined line: Do contract, protagonist agency, anticipation debt, and endgame reserves reinforce one another rather than merely remaining “safe”?

## III. Short-Form Checks

### Suffering–Payoff Pacing

Short-form-specific check: Is the distribution of suffering and payoffs appropriate?

```
理想分布：虐1 → 虐2 → 虐3 → 爽1(小) → 虐4 → 爽2(大)
禁忌分布：虐1 → 虐2 → 虐3 → 虐4 → 虐5（无爽点，读者流失）
禁忌分布：爽1 → 爽2 → 爽3 → 爽4（无铺垫，爽感疲劳）
```

Rules:

- Every scene or section should change at least one of risk, information, relationship, resources, decision, action, or reader understanding—or make the consequence of the previous choice land
- Pressure may come from a task, evidence, waiting, craft, space, or relationships; do not treat direct conflict as a fixed character-count quota
- Place the largest payoff where prior causes converge and the protagonist’s choices and evidence support it; leave room for consequences. Do not move it to a fixed percentage
- The ending should contain an emotional landing, local fulfillment, or clear aftermath; do not require one universal ending method

### Dialogue-Density Check

| Check | Passing Standard | Signal for Review |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Dialogue function | Changes information, strategy, power, relationship, or the next action | Removing the dialogue changes nothing in the scene |
| Dialogue-free scene | Driven by tasks, objects, evidence, space, waiting, or craft | Contains only explanation and repeated emotion, without visible action or new information |
| Dialogue rhythm | Turn length follows character power and immediate pressure | Consecutive micro-questions and answers, repeated paraphrases, or an action tag for every line |
| Dialogue share/count | Observational data only within the same genre and platform | No cross-genre passing threshold; do not add dialogue merely to raise the share |

### Protagonist Leverage and Emotion Check (Humiliation / Revenge Fiction)

| Check | Passing Standard |
|--------|----------|
| Is the protagonist’s composure visible? | Show it through evidence gathering, choice, delayed response, or resource allocation; do not force signature gestures such as holding a glass, adjusting a suit, or spinning a pen |
| Is emotional loss of control justified? | Frequency and placement follow accumulated pressure; it must change action, relationship, or cost and is not banned by percentage |
| Where does protagonist–villain contrast come from? | Leverage, judgment, consequences, or information gaps; the villain need not be hysterical |
| Does line length express power? | Length, interruption, silence, and evasion change with leverage; no fixed ratio |
| Is interrogative dialogue necessary? | Use only when a question forces a choice, exposes contradiction, or changes a relationship; no minimum count |

### Evidence-Chain Integrity (Revenge / Humiliation Fiction)

| Check | Standard |
|--------|------|
| Is evidence released as judgments change? | Every release changes action or the reader’s model; a complete evidence chain may land in one scene with no required number of releases |
| Is every item of evidence set up? | A clue must appear earlier |
| Does villain reaction follow causality? | Driven by leverage, misjudgment, and loss rather than repeating “smug, then humiliated” |
| Is the final evidence the most decisive? | The last item changes the entire understanding |
| Is “time-bomb” evidence needed? | Use only when advance planning fits the protagonist’s ability, timeline, and evidence-gathering conditions |

### Quick Reference: Reader Turnoffs / Identification / Astonishment / Opening / Ending / Emotion / Anticipation

> See `anti-ai-writing.md` for detailed classifications, identification methods, and fixes for reader turnoffs.

| Check | Standard |
|--------|------|
| Wish fulfillment is not satisfying | Demonstrate the special advantage clearly |
| Suppression has no purpose | Every suppression serves a later eruption |
| Villain’s ending is unrelated to protagonist | Villain’s death must result from protagonist action |
| Economic / power system collapses | Keep the premise consistent and anchor it to ordinary people |
| Female lead/supporting woman becomes irrational | Normal characterization is allowed; stale formulas are not |
| Reader expectation remains unsatisfied too long | Fulfill it at the right time or introduce a new expectation |
| Protagonist behavior is incomprehensible | It must be understandable and emotionally accessible |
| Abrupt joke breaks the atmosphere | Atmosphere > abrupt joke, except in comedy-first fiction |
| Hook is unresolved | Unresolved hook = failed ending |
| Layered astonishment | Point → network → depth; do not write only point-level surprise |
| Stair-step astonishment | Rise in steps, not a straight line |
| Broad astonishment | The entire relationship network should react |
| Conflict/anomaly in first 50 Chinese characters | It cannot be background setup |
| Central conflict known in first 100 | Mandatory |
| Opening emotional intensity | ≥7 on a 1–10 scale |
| Ending is concrete | Action / dialogue / image; no summary or reflection |
| Ending has an aftertaste | Readers still want more |
| Ending emotional intensity | Suffering ≥8, payoff ≥7, healing ≥6 |
| Proportional emotional release | Repay suffering proportionally; connect the villain’s ending to protagonist action |
| Anticipation management | Maintain “two long, one short”; keep the core selling point throughout |
