# Reader Profiling System

## Nine-Dimension Profile

| Dimension | What it includes | Example |
|------|----------|------|
| Platform | Qidian, Fanqie, Jinjiang, Zhihu, or Qimao | Qidian = paid and demanding; Fanqie = free and mass-market |
| Tags | Genre tags establish reader expectations | A “system novel” creates an expectation of progression |
| Reading volume | Low- and high-volume readers have different tolerances | High-volume readers recognize formulaic patterns; low-volume readers are more responsive to familiar formulas |
| Gender market | Male- and female-oriented markets have substantially different aesthetics | Male-oriented fiction emphasizes gratifying payoffs; female-oriented fiction emphasizes emotion |
| Age | Readers aged 16–25 and 25–35 identify with different characters | University students born after 2005 versus workers born in the 1990s |
| Region | Major cities versus lower-tier markets | Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen versus third- and fourth-tier cities |
| Education | Influences expectations for prose and logic | University student versus a reader who left school after junior high |
| Class | Working class, middle class, or wealthy | Determines which forms of upward mobility resonate |
| Social role | Student, stay-at-home mother, employee, or freelancer | Determines which daily experiences feel recognizable |

Example composite profile: “Qidian + historical officialdom + succession struggle + knowledge-copying advantage + high reading volume + female + born after 2005 + second-tier city + university student + middle class” -> fast pacing, satisfying political maneuvering, a sweet but intelligent romantic line, and fresh cultural references.

## Profile-Linked Elements Method (70/20/10)

Build reader identification through three layers:

- **70% nostalgic elements:** Situations resembling experiences the reader has lived through.
- **20% current life:** Pressures the reader currently faces.
- **10% trending topics:** Currently popular references and subjects.

### Sources for 70% Nostalgic Elements

| Source | Content | Applicable genres |
|------|------|----------|
| Shared generational memory | Internet cafés, MP3 players, and non-mainstream subculture of the 1990s-born generation | Urban rebirth and period fiction |
| Popular culture | Classic film and television, games, and music | Fan fiction and transmigration |
| School life | College entrance examinations, dormitories, and student clubs | Campus fiction |
| Childhood memories | Events in villages or residential compounds | Rural and urban slice-of-life fiction |

### Sources for 20% Current Life

| Source | Content | Applicable genres |
|------|------|----------|
| Work pressure | 996 schedules, hypercompetition, and side jobs | Urban and workplace fiction |
| Relationship status | Blind dates, marriage pressure, and choosing not to have children | Romance and social-condition fiction |
| Financial pressure | Mortgages and reduced consumption | Urban and farming-management fiction |
| Social anxiety | Social withdrawal, living alone, and fear of social interaction | Healing and slice-of-life fiction |

### Sources for 10% Trending Topics

| Source | Content | Applicable genres |
|------|------|----------|
| Social trends | News events and social issues | Urban and realist fiction |
| Online memes | Memes, reaction images, and popular phrases | Lighthearted fiction |
| Film and television tie-ins | Popular screen works that stimulate interest in a genre | Trend-driven work |

## Quick Reference: Platform Reader Profiles

### Qidian Male-Oriented Readers

- Core needs: gratifying payoffs, progression, and overwhelming victories
- Tolerance: a slow opening is acceptable, but not beyond five chapters
- Payoff preference: overwhelming strength > victory through strategy > good fortune
- Red lines: cuckoldry, prolonged frustration, or a heroine so powerful that she eclipses the hero
- Gold standard: one payoff every three chapters and one major climax every ten chapters

### Fanqie Readers

- Core needs: fast pacing, strong payoffs, and low cognitive load
- Tolerance: readers leave after three chapters without a payoff
- Payoff preference: public vindication > impressive display > fortunate opportunity
- Red lines: slow development, prolonged suffering for the protagonist, or plots requiring substantial interpretation
- Gold standard: a payoff in every chapter and a major event within 3,000 Chinese characters

### Jinjiang Female-Oriented Readers

- Core needs: nuanced emotion, strong characterization, and identification
- Tolerance: the opening may be slow, but characterization must be established
- Payoff preference: sweetness after pain > reversal and vindication > reversal of social identity
- Red lines: a naïve heroine, an abusive hero who never earns reconciliation, or morally incoherent framing
- Gold standard: visible character arcs and layered emotion

### Zhihu Salt-Selection Readers

- Core needs: emotional impact, reversals, and identification
- Tolerance: the first 500 Chinese characters determine retention
- Payoff preference: shocking reversal > healing after pain > recognition of real-life experience
- Red lines: preaching, moral coercion, or a forced happy ending
- Gold standard: emotional tension plus one explosive reversal

### Qimao Readers

- Core needs: fast pacing, strong plot, and no dragging
- Tolerance: the first three chapters determine survival
- Payoff preference: reversal of fortune > public vindication > gaining recognition
- Red lines: affected literary prose, slow pacing, or weak payoffs
- Gold standard: immediate opening conflict with no flat stretch

### Ciweimao Readers

- Core needs: anime culture, light novels, and entertainment
- Tolerance: the opening must be interesting
- Payoff preference: entertaining > referential humor > referential humor with surprise
- Red lines: excessive seriousness, excessive realism, or no anime-culture elements
- Gold standard: references and humor with occasional depth
