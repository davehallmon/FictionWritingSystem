# Source-Text Reader Psychology Analysis Criteria

> **Purpose**: reader psychological needs, genre-longevity patterns, cross-site adaptation, platform differences, title/blurb techniques, and identification management. Use as a reference during topic selection and positioning.
> **Voice**: imperative—turn every principle into an executable constraint on topic, positioning, title, copy, or opening.
> **Related material**: see analysis-short-patterns.md for short-form structural function chains and analysis-short-mechanics.md for core-mechanism observations.

## Use as Deconstruction Criteria

This file began as a writing-side playbook phrased as “how to write.” During deconstruction, `story-short-analyze` uses it as a
**comparative benchmark**: compare the source text with the standard patterns described here to identify which one it uses and how effectively it uses it. **Do not** use this file to write a new work during analysis.

From a reader’s perspective, these are industrial conventions for popular short fiction such as Fanqie short stories, Story Club tales, wife-chasing redemption, social-condition stories, rebirth, and revenge against abusers. They are the comparative source for “reusable structure / reversal design / genre formulas” and similar report sections.

## Reader Psychological Needs and Genre Alignment

### Infer Reader Psychology from Hits

- Extract core plot elements from hits → define reader profile → infer psychological needs → validate from both directions.
- A hit sustains one emotion throughout (*Battle Through the Heavens*: “Do not despise a poor youth” from start to finish); weaker works scatter several emotions in parallel.
- Unify smaller emotions—progression, revenge, treasure hunting—beneath the primary emotion.
- Validate inferred needs against the target reader’s actual life circumstances.
- Pay special attention to elements outsiders call “toxic” but core readers love.

### Genre → Reader Profile → Deep Need

| Genre | Reader profile | Three-layer psychological need |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Apocalyptic rebirth | People who despair of upward mobility | Reversed order (desire for power) + insecurity (survival anxiety) + bargain capture (maximum return for minimum effort) |
| War-god live-in son-in-law | Middle-aged men under financial pressure | Pain of powerlessness → anger at not being understood → shame and inferiority → excuse that rationalizes endurance → explosive reversal |
| General web fiction | — | Deep need for recognition/understanding/respect; public humiliation reversal is only the shell |

### Excavating Deep Needs

- Enter the audience’s collective unconscious: what do they love, hate, desire, and avoid?
- Reader profiles differ by genre; identify the reader before designing emotion.
- Before writing, ask what target readers most lack and want in real life.
- The depth of the emotional need sets the work’s ceiling; deeper needs generate stronger resonance.

### Emotional Roots

Emotion arises from “unfairness” and “why.” Unequal treatment → readers naturally perceive injustice → emotion follows.
Urban example: modest career success → father wants to start a business → mother wants to buy the younger brother a marital home → injustice arises naturally.

## Genre-Longevity Patterns

- Judge longevity through current samples, not historical experience treated as present fact.
- Use the current scan/analyze process or user-selected samples to confirm continuing target-platform response.
- Classify the stage: novelty / maturity / aesthetic fatigue.
- During novelty, prioritize creative directions; during maturity, reliably deliver boundary expectations; during fatigue, provide a new entry point.
- Trend-following may reuse structural functions, never plot content.
- If the stage cannot be confirmed, treat it as mature: satisfy boundary expectations conservatively and limit minor innovations to three.

### Three Eras of Change

| Era | Characteristics |
|------|------|
| Free era | No fixed style; inspiration from traditional literature and comics |
| Gamified era | Levels + stages + Bosses + instances + equipment, driving consumption |
| Life-oriented tendency | Story over progression, lived detail, contemporary relevance, anti-gamification; validate applicability with current platform samples |

## Cross-Site Genre Adaptation

The same genre requires radically different treatment across sites because audiences differ. Never apply Site A’s sample directly to Site B; calibrate expectations, pacing, and taboos with target-platform samples.

### Core Platform Differences

| Platform | Core driver | Poorly suited genres |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Ciweimao | Meme play and characterization, grounded in anime fan fiction/mobile games | Mass-participation and high-martial-power streams generally fail |
| Qidian (non-light-novel) | Normal plot progression, pacing, identification | Pure meme play is hard to sustain |
| Qidian light novels | Meme play + characterization + pacing | Requires continual inventive play |
| Fanqie | Strong emotion, gimmicks, direct gratification | Genres needing slow setup |

### Audience Differences

| Platform | Reader traits |
|------|----------|
| Qidian | High filtering cost; more patient and tolerant of slower pacing |
| New media (30+) | Prefers near-real stories in which life’s blows are normal |

**Key**: identical methods perform differently across platforms; adapt to the platform.

### Ciweimao Essentials

Validate fandom samples first | Build imaginative ideas on subculture | Let community acceptance set logic | Favor daily life over combat | Calibrate meme demands with current rankings/comments.

### Fanqie Title-Test Renaming Strategy

Create A/B titles related to the special advantage / first story unit / main line / a conservative variant near the original / a slightly exaggerated traffic-seeking variant. Filter them through target-platform rules and user requirements.

## Genre Boundaries

### Clear vs. Unclear Boundaries

| Type | Characteristics | Suitable case |
|------|------|----------|
| Clear boundaries | Mature audience and known method | Strong sample validation and reliable delivery |
| Unclear/innovative | Uncertain audience and exploratory method | High risk; reduce length and number of innovations |

### Three Cores of Fantasy

- **Identification**: a fantasy dynasty supports identification far better than cyberpunk; bodily transcendence and physical blows are easier to inhabit than exchanged spell blasts.
- **Anticipation**: preserve it throughout and reader loss remains slow.
- **Recognition**: identification with and approval of the protagonist produces loyal readers who rarely leave. “Small virtues may be absent; great righteousness must remain.”

### Four Elements of Urban Romantic Ambiguity

1. **Women**: male-oriented urban fiction requires women.
2. **Ease**: readers seek relaxation and pleasure, not relentless bitterness.
3. **Interaction**: character interaction plus the reader’s “secret” interaction with the protagonist.
4. **Privacy**: conceal the protagonist’s ability as a secret shared with readers.

### Special-Advantage Design

Tie it closely to the protagonist’s life or profession. A doctor receiving a medical manual is coherent; invisibility is forced. Connect it to the main line, allow skills to improve, derive varied effects from one skill, and avoid continually adding new advantages.

### Historical-Fiction Red Lines

- Core position: “nationalist enlightened-ruler equality”; inconsistent positioning causes severe rupture.
- Historical web fiction is not history. Readers, not factuality, are the first consideration.
- Follow contemporary authoritative consensus when judging historical figures, especially evaluations of the Chairman; do not cross that line.
- Manage gratification thresholds: excessively rapid early family/faction advancement leaves nowhere to go.

## Identification and Artificiality

### Identification

Identification heavily overlaps gratification. With low identification, none of the protagonist’s actions feel satisfying. More identification increases gratification, which increases the desire to continue.

### Sources of Artificiality

Immortal fantasy that feels un-immortal, martial fiction without chivalry, or a fractured aesthetic such as scientific research in an immortal-fantasy world. Remove artificiality by making the world feel real rather than made of paper.

### Why Fan Fiction Has Strong Opening Retention

Its world is already solid and initial identification is high. Exception: comedy and meme fiction prioritize amusement, make gratification secondary, use an observer viewpoint, and need less identification.

### Effective Ranking Scan

Rules: 1) run the current scan/analyze workflow for recent target-platform samples; 2) exclude top works with built-in fandom; 3) analyze reusable commonalities in opening structure. Establish the core conflict within 300 Chinese characters; quality separates decisively by 10,000.

### Commercial Understanding

Personal-expression rule: non-core-emotion expression stays below 5% and never interrupts narrative rhythm. Commercialization does not mean deliberately disgusting readers. Every personal expression must serve the core selling point and never exist independently of the main plot.

## Title Techniques

### Identity Method (About One-Third of Titles)

| Category | Formula | Example |
|------|------|------|
| High-status identity | [Immortal Emperor/Demon God/tycoon] parents + [contrasting action/objective] | “A Martial-God Father and Tycoon Mother—What’s Wrong with Coasting to Victory?” |
| Dramatic identity contrast | [Low-status person] does [unexpected task] for [high-status person] | “You Were Hired as a Page—Did You Take the Imperial Examination for Your Young Master?” |
| Suggestive identity relation | Relationship implies romantic ambiguity | Risky but strongly attracts traffic |

### Other Methods

| Method | Core | Platform |
|------|------|----------|
| Hidden-identity reveal | Hidden identity gradually exposed | Use cautiously if current target-platform samples are saturated |
| Elevated naming | Evocative words | Qidian |
| Antonym method | Positive–negative contrast creates curiosity | General |
| Main line + payoff | Core main plot + gratification signal | Qidian |

### Platform Naming Differences

| Platform | Style | Key principle |
|------|------|----------|
| Fanqie | Gimmick first, traffic first | Title determines 80% of the traffic ceiling |
| Qidian | Light contrast + special advantage + main line + gratification | Content must match the title |

## Blurb and Copy Taxonomy

### Five Basic Types

| Type | Structure | Effect | Best use |
|------|------|------|------|
| Four-part success | Situation → conflict → action → success | Safety and explicit victory | Most general |
| Three-part action | Situation → conflict → action (cut off) | Suspense | Career-focused work |
| Two-part mystery | Situation → one reversal/mystery | Curiosity | Mystery/cerebral/niche |
| Four-part failure | Situation → conflict → action → failure | Pain and anger | Wife-chasing redemption |
| Three-part success | Situation → turn → success | Concise and brisk | Sweet/light fiction |

### Combination Strategies

- Success + mystery = safety + curiosity.
- Action + success = anticipation before satisfaction.
- Failure + mystery = suspense after pain.

### Copy Essentials

- Show the heroine’s agency; weak agency increases attraction and retention risk.
- Readers need safety; at least imply that the protagonist will succeed.
- A mismatch among copy, title, and content drives readers away.
- Do not stack too much mystery; excessive riddling becomes incomprehensible and lacks safety.

## Quality Checklist

> Verify each item after topic positioning. All must pass.

- [ ] **Explicit reader profile**: describe in one sentence who target readers are and what real life denies them.
- [ ] **Deep need aligned**: the genre’s core emotion matches a deep reader need rather than author self-indulgence.
- [ ] **Genre longevity assessed**: current samples establish the stage; historical popularity is not treated as current fact.
- [ ] **Platform adapted**: writing is calibrated to the target, not transplanted from Site A to Site B.
- [ ] **Boundaries confirmed**: available material, knowledge, and length support the genre.
- [ ] **Title captures within three seconds**: within platform naming rules, it communicates the core selling point or hook immediately.
- [ ] **Blurb has safety plus hook**: the right type supplies security and leaves suspense.
- [ ] **Identification without artificiality**: world and aesthetic are coherent, without fractures such as scientific research in immortal fantasy.
- [ ] **Title, blurb, content unified**: title’s selling point = blurb’s promise = delivered content.
- [ ] **Advantage tied to life**: it closely connects to the protagonist’s work or life rather than being pasted on.
