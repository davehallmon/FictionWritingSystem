# Opening Design: A Practical Guide to Starting Web Fiction

> Load this when launching a new book or writing the first three chapters. Use the decision tree to choose an opening type, then apply the genre template.

---

## Decision Tree

```
你的故事是？
|-- 女频言情
|   |-- 总裁豪门 → 用"被迫+极端环境"或"纪念日+白月光"
|   |-- 古代宅斗 → 用"被害+回归+身份反差"
|   +-- 青春校园 → 用"苦难叠加+遇见"
|-- 男频爽文
|   |-- 都市脑洞 → 用"中年危机+系统"
|   |-- 玄幻修仙 → 用"群众恐慌+主角报名"
|   +-- 动漫衍生 → 用"原作场景+系统选择"
|-- 现实题材
|   |-- 家庭伦理 → 用"场景冲突+被背叛"
|   +-- 行业文   → 用"专业场景+异常事件"
+-- 轻喜剧       → 用"吐槽式反应+认知错位"
```

> **The decision tree only illustrates one viable opening direction for each genre; none of these are mandatory formulas.** Each arrow marks one possible starting point, not the only answer available from a lookup table. Choose or invent an opening angle based on the protagonist, selling points, and comparable titles. Do not reuse the same structural formula across consecutive books in one genre—doing so creates a searchable opening fingerprint (the same principle as the physical-detail rule in writing-craft.md: copying or reusing it turns the result into a new template fingerprint). The “Genre Opening Templates” below work the same way: they are a menu, not a mold.

### Choosing an Opening Strategy

| Strategy | Execution | Best For |
|------|------|------|
| Crisis opening | Begin under life-or-death pressure | System fiction, fantasy, urban fiction |
| Mystery opening | Use suspense to hook curiosity | Mystery, mistaken identity |
| Reversal opening | Subvert reader expectations | Rebirth, transmigration, high-concept fiction |

## Golden First-Chapter Checklist

The first three chapters must pass every check below:

### Required Targets

- [ ] Start with the story’s most compelling, conflict-heavy moment
- [ ] Put the protagonist in one of three states: in danger / enviable / thrown into an unfamiliar environment
- [ ] Introduce the protagonist within 300 Chinese characters and deliver a payoff or anticipation point within 1,000
- [ ] Establish all three foundations within the first three chapters (characterization / entry point / special advantage)
- [ ] Make the first conflict consequential, and state beforehand what is gained by resolving it and lost by failing
- [ ] The first chapter must establish the protagonist’s goal and the story’s selling point

### Absolutely Avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|------|------|
| Long background exposition | An opening made entirely of “slow” material and no conflict makes readers leave immediately |
| Opening with weather or scenery | Unless the contrast is extreme |
| Introducing more than three major characters | Information overload |
| A prologue, preface, or introductory section | Unless it is narrative-driven, sufficiently brief, and tightly connected to Chapter 1 |
| Flashbacks, POV switches, memories, or dreams | Keep the primary narrative chronological |
| Detailed worldbuilding exposition | Wait at least until the first major story unit is complete |

### Conflict Pacing

- Integrate background into conflict (for example, use bystanders’ conversation to deliver context and conflict at once)
- Place relief between two tense scenes, but remember that relief does not mean no conflict
- Release background information in batches (information clusters). Prioritize: sense of crisis > characterization > hints of the special advantage > worldbuilding. Every release should serve the current emotional goal

### Sample Opening Structure (*Demon Slayer*)

| Stage | Content | Function |
|------|------|------|
| Setup | Selling charcoal in the snow + family life + helping people with his sense of smell + dialogue that introduces the world | Establishes the character from four angles and embeds the main plot |
| Turn | Massacre → sister becomes a demon → Giyu appears → pleading on his knees → clever counterattack | Compounds the crisis and creates motivation and a goal |
| Resolution | Leaves short- and mid-term goals + reinforces the mood with bleak scenery + runs toward the unknown with his family member | Launches the next chapter of the story |

## The Three Foundations and Core Templates

| Foundation | Content | Purpose | Deadline |
|------|------|------|---------|
| Characterization | Show the protagonist’s defining personality and circumstances | Build identification and empathy | First three chapters |
| Entry point | The protagonist’s first conflict or opportunity | Create urgency and momentum | Ideally Chapter 1 |
| Special advantage | Reveal the protagonist’s unique edge | Create anticipation and differentiation | First three chapters |

For a dilemma-driven opening, establish characterization first. For an advantage-driven opening, reveal the special advantage first. The entry point must connect strongly to the main plot.

### Template 1: Dilemma-Driven Entry

```
1. 主角处于不利处境（人设基点=共情入口）
2. 遭遇压迫/危机（切入点=紧迫感）
3. 金手指激活/展示（金手指=反转期待）
4. 初步解决问题但引出更大问题（钩子）
```

### Template 2: Advantage-Driven Entry

```
1. 主角已有独特优势但被低估/隐藏（人设+金手指合一）
2. 优势在某个场景中意外暴露（切入点）
3. 周围人的认知被刷新（震惊反应）
4. 暴露引来更大的关注/麻烦（钩子）
```

## Quick Reference: Common Opening Problems

| Problem | Symptoms | Fix |
|------|------|---------|
| Unclear information delivery | The protagonist’s location, actions, or motivation are unclear | Establish the setting and circumstances, clarify the goal, then begin the event |
| Protagonist lacks presence | Gets nervous around attractive people / indulges in pointless internal monologue | Give the protagonist presence and supporting characters distinctive traits |
| Missing emotion | Fights feel dull / readers lose interest after a few paragraphs | Weave emotion into action—fear, anger, determination |
| Dragging pace | Too many irrelevant characters appear / setup runs too long | Cut irrelevant interactions and enter the main plot quickly |
| Special advantage appears abruptly | It pops up like an entry in a diary | Trigger it through a crisis or dilemma |
| Setup chapters lose returning readers | The chapter title reveals that it is only setup | Use a dramatic title to “trick” readers into entering |

### Designing the Emotional Curve

Alternate rises and falls: transmigrates as an immortal (rise) → is only a nominal disciple (fall) → gets exhausting chores and takes the blame (fall to the bottom + point of resonance) → gains a special advantage (rise) → the advantage is mutually assured destruction (fall + drama) → how is this any different from life before transmigration? (bottoms out)

The key: support every fluctuation with concrete information; emotional resonance is essential; dramatize pivotal events on the page.

### Information Gaps and Anticipation

Anticipation formula: emotion + plot (characterization + motivation) + information gap. Leave out any one and the result is insufficient. Core loop: information → gain → astonishment, then transition briskly to the next useful plot beat.

### Three Paths to Anticipation

- **Career arc**: The premise is fresh; readers can foresee the outcome but not the process
- **Romance arc**: The character pairing is compelling, and the opening presents its appeal
- **Interwoven dual arcs**: The career and romance arcs advance together and affect each other

### Directions for Improvement

- **High-emotion opening**: Enter through a climactic event or another intense emotion such as revenge; major events cannot read like horseplay
- **Information and anticipation**: Every piece of information should create anticipatory value; pivotal information should make readers ask, “And then what?”
- **Goal-driven action**: State the protagonist’s goal clearly; fights must arise from that goal
- **Resonance**: Create empathy through the character’s circumstances and get readers on the protagonist’s side first

### Developing an Idea Correctly

- Idea validation rule: Every creative idea must become a concrete plot conflict rather than remain only a concept
- Keep the title, synopsis, and main text aligned (a title that promises “lying low” while the protagonist shows off violates consistency)

## Genre Opening Templates

> Each genre below offers only one fill-in-the-blank framework. **It illustrates a direction; it is not a mold to complete verbatim.** Turning the blanks directly into a draft makes two books in the same genre structurally identical sentence by sentence (the same location sentence → setting sentence → abnormal-action sentence → suspense sentence), which is exactly where homogenized openings come from. Use it to determine what the opening must accomplish—quickly introduce conflict, an identity contrast, or suspense. Vary the specific angle, sentence order, and release of information for every book according to its protagonist and comparable titles. Do not reuse one framework across consecutive books in the same genre.

### Urban High-Concept Fiction

Example: *My Yandere Moms Came Knocking* (*病娇妈妈们找上门*)

```
{城市名}。
{简短场景描述}。
{主角}{一个反常行为}。
{一句话解释为什么这么做，但留悬念}。
实际上，{真相，制造更大悬念}。
```

### Wealthy-CEO Romance

Example A: *Forbidden to the Bone* (*禁她入骨*)

```
{极端/异国地点}。{季节}。
{2-3个感官细节}。
{困境描写，1-2句}。
{主角身份交代}。
{一句话揭示谁害了她}。
```

Example B: *I Won’t Let You Bow Your Head Again* (*不愿再让你低头*)

```
出了{医院/法院/某场所}，{天气映射心情}。
{主角}{一个会改变现场的选择、台词、任务动作或后果；必要时才用身体细节}。
脑海中闪过{某人}的话——
"{冲击性的一句话}"
{主角的反应}。
{一个动作推动下一步}。
```

### Ancient Household Intrigue

Example: *Ten Years Ago, They Made Me Take My Sister’s Place in a Brothel* (*十年前他们要我顶替妹妹进青楼*)

```
{N年前，被害/被弃的事件}。
{亲人选择救别人不救主角}。
"{被弃时的伤人台词}"
{N年后，主角已{隐藏身份/实力}}。
{回归场景，制造身份反差}。
```

### Fantasy Cultivation

Example A: *Journey to the West: You Call Me a Monkey?* (*西游你叫我吗喽*)

```
"{一条震惊的新闻/传闻}"
"{群众反应/讨论}"
——安静——
{场景转换：学校/公司/日常}
{权威人物宣布一个危险任务}
{所有人退缩}
"我报名！"
{主角站出来，所有人震惊}
{暗示主角有隐藏优势}
```

Example B (anime derivative fiction)

```
{原作角色名}的声音{描述}
{原作角色向主角提出请求/挑战}
主角内心：{穿越者视角的分析}
系统/面板出现：
【选择一：{选项}】奖励：{xxx} 代价：{xxx}
【选择二：{选项}】奖励：{xxx} 代价：{xxx}
主角选择+理由。
```

### Youth and Campus Fiction

Example: *You Brought Midsummer and Carried Me Through Winter* (*你携盛夏，渡我寒冬*)

```
最{负面状态}那年，我遇到了{对方}。
我{被欺负}，被{家人伤害}，被{社会歧视}。
{对方}则{不良/叛逆行为列举}。
我走投无路。
"{交易/请求台词}"
"{对方的反应，出人意料}"
```

### Realistic Emotional Fiction

Example: *Five Years of Devotion, Settled in a Day* (*五年恩情，一朝两清*)

```
{对方}{重要事件}那天，是{我们的重要日子}。
{被忽视的场景细节，暗示等待}。
手机屏幕亮了："{敷衍的消息}"
{主角查看{朋友圈/聊天}——真相}。
{一句话内心独白，冷静但心碎}。
```

### Period Fiction

```
{主角}这会儿{状态}，{反应}。
明明{昨天在做的事}，一{闭眼/醒来}，居然就{穿越了}。
{记忆涌入——原主的悲惨经历}
"{情绪爆发——愤怒/不甘}"
利用{前世知识/穿越者优势}，{第一个行动目标}
```

## Unifying the Title, Synopsis, and Opening

- The title tells readers the special advantage and genre
- The synopsis covers only three things: the protagonist’s circumstances, what the special advantage can do, and a preview of the first payoff
- The opening must fulfill the title and synopsis’s promises
- Title warning: Three- or four-character titles that reveal no appeal are a major mistake. Do not confuse suspense with anticipation; tell readers directly what the protagonist can do

### Three Types of Gimmicks

| Gimmick Type | Approach | Key Point |
|----------|------|------|
| Event gimmick | The title names the event, and the story begins extremely quickly | Open with a familiar everyday event; enter it immediately without setting up transmigration or the special advantage |
| Special-advantage gimmick | Protagonist → dilemma → goal → first use of the advantage → modest result | Can be combined with the event approach: extend the event into the main plot → create a goal → introduce the advantage |
| Characterization gimmick (high complexity) | A standout characterization directly shapes the plot | Use only when that characterization can continuously drive plot and conflict; merely being “decisive and ruthless” is not distinctive characterization |

Once the gimmick hooks readers, connect it to the main plot immediately. As soon as the event ends, introduce a new goal.

## Matching the Special Advantage to the Characterization

| Characterization Type | Suitable Special Advantage | Mismatched Combination |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| Cheerful/comedic | Self-destruct type (showy payoffs pair well with banter) | Comedic + self-destruct advantage = mismatch |
| Ruthless | Traditional type (leveling, acquiring resources) | Cold-blooded + comedic advantage = jarring |

## Seven-Step Word Allocation

| Section | Length | Content |
|------|------|------|
| Worldbuilding + fact of transmigration + predecessor’s history | 300 Chinese characters | Establish the background quickly |
| First attention-grabbing mini-plot | 500 Chinese characters | Create the initial hook through an event |
| Early special advantage creates security | 400 Chinese characters | First demonstration of the advantage |
| A sense of danger emerges | 400 Chinese characters | A new threat appears |
| Crisis arrives | 500 Chinese characters | The central conflict begins |
| Use the special advantage directly | 400 Chinese characters | Solve the problem and complete the first payoff |

## Designing and Validating Selling Points

### Explicit and Implicit Layers of Selling Points

- Commercial selling points (gimmick, innovation, fresh special advantage) vs. literary selling points (style, prose craft, worldbuilding)
- A selling point drowned by the “ocean” requires more effort (Fanqie has the roughest waters, followed by Qidian)
- Even a strong brand or IP needs multiple layered selling points

### Five-Step Validation Method

1. Check the core hook, special advantage, and opening plot (“leveling up” and “wish-fulfillment fiction” cannot themselves be selling points)
2. Tailor the worldbuilding and plot around the core
3. Check the characterization contrast and selling points
4. Check the author’s distinctive approach
5. Tap into trending subjects or conversations

## Platform Differentiation

| Dimension | Fanqie | Qidian | Jinjiang |
|------|------|------|------|
| Core tradeoff | Pacing > identification | Identification > pacing | Characterization > plot |
| Opening strategy | Intense emotion + strong gimmick | Character groundwork + a slow burn is acceptable | Establish character appeal first |
| Anticipation | Dig deeply into the core emotion + mention it repeatedly | Lay groundwork across multiple arcs + foreshadow | Emotional progression itself creates anticipation |
| Payoff density | One every 2,000 Chinese characters | One every 3,000–5,000 Chinese characters | Emotional progression itself is the payoff |
| Pacing | Fast; it cannot stop | It may be slow, but cannot meander | Detailed, but never padded |

## Reference Loops

| Type | Loop Pattern |
|------|----------|
| Progression fiction | Show the advantage → create an information gap → reveal the gap → show off and astonish → collect the reward → set up what follows |
| Slice-of-life / romance-fluff fiction | Stable characterization + consistent plot flow = reliable reader anticipation |
| High-martial-arts / progression fiction | Train → find a master → compete; stable anticipation from the progression loop retains readers |

---

## Content Red Lines

- Suggestive content and morally questionable themes carry extreme risk. If you insist on writing them, use a foreign setting and notify your editor in advance
- Special rule for female-audience fiction: mutual chastity is the baseline; do not challenge it. As a workaround, reveal that the partners were “actually the same person (reincarnation/transmigration)”
