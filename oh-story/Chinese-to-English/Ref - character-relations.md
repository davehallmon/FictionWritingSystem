## Character-Relationship Types

Choose the relationship type from the table, then apply its principles.

| Type | Definition | Function | Example |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Conflict | Opposing interests or beliefs | Creates tension and advances plot | Nemeses, competitors |
| Alliance | Shared objective | Provides support and creates bonds | Comrades, mentor and student |
| Intimacy | Emotional connection | Creates vulnerability and emotional support | Lovers, family, siblings |
| Authority | Superior/subordinate or controlling relationship | Creates pressure and restricts action | Master, boss, supervisor |

**Rules**:

- Test every important relationship at least once through betrayal, sacrifice, or misunderstanding.
- Every relationship needs a changing arc: enemy to ally, ally to opponent.
- Do not make every relationship uniformly harmonious.
- A relationship must serve the plot, not exist only to provide sweetness or anguish.

## Core Method for Emotion-Driven Characterization

In emotion-centered fiction, plot serves characterization. Every event must deepen a character or advance the relationship; otherwise, it is ineffective.

### Construction Steps

1. **Define the core**: write the first defining feature that comes to mind, such as “a prince who wants the throne” or “a sickly prince.”
2. **Expand around the core**: answer why it exists, what happened in the past, how the environment shaped it, and what caused the personality.
3. **Extend plotlines**: write the plot inherent in the core. A goal creates a goal line; a wound creates a healing line.

### Rules

- A familiar plot paired with rich characters can escape a formulaic feel.
- Both leads need strengths and independent paths of change. One cannot shine while the other fades.
- A highlight is not only showing off. Any moment that makes a character unforgettable is a highlight, including devastating pain.
- Life is larger than romance. A character may long for love, but a life containing only love feels thin and cannot stand.

## Underlying Differences Between Male- and Female-Audience Romance

**Identify the target audience first, then choose the corresponding logic. Mixing them makes the story feel wrong to readers.**

### Male-Audience Romance Logic

| Dimension | Description |
|------|------|
| Core | “External-factor display”: the couple demonstrates how exceptional both people are |
| Three-step logic | “If only she were mine” → “someone this exceptional is mine” → “someone this exceptional loves me, so I must be even better” |
| Onlooker's thought | “I envy him for winning such an exceptional woman,” not “their romance is so sweet” |
| Heroines | Several women preferably like the protagonist; greater interest signals greater excellence |
| Essential role of partner | A “trophy”; the fundamental motivation is victory |

Design male-audience romance events around displaying excellence.

### Female-Audience Romance Logic

| Dimension | Description |
|------|------|
| Core | “Connection”: a unique, indestructible, highest-priority emotional bond |
| Requirement | “I love you as a person; appearance, wealth, and talent cannot replace this connection” |
| Purity | Once formed, the connection should gradually shed the influence of external factors |
| Both partners | Ideally each other's first love and mutually exclusive, preserving purity and uniqueness |
| Onlooker's thought | “Their romance is so sweet; I envy them” |

Design female-audience romance events around deepening the connection.

## Character Selection for Book/Game Transmigration

### Required Conditions

- The viewpoint protagonist must know the original world well; this is the main source of anticipation.
- Confirm arrival in the familiar world in one or two sentences. Do not spend more than a chapter without establishing where they are.
- Choose a transmigrated role with strong dramatic potential or conflict, such as the vicious female supporting character.

### Information-Gap Design

- Add suitable information gaps within the original world to give the viewpoint protagonist room to explore.
- Let knowledge of the original world exploit loopholes for extraordinary gains.

## Resolving a Romantic Battlefield

A romantic battlefield is a form of conflict. Control its intensity so love interests never develop irreconcilable hostility.

| Resolution | Operation |
|---------|------|
| Reconnect to the career plot | Convert rivalry into competition over who contributes more to the protagonist's goals |
| Insert a sudden career event | When confrontation peaks, make everyone stop infighting and unite against an external problem |
| Pass over briefly | At most, two people dislike each other, exchange a couple of barbs, and move on with restraint |

## Avoiding Pitfalls with a Sweet but Naive Character

### Six Pitfalls

1. Her mistakes cannot be deliberate or knowingly reckless.
2. Her mistakes cannot violate morality. A sweet innocent character's defining trait is childlike moral clarity.
3. She cannot condemn others from a moral high ground while failing the same standards herself.
4. After moralizing causes conflict with teammates, do not make those teammates apologize and praise her.
5. Correct pattern: she holds a higher moral standard → her actions meet it → this makes her distinctive and slightly naive.
6. A growth arc can place her in the criticized person's position, where she discovers that their choice was already the best available.

### Reverse Use

To create an antagonistic sweet-but-naive persona, give her all six failures so readers dislike her immediately.

## Two-Way Reversal for Character Change

Use this method to adjust characterization when a romantic relationship changes.

### Core Idea

- The strongest change affects both people and reverses their earlier relational roles.
- Base change on local adjustments to established characterization; retain close ties to the original personality and avoid arbitrary reconstruction.

### Rules

- After the “love separated by mountains and seas” stage, especially after the greatest obstacle, stop designing new conflicts and deliver romantic rewards.
- The real obstacle has already been solved; readers now want abundant sweetness.
- Show love through concrete couple behavior and attentive care, not industrial-sweetener declarations of “I love you.”

## Character Behavioral-Consistency Check

### Step 1: Exclude External Plot Force

Check whether behavior exists only to push the plot in a chosen direction. If a character “happens” to do what advances the plot without personal motivation, mark “characterization drift” and add a character-level reason.

### Step 2: Confirm Emotional Objective

Before each segment, define three elements:

1. Target emotion: what should readers feel?
2. Does the personality support that emotion: do established attributes lead naturally toward it?
3. Does behavior fit the assigned function: does the character act consistently with their role in the story?

### Step 3: Derive Behavior from Characterization

From established experience, personality, goals, and current state:

- List two or three possible reactions in the scene.
- Evaluate each against established behavioral patterns.
- Choose the reaction that best fits the character and creates the most dramatic tension.

### Step 4: Validate Emotional Consistency

Check whether the core emotion expressed by the character matches the scene's target emotion. If not, adjust behavior or the target so the output remains emotionally coherent.

**Two execution paths**:

- Path A: define target emotion → derive behavior from characterization → validate consistency
- Path B: derive behavior from characterization → infer the scene's target emotion → validate consistency

## Supporting-Character Conversion Buffer

The process by which a supporting character's attitude toward the protagonist changes is the process of winning them over, creating substantial anticipation and payoff.

### Buffer Types

Online vs. offline, discussion behind someone's back, geographic separation, status gap, intimacy gap, trust level, information gap, etc.

### Steps

1. Always keep a buffer in place.
2. Select five to seven event turning points in the volume outline.
3. At each point, record the supporting character's state and attitude change.
4. At every decisive conversion point, clearly write the attitude change.
5. The change itself creates emotional movement and anticipation.

### Rules

- A supporting character cannot stand like an NPC waiting for activation.
- Give supporting characters their own actions and let their viewpoints initiate events.
- Positive characters also need independent action and motivation even when aligned with the protagonist.

## Using Identity Perception Gaps to Create Conflict

A character's “reputation” varies dynamically among observers; it is not constant.

| Viewpoint | Values | Evaluation of the male lead |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Conventional society | Comparable family background and resilience | Match of overall conditions |
| Male lead | Earning for family + affection | Relatively well matched |
| Female lead | Affection + faithfulness + love | Caring makes them well matched |
| Wealthy rival | Appearance and family compatibility | “I am the better match” |
| Bystander | Comparison of overall conditions | “She should marry the wealthy heir” |

**Key points**:

- Different evaluations of the same person naturally create conflict.
- One major payoff in romance is the gap between evaluations on different dimensions.

## Friendly Rivals

The most compelling relationship type.

### Rules

- Both characters must be compelling; otherwise, forced equality makes one a clinging nuisance.
- Core: profound recognition + absolute conflict → mutual respect. Remove either and the relationship loses its appeal.
- True nemeses recognize one another. An outsider's label does not count.

## Rival Positioning and Family-Arc Uses

### Rival: The Catfish Effect

- Function: increase pressure on a lead and create urgency that advances romantic progress.
- Place primarily before a climax as setup.
- It contributes little after the climax and cannot sustain a fading plot.

### Four Directions for Family Arcs

| Direction | Function | Timing |
|------|------|---------|
| His family helps | Provides money, status, or power | Before climax |
| His family obstructs | Creates barriers the protagonist must overcome for recognition | Before or after the node |
| Her family helps | Provides emotional refuge | Before climax |
| Her family obstructs | Parental disapproval → work to earn recognition | Before or after the node |

Help works mainly before a climax; obstruction works on either side and offers more flexibility.

## Male-Audience Romance Strategy

### Why Readers Choose Romance

| Value | Source |
|------|------|
| Emotional | Fills the need to feel needed and cared about |
| Self-esteem | A highly desirable heroine creates status and envy |
| High-status heroine | Stronger instrument for public triumph and greater self-esteem payoff |

### Core Technique: Write Romance Like Progression Fiction

- The ambiguous push-pull is the most attractive part.
- Treat affection as a progress bar; each advance is a new “level.”
- Each event is an upgrade, raising affection through conflict, resolution, understanding, and resonance.

### Affection Progression

Stranger → good person (helps an elderly woman cross the street) → upright, courageous person (steps forward in danger) → good friend (understands family hardship) → in love without recognizing it (a special event reveals each person's hidden side)

### Key Principle: The Protagonist Does Not Pursue Aggressively

- Current male-audience readers generally dislike a groveling pursuer.
- Affection grows because the male lead's strengths attract her, not because he chases.
- He helps casually because it is easy for him; she remembers it.

### Asymmetric Romantic Progression

- Use two lines: public social relationship, from strangers to friends to partners, and actual emotional affection.
- The lines should not advance together. One moves faster, creating richer conflict.
- Emotion faster than social status → *Kaguya-sama* mode: both are deeply attracted but refuse to confess.
- Social status faster than emotion → live-in-son-in-law/*Way of Choices* mode: publicly married, emotionally at zero.

### Showing the Heroine Caring for the Protagonist

| Heroine type | Method |
|---------|---------|
| Insecure and socially anxious | Secretly leaves helpful things while fearing discovery |
| Tsundere | Makes a careful lunch but insists it was merely extra |
| Direct and bold | Gives gifts openly or confesses directly |

### Use Contrast

| Contrast | Operation |
|---------|------|
| Timeline | Compare her attitude upon meeting with her attitude after familiarity |
| Double standard | Others cannot touch her head, but the protagonist can, establishing special status |
| Information gap | Online vs. real-world dual identities build anticipation for the reveal |

## Affection System: General Framework

### Four Stages

Chance meeting → romantic comedy → love separated by mountains and seas → finale

### Affection × Relationship Stage

Affection (negative/zero/half/full) × relationship stage (familiar/testing/ambiguous/confirmed)

**Use the lower side** to determine the stage. High affection without sufficient relationship development makes behavior abrupt.

### Three Thresholds for Couple Behavior

| Behavior | Threshold | Explanation |
|---------|------|------|
| Requires tolerance | Half affection or more | Initiated intimacy or compromise needs compensation or it becomes groveling |
| Special treatment | Half affection or more | Possessiveness, trust, sacrifice, privilege, reassurance |
| Bonding behavior | Both at half affection or more | Tacit understanding, sharing, companionship |

Writing tolerance-requiring behavior without sufficient affection feels greasy or harassing.

### Five Romance-Arc Templates

| Template | Core |
|------|------|
| Affection change | May rise/fall/already fell → corresponding paths → aftertaste |
| Benefit | Enjoying benefits from a partner is the core romance-status payoff |
| Jealous rivalry | Several characters compete for the protagonist; the protagonist does not jealously compete |
| Blocked development | Obstacle → attempted solution → solution → aftertaste |
| Public sweetness | Everyday couple interaction |

## Goal Independence and Relationship-Arc Design

### Protagonist Goal Independence

- The protagonist's goal must belong to them, not merely help someone else achieve a goal, or the protagonist becomes a supporting tool.
- Convert another person's objective into the protagonist's own: suppressing a rebellion protects personal interests, earns recognition, or gains resources.
- Self-check a random chapter: what is the protagonist actively pursuing? If nothing, they have become someone else's pawn.

### Layered Romance Design

- Romance should not progress linearly. Use upgrade nodes, each tied to a plot climax.
- Misalignment between social status and genuine affection creates tension.
- Ideal pace: slow then fast. Build affection early, release it later, and match the culmination with a grand ritual.

### Functional Supporting Characters

| Type | Function | Timing |
|------|------|---------|
| Rival (catfish) | Creates pressure and drives action | Before a climax node |
| Helpful friend/family | Provides resources or emotional support | Before climax |
| Obstructive friend/family | Rejects → sets conditions → protagonist overcomes → grants recognition | Before or after a node |

---

## Manipulative or Negative Characters

- They can advance plot but require caution.
- Confirm that the character is irreplaceable.
- When the protagonist is strong, any character can become positive feedback.
- When the protagonist is weak, readers will hate negative characters.

## Quality Checklist

After designing relationships or romance, check each item:

- [ ] **Clear relationship type**: every important relationship is conflict/alliance/intimacy/authority
- [ ] **Relationship arc**: every important relationship experiences at least one test or change
- [ ] **Core characterization**: the protagonist has a clear central trait and reasons extending from it
- [ ] **Goal independence**: the protagonist pursues a personal goal rather than another person's objective
- [ ] **Affection fit**: couple behavior matches the current affection stage, calculated from the lower side
- [ ] **Audience fit**: male-audience events display excellence; female-audience events deepen connection
- [ ] **Supporting characters act**: they are not NPCs waiting to trigger and have their own actions and motives
- [ ] **Buffer exists**: conversion retains a buffer and marks attitude changes at turning points
- [ ] **Romantic battlefield controlled**: love interests have no irreconcilable conflict
- [ ] **Correct timing for sweetness**: after the greatest obstacle, stop adding new conflict and deliver concrete couple behavior
- [ ] **Life beyond romance**: characters have lives beyond love and are not thin emotional tools
