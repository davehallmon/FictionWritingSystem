## Escalating Confrontation Between Protagonist and Opponent

### Core Principle: Contest, Not Steamrolling

The protagonist should not steamroll the villain throughout every confrontation. Give the protagonist a slight advantage in each small contest, followed by a major victory at the end.

Dou Dizhu analogy: The protagonist plays a three, the villain a four, the protagonist an ace, the villain a two, and finally the protagonist ends it with both jokers.

### Levels of Showing Off

| Level | Characteristics | Use |
|------|------|----------|
| Small everyday display | Requires no plot; demonstrates an advantage in daily life | Sustain reader patience when the next major payoff is distant |
| Core payoff | Occurs on the main plot and demonstrates power around its goal | Advances the main plot while satisfying readers |
| Displaced payoff | Leaves the main plot to show off elsewhere | Avoid; it wastes reader patience |

### Managing Character Presence

- Manage the progression line separately from the protagonist’s reaction line.
- Faced with the same insult: raging retaliation = reader turnoff; a slight, unconcerned smile = presence.
- Protagonist-reaction rule: Advancement improves power but does not automatically alter the protagonist’s composed responses. When facing low-level provocation, the protagonist should refuse to be led by it.

### Payoff-Density Rules

- Do not lengthen the setup for one payoff; invent several payoffs instead.
- Use layered division to create a chain of astonishment: spectators at the scene -> spectators elsewhere -> experts -> a supporting character’s information gap closes -> renewed astonishment.

## Emotion-Driven and Event-Driven Narratives

### Event-Driven Narrative

- The story is linked by visible external events: humiliation -> progression -> counter-humiliation.
- Reader stimulation comes from the event’s outcome: “Can he win?”
- Advantage: Fast pace, clear payoffs, and easy to learn.
- Disadvantage: If the opening uses an S-tier event, later A- or B-tier events will tire readers.

### Emotion-Driven Narrative (Character-Driven)

- The story advances through inner motivation, emotional change, and relationships.
- Event suspense is temporary; character suspense persists.
- Readers identify more easily, and the emotional push and pull lasts longer.

### Essence

Both approaches rely on “emotional fluctuation.” Event-driven fiction creates it through external conflict; emotion-driven fiction creates it through internal conflict and interpersonal tension. Choose this way:

- Fanqie wish-fulfillment / humiliation fiction -> event-driven: Deliver one external result per chapter—the protagonist wins, advances, or defeats an opponent.
- Regretful-partner / tragic-romance / social fiction -> emotion-driven: Events may be sparse, but the character’s emotional knot must remain suspended. Does he regret it? Has she forgiven him?
- Hybrid -> Push the main plot forward through events, then insert an emotional pause every 3–5 chapters—solitude, memory, testing—to let the emotional knot breathe.

## Core Techniques for Emotional Escalation and Contrast

### Emotional Escalation

- A payoff is not delivered all at once; it needs layered setup and escalation.
- Escalate negative emotion gradually to saturation, then transform it into positive emotion.
- Repeated refusal, ridicule, and doubt continually increase the negative emotion.

### Contrast

- Contrast is one of the fundamental principles of wish-fulfillment fiction.
- Supporting character fails vs. protagonist succeeds—Guan Yu slays Hua Xiong before the wine cools.
- Supporting character boasts vs. protagonist overwhelms—comparing kasayas: Elder Jinchi gloats over his robe until Tang Sanzang’s kasaya completely outclasses it.
- “Without comparison, there is no pain.”

### Correct Use of Negative Emotion

- Negative emotion is only one way to make the hen lay eggs, not the only way.
- The key is understanding the direction of the genre’s emotional chain.
- *Ascension to Godhood* (*登神*) uses almost no emotional suppression formulas yet remains highly satisfying—the “emotional gap” comes from reader fatigue with conventional plots.

### Practical Material for Emotional Push-Pull

- Transitions, daily life, and quiet plot events can also create emotional push and pull.
- “Asking for a favor” sequences can use obstacles, demonstrations of power, and strategic retreat.
- Strategic retreat and moving right before going left are effective push-pull structures in transition chapters.

## Payoff Design System

### Essence of a Payoff

- Payoffs extend beyond showing off and humiliating opponents; they include many forms of emotional satisfaction.
- Purely positive emotional output can create a payoff but cannot maximize it.
- Transforming negative emotion into positive emotion = maximum reader satisfaction.

### Six Payoff Types

| Type | Core | Functional Extension |
|------|------|----------|
| Overwhelming ability | Power far exceeds the opponent, creating pure superiority | -- |
| Goal achievement | Complete a stage-level goal | Introduce the action line and next plan |
| Inventorying gains | Count acquired resources, connections, or abilities | Use gains to introduce the next plot |
| Change in attitude | Supporting character moves from contempt to admiration | Use the supporting character to introduce the next plot |
| Hidden identity / identity reveal | Identity emerges gradually | Continually create astonishment |
| Emotional fulfillment | Satisfaction from winning back a husband/wife or repairing a relationship | -- |

### Reverse-Engineering a Payoff

1. First decide how readers will be satisfied—the payoff type.
2. Then design how to create anticipation—the anticipation point.
3. Finally, design the setup.
4. Use this order when creating chapter outlines as well.

## Emotional-Module and Dramatic-Unit Categories

### Levels of Abstraction

Extract reusable tools from an archetypal story one level at a time. Core principle: **Drama wears out; emotion does not.**

| Level | Name | Content | Explanation |
|------|------|------|------|
| Level 0 | Specific story | Complete source plot | Direct reuse = plagiarism |
| Level 1 | Classic plot | Remove description, retain material such as names, places, and events | Same medicine in a new bottle; still risks similarity |
| Level 2 | Story configuration | Remove the material, leaving only logical direction | Combination of dramatic unit + emotional module |
| Level 3 | Dramatic unit | Abstraction of dramatic direction | Logical structure that can be combined freely |
| Level 4 | Emotional module | Abstraction of what readers want | Direction of anticipation + direction of satisfaction |

**Story configuration + material = specific story**. Two uses: ① **reconstruct**—retain the material but completely replace the configuration, essential for long-form fiction; ② **fine-tune**—use a classic plot but refine it around the characterization, suitable for short fiction. When characterization conflicts with the classic plot, reconstruction is mandatory.

Running example, abstracting a regretful-partner story one level at a time:

- Level 0 = The complete source text of *A Certain Regretful-Husband Story* (*某追妻文*), copied with dialogue and scenes intact.
- Level 1 = Remove description and retain only the material: “Ex-husband asks to reconcile and is refused.”
- Level 2 = Remove even the material, leaving only direction: “The abuser regrets the harm -> the victim no longer cares.”
- Level 3 dramatic unit = “Regret that comes too late.”
- Level 4 emotional module = Two things readers want: “the scumbag regrets it + the protagonist never looks back.”

Re-skinning example: Keep the Level 2 direction “the abuser regrets it -> the victim no longer cares,” and replace all the material to create a new story—a workplace version (the supervisor who sabotaged you fails and seeks you out after you changed jobs and became a boss) or an ancient-romance version (the husband who divorced you falls from power and asks to reconcile after you have received a noble title).

### Relationship Between Emotional Modules and Dramatic Units

- Large and medium dramatic units abstract drama and establish the tone of an entire book or major plot section.
- An emotional module abstracts the emotional core and corresponds to a specific emotional payoff.
- Narrative elements and structural techniques together are “supporting tools.” They cannot independently provide a complete framework and must be applied within one.

### Relationship Between Drama and Emotion

- Repeating the same formula, such as a heroic rescue, does not inherently become stale; an unchanged structure does. Change the setting or opponent or add a new emotion each time, and it is not repetition.

### Emotional-Module Cards

#### Romantic Rivalry Card

- Several people love the same person and compete in front of them.
- Escalation: early (shopping with Man 1 when Man 2 sees them) -> middle (kissing Man 2 when the male lead sees them) -> late (Man 1 and Man 2 fight).

#### Countdown Card

- Establish a timer and make something happen when it expires.
- Often paired with a battle against overwhelming strength: The protagonist cannot win but must fight.

#### Grievance-and-Affection Card (Large / Medium Dramatic Unit)

- A specific event creates a relationship between two people bound by powerful emotion or moral obligation.
- The focus is not the process of creating the grievance, but the behavior and emotion afterward.
- As a large dramatic unit, it becomes the “tone” surrounding the entire book.

#### Dual-Identity Card

- The instant two identities overlap surprises readers.
- Essentials: Daily establishment of Identity A + hidden information + impact when Identity B is revealed.

#### Special-Treatment Card

- A powerful tool for saving a character from acting out of character.
- Use “special treatment” to justify behavior that deviates in a specific situation.

#### Affection-Change Card

- The core is demonstrating a qualitative transformation in the emotional chain.
- A heroic rescue is only the vehicle; the relationship change matters more than the rescue method.
- Avoid repetition: Increase complexity and gains in every volume so emotion rises in a spiral.

#### A Deeper Understanding of Showing Off

- The core is contrast between impressive and unimpressive.
- Compare “fractions of full power”: After exchanging moves, boast about what fraction was used.
- Early setup: The emotion of ridicule and suppression, plus the protagonist concealing strength.

#### Small Stake, Great Gain

- The ratio between cost and gain must be enormous.
- The process needs randomness and agency; readers cannot believe success is 100% guaranteed.
- Failure is allowed, but repair the emotional gap through accidental success, support from a benefactor, or similar means.

#### Misunderstanding Card

- Misunderstanding sequence: Define the central misunderstanding -> naturally derive smaller misunderstandings -> define the resolution conditions -> design obstacles.
- Resolution rule: Any misunderstanding harming the career or romance arc must be resolved; one with no negative effect or a positive effect may remain unresolved.

### Narrative-Element Cards

| Card | Core Use |
|------|----------|
| Utility-character card | Fulfills a narrative need in a specific scene: reinforcement / advancement / information |
| Mysterious-girl card | A clue in a dream, memory, or hallucination that matters to the plot; do not place it casually at the opening |
| Incapacitation card | A character cannot consciously control their body or an item; may be bound to characterization as a flaw |
| Relationship card | A specific relationship configuration that determines the basic interaction pattern |
| Secret card | Keep a secret / investigate one / learn one accidentally |
| Falling-out card | Disagreement / misunderstanding / harm -> argument / confrontation / separation -> regret / negotiation / crisis-driven reconciliation |
| Dual-world connection card | Transmigration / connection / fusion |
| Close-pursuit card | Chase / tracking / tailing |
| Covert-action card | A character’s covert action / their investigation of another’s / another’s covert action against them |
| Future-glimpse card | Prophecy / transmigration memory / system alert; responses: avoid / pursue / exploit / exchange for benefit |
| Taboo card | A taboo involving a person, group, item, place, or activity; explicitly state the consequence of violation |
| New-environment role card | Undercover work / captivity / amnesia / transmigration; confirm the other side’s background, personality, skills, and weaknesses |

### Structural-Technique Cards

| Card | Core Use |
|------|----------|
| Differing-evaluations card | Two approaches: suppression and reversal; demonstrating indirectly through another person is a variation |
| Dramatic-triangle card | Persecutor, victim, and rescuer can exchange roles |
| Stirring the World | Emphasize the meaning and effects of the protagonist’s behavior for different characters |
| Puzzle design | Set propositions P1/P2 -> derive P3 -> derive P4 by negation -> hide P3 to misdirect |

### Combining and Extracting

- Level-one extraction: Extract a basic narrative pattern from a successful work.
- Level-two extraction: Add genre-specific elements to the level-one framework.
- Different card types can stack: core-hook card + relationship card + constraint card = complete plot unit.
- Reconstruction: Retain the names of material while replacing the story direction.
- Fine-tuning: Retain the story direction while adding new emotional modules.
- Upward spiral: Every return of one formula increases its complexity and rewards.

### Common Mistakes

1. **Story similarity**: Only performing Level 1 extraction—changing names without changing structure—looks no different from plagiarism to readers.
2. **Broken characterization**: Forcing a character to act against their characterization to fit a classic plot.
3. **Uncontrolled innovation**: “Innovation” detached from emotional modules easily becomes a reader turnoff.
4. **Killing the core idea**: Do not turn “what readers enjoy” and “what you enjoy writing” into a binary choice.

## Creating Push-Pull Through Misunderstanding

### Essence of Misunderstanding

“I originally believed A, then discovered I was completely wrong”—an information gap creates an emotional gap. Information-gap design is the core, and every step must contain one explicitly.

| Situation | Use |
|------|------|
| Showing off and humiliating | Protagonist acts modestly -> supporting character assumes weakness -> reversal and humiliation feel stronger |
| Romance arc | Misunderstanding creates a barrier -> blocks the relationship -> resolution feels sweeter |
| Characterization contrast | “I thought the protagonist was weak, but he is incredibly strong” |
| Worldbuilding premise | “I thought cultivation was possible in this world, but it actually is not” |

### Execution Essentials

- In a romance arc, do not truly tear the couple apart; create “lingering resentment” or “time to cool off,” not a breakup.
- Use a heroic rescue to bring the relationship closer and a misunderstanding to pull it apart.
- In showing-off sequences, the protagonist’s modesty or restraint naturally generates misunderstandings.

## Building Plot Around a Hook

### Four Parts

1. **Occurrence**: Establish the initial situation and the hook’s prerequisites.
2. **Development**: Use repeated setbacks or accumulation to approach the trigger point.
3. **Turn**: Use the special advantage or a key method to reverse the hook.
4. **Climax**: Release the hook completely, maximizing before-and-after contrast.

Work backward from the hook as the climax to avoid an episodic list of events. As long as fresh hooks exist, the story can remain interesting.

## Pacing and Climax-Control Techniques

| Element | Rule |
|------|------|
| Major-climax cycle | Complete in 7–10 days, with two daily updates totaling 14 chapters; beyond 10 days, readers easily become frustrated |
| Minor-climax cycle | Keep it around three days |
| Post-climax transition | Add 1–2 slice-of-life chapters so readers can breathe |
| Response to declining retention | Enter the next story section quickly; do not hesitate in a low period |
| Five-chapter public-access loop | One characterization chapter + two setup chapters + one humiliation chapter + one reaction chapter |
| VIP pacing | 1–2 characterization chapters + five setup chapters + 1–2 humiliation chapters + one summary chapter |
| Multiple tracks reduce fatigue | Insert half a chapter from a supporting character’s POV to change perspective and pace |
| Climax transition | Introduce a subplot character before the climax as foreshadowing, then bring that thread forward afterward for a natural transition |

## Four-Step Formula for Driving Reader Desire

**Core formula**: create a need -> offer hope -> strive toward a solution -> fulfill the desire

### Step 1: Create a Need

- Use “low status,” “hardship,” “an imminent threat,” or “something never possessed” to create identification.
- When readers see false accusation, injustice, or bullying by the powerful, they naturally think, “This should not be happening.”

### Essence of the Special Advantage

A special advantage is a trait or ability difficult to possess in real life. Without one, the suffering of the dilemma makes readers feel powerless.

### Essence of Pace

- Continually resolve existing conflicts + continually create new ones.
- Method 1: “Gradual improvement” through divided goals.
- Method 2: “New dilemmas” through escalating conflict levels.
- Good pacing: Dilemmas escalate level by level, while their types and solutions remain varied.

## Quality-Control Checklist

After completing a chapter or plot section, check each item:

- [ ] **Decision guide**: Did you select the correct method from the routing table?
- [ ] **Escalating confrontation**: Does every exchange between protagonist and opponent rise in intensity? Does the protagonist steamroll from the beginning?
- [ ] **Showing-off level**: Are small everyday displays and core payoffs clearly distinguished? Is any display displaced from the main plot?
- [ ] **Payoff type**: Is one of the six payoff types used explicitly? Can you identify it?
- [ ] **Reverse-engineered payoff**: Did you define the payoff type first, then design anticipation, and finally add setup?
- [ ] **Emotional escalation**: Does negative emotion increase gradually to saturation before release, or arrive all at once?
- [ ] **Contrast**: Does a supporting character amplify the protagonist’s satisfaction through contrast?
- [ ] **Misunderstanding push-pull**: Does a romantic misunderstanding remain “lingering resentment” rather than causing a true breakup?
- [ ] **Four-part hook**: Are occurrence -> development -> turn -> climax all complete, without jumping directly to the climax?
- [ ] **Pacing cycle**: Does the major climax finish within 7–10 days and the minor climax within about three?
- [ ] **Reader desire**: Does the sequence complete “create a need -> offer hope -> strive toward a solution -> fulfill the desire”?
- [ ] **Card combination**: Are emotional modules, narrative elements, and structural techniques stacked rather than relied upon individually?
- [ ] **Upward spiral**: When a formula returns, are its complexity and gains greater than before?
