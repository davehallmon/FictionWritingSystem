## Key Points from the Thousand-Average-Readers Writing Guide

- The cheat ability sets the book's tone. A collapse in follow-through reading most likely means the story has drifted from its core hook.
- Progression must be neither too fast, which destroys anticipation, nor too slow, which exhausts patience.
- After an upgrade, demonstrate the power of the new ability to release satisfaction, then introduce a larger crisis to rebuild anticipation.
- The protagonist's actions must advance the plot; they cannot wander without purpose.
- Basic three-act structure: setup → confrontation → resolution.
- Each act transition needs a “gap”: a mismatch between the protagonist's plan and the actual result → surprise → suspense → continued reading.

## Retention Techniques for Traditional and High-Concept Fiction

### Four Pillars of Retention

- **Progression**: power/status/money/resources
- **Resource constraints**: cultivation requires resources → creates anticipation
- **Goals**: major goals + minor goals + false goals, where a reasonable failure creates a fresh surprise
- **Mystery-solving**: reveal the tip of the iceberg → uncover layer after layer → clear the fog

### New-Map Framework (Purpose + Climax)

Every new location needs two things:

1. A purpose for going there → what must be obtained or what truth must be uncovered
2. A major climax at the end → leave readers wanting more

### Move Quickly Through Flat Plot

- Establish an intense goal → transition toward a major climax
- Introduce a sharp conflict immediately → energize the plot first
- Study openings from fast-paced works in the same genre and raise the current section's goal-and-conflict density to at least one turning point every 500 Chinese characters

## Three Elements of Writing: Tropes, Pace, and Emotion

**Tropes**: Prioritize structural functions already validated by current samples or comparable books. Whether a trope still works must be verified against target-platform samples.

**Pace**: Common problems include useless dialogue, self-indulgent details, and long setup. Cut them. If one sentence is enough, do not use two.

**Emotion**: The heart of wish-fulfillment fiction is satisfaction; its emotional construction depends on strongly connected rises and falls.

## Core Hooks and Structural Thinking

- A core hook is a fixed plot-development pattern supported by the main selling point and repeated with variations.
- Most ranking titles have a core hook of their own.
- Replace abstract “level-X structure” with progressive detail: one-sentence main plot → volume outline → chapter outline → prose.
- Prefer repeated tropes that consolidate the main plot, with occasional subplots for relief.

## Emotional Handling and Cognitive Techniques

### Primacy and the Peak-End Rule

- The first information presented has greater influence when shaping perception.
- Front-load a negative element to strengthen its negative effect.
- Title, synopsis, and brand reputation form the reader's crucial first impression.

### Strengthening Emotion

- Examples are usually the best way to build understanding.
- Before applying a technique, ask why it works; do not use it merely to check a box.

## Categories of Writing Techniques and Common Misuse

### Two Categories

1. Attract readers: title/synopsis/opening/payoff/gimmick/explosive hook
2. Keep readers: worldbuilding/characters/progression/pacing/anticipation/identification

### Common Misuse

- Applying a technique designed for sustained anticipation to the opening, even though the objectives differ
- Ignoring platform fit; techniques must be adapted across sites

### Three Elements of Writing

Concept selection (target readers and genre boundaries) + plot design (master/volume/chapter outlines) + prose (delivery). All three must be reflected in the artifact.

## Anti-Tropes and Understanding Repetition

### A Point of Interest Is Not Necessarily a Gimmick

- Some selling-point hooks attract readers only once; use them as an introductory gimmick.
- Few selling points can sustain millions of Chinese characters alone; plot, world, and characters must carry the story.

### Data-Based Judgment of Repetition

- Repetition includes character beats, trope beats, and plot beats, in increasing order of value.
- If losing the core attraction destroys the data, switching attractions fails too, and performance remains poor, readers wanted that repeated element.
- Maintain a repetition strategy while the core attraction performs steadily in current samples or feedback. When feedback declines, determine whether the form of repetition needs to evolve.

## Creative Pacing Method

### Rules for Slice-of-Life Fiction

Everyday life does not mean flatness. A romantic “battlefield” can be daily life filled with conflict. The key is to create contradictions and use tropes playfully.

### Pace Self-Check

Before writing each chapter, read the state summary in `追踪/上下文.md` and use its `## 近三章速记` section to assess the current pace:

- Dragging signal: two consecutive chapters without goal progress, escalating obstacles, or new information → increase conflict density in the next chapter.
- Rushing signal: two consecutive chapters contain only explosive beats with no aftermath → add one or two connective scenes, but each must advance a relationship or planted clue.

### Climax Timing Standards

- Major climax: complete in 7–10 days; follow-through reading declines beyond 10 days
- Minor climax: complete in about 3 days
- After a climax → add 1–2 chapters of everyday transition

### Two Major Causes of Mid-to-Late Subscription Decline

1. Repeated payoffs → reader fatigue
2. No long-range anticipation

Solution: decide the ending during outlining, then design mid-to-late explosive beats around it.

### Foreshadowing vs. Deliberate Obscurity

- Deliberate obscurity: intentionally withhold the reason while hinting at hidden significance → creates curiosity
- Foreshadowing: blend a clue naturally into the plot → later revelation creates surprise

## Diagnosing and Correcting Core Selling-Point Drift

### Selling-Point Drift

- Selling-point drift means the central attractive element has been diluted or replaced.
- Common example: wish-fulfillment fiction gradually becomes farming fiction → core readers leave.
- Self-check every ten chapters: “Is the selling point that first attracted readers still present?”
- Root cause: the writing process replaced the central selling point, but reader expectations did not change with it.

### Reader Needs and Web-Fiction Types

| Level of need | Web-fiction type |
|----------|----------|
| Physiological | Erotic/harem fiction |
| Safety | Overpowered-protagonist/system fiction |
| Social | Harem/team fiction |
| Esteem | Wish-fulfillment/face-slapping fiction |
| Self-actualization | Progression/exploration fiction |

Meeting needs across levels creates breakout potential. Before writing, identify the need being served and build every design choice around it.

### Cheat Ability + Conflict = Plot

- Cheat ability too strong + insufficient conflict = boredom
- Cheat ability too weak + overwhelming conflict = reader anxiety
- Ideal: the cheat ability just resolves the current conflict → exposes a larger conflict → escalates layer by layer

### Thematic Unity

- A book needs one coherent core emotion from beginning to end.
- Minor emotions serve the major emotion.
- Test: open a random chapter. Does its emotion point toward the book's core?

## Contradiction Techniques That Improve Readability

### Three Levels of Conflict

- Ordinary story → conflict remains between characters
- Good story → conflict sits within a larger context
- Clever story → conflict exists between the work and the reader through a mismatch of assumptions

### Mismatched Assumptions

- Make the protagonist's common sense oppose the world's common sense → readers keep thinking, “You can do that?”
- Example: a modern protagonist transported to ancient times publicly audits accounts and exposes hypocrisy. Readers share the protagonist's modern values while supporting characters are shocked; the protagonist's “abnormal response” contrasts with their “normal response.”
- Two modes:
  - Shared cognitive setting + differently minded protagonist: the protagonist stands out
  - Familiar protagonist + unfamiliar cognitive setting: in a transmigration/apocalypse/medieval world, readers use the protagonist's viewpoint to oppose the world

## Strategy for Reaching Premium Quality

### The Golden Three Chapters

The first three chapters must establish tone and retention. If the project has a strong author brand/IP foundation or the user requests otherwise, formulaic structure can be reduced. Otherwise, check for three elements: central conflict, cheat ability/advantage, and long-term anticipation. If imposing a template would damage the established tone, preserve the work's tone.

### The Core of Retention: Uninterrupted Anticipation

- Anticipation peaks when the protagonist is about to obtain something but has not yet obtained it.
- Before fulfilling one hook, attach another → repeat.
- Keep at least two anticipation threads active at all times: a major expectation plus interwoven minor threads.

## Six Core Modules of Commercial Web Fiction

### 1. Tone: The Golden Three Chapters Must Answer Expectations

- Formula: unsettling circumstances + status that urgently needs improvement + a hopeful advantage + a desirable future readers can imagine
- Three levels of tone: emotional atmosphere → story direction → value orientation

### 2. Cheat Ability: Simplicity Is Essential

- Core categories: information advantage (best and least prone to losing control), accelerated progression, and object-access ability
- Most important property: simplicity. A gamified panel understood at a glance is ideal.

### 3. Characters: Archetypes Are Not Inherently Bad

- Tag every character; more important characters receive more tags, and behavior follows those tags.
- Design characters strategically: giving resources, being face-slapped, becoming a follower, or acting as a guide. Give each a clear role.

### 4. Pace: Nested Loops

- Essential loop: establish a goal → act → complete the goal → improve → establish a new goal
- Counter reader fatigue by shifting from numerical confrontation to mechanism-based confrontation.

### 5. Payoffs: Create Discomfort, Then Remove It

> Related: For the nature, six types, and reverse-design method of payoffs, use the “Payoff Design System” in [plot-emotion-system.md](plot-emotion-system.md) as the authority. This file covers payoffs only from the perspective of commercial retention.

- Formula: create a need → offer hope → work toward a solution → fulfill the desire
- The essence of overpowered-protagonist fiction is public demonstration of supremacy.

### 6. Conflict and Suspense

- Conflict can be simple, direct, and formulaic; suspense requires careful design.
- Gradient of suspense disclosure: end each chapter at the threshold of “about to succeed.”

### Three Forms of Micro-Innovation

- Popular premise + established genre formula
- Popular category system + popular gimmick
- A different viewpoint within a mature world model

## Emotional-Gap Analysis and Reader-Persona Method

- Breakout books sustain one emotional current from a single source; weaker books scatter emotion across unrelated directions.
- Minor emotions such as progression, revenge, and treasure hunting should serve the main emotion.
- **Reader-persona method**: extract plot elements from breakout web fiction → define the reader persona → infer psychological needs → compare and analyze.
- Dig deeper than stated reader needs; identify attachments readers may not recognize themselves.
- Extract frequent emotional keywords from reader comments—love, hate, longing, avoidance—and compare them one by one against the current emotional selling point to find unmet needs.

### Examples

**Apocalyptic rebirth fiction**:

- Apocalypse = social order overturned → desire to see elites fall and ordinary people rise
- Stockpiling = severe insecurity → possession of vast supplies → overwhelming safety
- Rebirth = exploiting advance knowledge → tiny investment yields enormous returns

**New-media God-of-War live-in-son-in-law fiction**:

- Core pain: helplessness → unable to support parents or care for children
- Second layer: family rejection → every sacrifice goes unrecognized
- Third layer: shame → unable to confront one's own helplessness
- The God of War returns → everything reverses → massive satisfaction

## Rules for Emotional Engagement and Commercial Execution

### What to Imitate

- Imitate: story formulas/dramatic techniques/narrative structures/character types
- Do not imitate: wording/iconic scenes/language style

### The Essence of Commercialization

- Web fiction is a product; commercialization means respecting the target reader's expectations.
- Web fiction continuously orchestrates reader emotion; every chapter needs a clear emotional delivery.

### The Ultimate Principle of Emotional Engagement

- Successful work depends on several interacting factors, not one isolated technique.
- Most anticipation comes from long-term emotional investment in characters, not surface-level prose.

## Multidimensional Use of Tropes

- A single payoff trope can be written at least five or six ways: straight, subverted, subverted again, repeatedly subverted, or subverted then restored.
- Five versions of “humiliating the show-off”:
  - Straight: the opponent mocks the protagonist, who immediately reveals their identity and humiliates them.
  - Subversion: the protagonist pretends to be weak, lets the opponent gloat, then reverses the situation.
  - Second subversion: the opponent assumes the protagonist is pretending to be weak, but the protagonist is genuinely strong and never pretended.
  - Sustained subversion: the opponent repeatedly misjudges the protagonist, believing each time that they have seen through them, only to be countered again.
  - Subvert then restore: after every elaborate variation, the protagonist still crushes the opponent through raw strength.
- Advanced method: preserve the trope's underlying logic while changing its shell, setting, and characters. The surface differs; the engine remains the same.

## Choosing Chapter-Outline Granularity

| Project complexity | Chapter-outline granularity | Use when |
|------|------|------|
| High | Secondary structure / step-by-step setup-development-turn-resolution | Multiple threads, strong reversals, complex worldbuilding |
| Standard long-form | 300–400 Chinese characters per chapter outline | First volume, key volume, climax volume |
| Low / rolling continuation | A short purpose sentence + key emotion/hook | Stable volume outline and tracking files already exist |

Rule: the less context available, the stronger the reversals, and the more character threads involved, the finer the chapter outline should be. Simplify when stable structure and tracking files already support the work.

## Selling-Point Theory and Practical Method

### Four-Step Selling-Point Method

1. **Think**: clarify the whole-book selling point, title selling point, synopsis selling point, and selling point of each plot segment
2. **Design**: expand the design around the selling point
3. **Refine**: which readers does it target, and what style suits them?
4. **Deliver**: give each chapter one purpose word and write closely to the outline and purpose

### Expressing the Selling Point

- Let readers discover it while reading; discovery is ten times more satisfying than being told.
- Show it indirectly through plot, dialogue, and reactions.
- Three-step escalation: opening hint → middle development → climactic release.

### Title and Synopsis

- Title = first door, synopsis = second door, opening = third door. Each filters readers and carries forward the same core promise.
- Do not use a literary title that cannot communicate the core selling point.

### Chapter-Purpose Method

- Summarize each chapter in one sentence + label a “purpose” word: setup/climax/payoff/face-slapping/characterization/worldbuilding.
- Write closely to the outline and purpose to prevent drift.

## Core Principles of Cheat Abilities and the Evolution of System Fiction

Story process: inciting incident → desire → goal → action → obstacle → overcoming difficulty → reward

**A cheat ability can replace any stage of that process**:

- Replace “establish a goal”: the system issues a mission
- Replace “overcome difficulty”: press a button to solve the problem, but pay a cost
- Replace “preparation”: skip preparation and fast-forward to the obstacle
- Replace “inciting incident”: travel a thousand years into the future
- Replace “reward”: the system lets the protagonist profit from public service

**Why package a cheat ability as a system?** System constraints preserve the complete story structure. A limited system forces the protagonist to act step by step.

## The Essence of Need-Driven Story and Pace

### Essential Logic of Entertainment

Create a need for readers, then satisfy it.

- Formula: create a need → offer hope → work toward a solution → fulfill the desire
- “This should not be happening” is the fundamental impulse: readers see injustice or regret and naturally want change.
- A strong concept evokes regret or the desire to change something.

### A Genre's Core Is Not Its Surface Premise

- Stories built only on an interesting premise often perform poorly; stories centered on the core conflict perform well.

### The Essence of Pace

- Continually resolve existing conflicts → continually create new ones.
- Good pace: escalating levels of hardship + progressively greater difficulty + varied types of hardship.

## Creative Philosophy: Map Changes and Pace

### Reader Transition When Changing Maps

Subscriptions plunge when an urban story suddenly moves to an immortal realm. Solution: build a buffer zone that gradually guides readers into the new genre space.

### Outline Philosophy

- Outline only the main plot; develop branches gradually during writing.
- An outline must remain editable. Excessive rigidity destroys spontaneity.

### Pace and Emotion

- Pace means giving readers an enjoyable experience; face-slapping matters for quality, not sheer quantity.
- Wish-fulfillment fiction is not synonymous with face-slapping. It should feel easy to read and sustain anticipation.

## Three Forms of Anticipation and Analysis of Collapse Causes

### Three Forms of Anticipation

1. **Plot anticipation**: major goal + unanswered questions + compelling romantic interests → keeps readers waiting
2. **Theme**: the daily treat each chapter provides → while the theme stays on target, readers want most chapters
3. **Freshness**: novel world conflicts + varied plot forms

| Type | Function | Rhythm |
|------|------|------|
| Plot anticipation | Keeps readers waiting | Fulfilled occasionally |
| Theme | Daily treat | Fulfilled continuously |
| Freshness | Makes readers willing to return | Intermittent stimulation |

Combine all three to prevent collapse.

### Root Cause of Collapse: Selling-Point Drift

- Loop-based stories: too few angles on the repeated selling point → reader fatigue.
- If technique is sound but performance still collapses, first check whether the central selling point remains and whether anticipation threads still have room for fulfillment.

### Extend Strengths vs. Repair Weaknesses

- Prioritize the genre's strengths: core selling point, target emotion, and most frequent payoff.
- Do not introduce subplots that dilute the core selling point merely to repair a weakness.
- Before launch, check: (1) the emotion behind the core selling point is clear; (2) the same point can expand in at least three directions; (3) genre strengths match existing materials and comparable assets.

## General Creative-Thinking Method

### Three-Step Creative Method

Choose the protagonist's identity → match the cheat-ability type → establish the opening environment.

### Two Types of Opening Pressure

- Conflict pressure: clearly directed and fast, but more prone to later collapse.
- Environmental pressure: small pressures of daily life, easier to sustain.
- Recommendation: favor environmental pressure at the opening.

### Cheat-Ability Feedback

- A cheat ability must produce an immediate change.
- Weave the process of change into the story.
- Make the ability fit the protagonist's current profession or serve as the key out of the current predicament.

### Different Genres Need Different Methods

- Eastern fantasy: fast pace; only the protagonist appears in Chapter 1; enter the story quickly.
- Low-status Western fantasy: slower pace; multiple characters and dialogue create atmosphere and gradually reveal the setting.
- Do not apply formulas mechanically; adapt to genre and cheat ability.

## Trope Collection and Class-Mobility Payoffs

### Collecting Tropes

- Extract useful trope elements from frequent words in recent target-platform comments and bullet comments; create a trope-to-scene mapping table.
- Tropes are sources of inspiration, not items to pile up.
- Readers still anticipate classic tropes; use them skillfully to fulfill that expectation.

### Class-Mobility Payoff

- The first substantial earnings that move the protagonist into a higher class create the strongest satisfaction.
- Going from poverty to modest comfort is especially relatable to mass-market readers.

### Avoiding Travelogue Transitions

- Every story arc must leave one unresolved conflict thread that connects to the next arc.

### Hooks That Build Anticipation

- Reveal a character's special skill/background early → readers anticipate its later use.
- Establish staged hooks → reveal them fully at the climax.

## Creative Workflow from Concept Selection to Retention

### Genre Positioning and Reader Filtering

- Can the genre keyword act as a signal that selects target readers and lets them rapidly decompress the information?
- Micro-innovation = place a fresh gimmick over a classic genre.
- Genre-combination formula: new element + old element + popular vehicle = new genre.
- Compensation = give the protagonist what reality denies them. A live-in son-in-law is secretly a God of War, compensating for “being looked down on.”
- Shortcut = the cheat ability lets the protagonist skip the grind, such as a check-in system or overnight wealth, delivering the opening relief of “winning without years of effort.”

### Traffic-Attracting Elements

- Title construction: conflict/revenge + an exclusive advantage + a provocative line.
- Highest standard for a synopsis: interesting + provocative + suspenseful.
- A synopsis cannot depend on author brand; it must deliver the selling point directly.

### Retention = Emotion + Hunger

- Emotion: rapid identification → rapid immersion → resonance with the character
- Hunger: use an information gap to plant questions in the reader's viewpoint
- Onion-peeling technique: plant the central mystery in the synopsis → plant a smaller question at the chapter opening → cut off key information at the chapter ending

### Opening Design

- The first 20,000 Chinese characters need to solve only one problem: survival.
- Lighthearted opening: the protagonist has nothing + the cheat ability is immediately understandable.
- Within 300–500 Chinese characters, establish circumstances + source of danger + hope of escape.
- Nothing about the protagonist can be perfect at the opening; begin from the lowest point before recovery.

### Master and Detailed Outlines

- Notes = expression bank; master outline = core skeleton + characters + background.
- Detailed outline = the stories that happen among people on the stage.
- Chapter outline = adjust the detailed outline for clarity, readability, and pace.
- Core hook = a fixed plot-development pattern → repeat it with constant variations.

## Additional Selling-Point Principle: Artistic Transformation of Core Emotion

Do not judge a selling point crudely by “how many people sell it.” Core emotions are universal experiences that people recognize in themselves.

The essence of fiction: take one ordinary emotion shared by everyone and transform it through art, abstraction, and extremity.

| Treatment | Operation | Example |
|----------|------|------|
| Extremize | Push an everyday grievance into life-or-death stakes or an enormous wager | A live-in son-in-law is despised → the returning God of War publicly reveals a fortune worth tens of billions |
| Dramatize | Do not write “he felt wronged”; enact it | He silently pushes over his pay card and walks away |
| Abstract | Choose one emotion to carry the whole book and cut tangential emotional threads | The entire book holds onto “never being recognized”; family and romance both serve it |

## Quality Checklist

After designing the outline, concept, or selling point, check each item:

- [ ] **Clear core selling point**: one sentence explains the emotion the book sells
- [ ] **No selling-point drift**: every ten chapters, ask, “Is the selling point that first attracted readers still present?”
- [ ] **Simple cheat ability**: readers understand it immediately; functions and progression rules are clear
- [ ] **Cheat ability matches conflict**: it just resolves the current conflict and exposes a larger one
- [ ] **Three anticipation threads coexist**: plot anticipation + thematic treat + freshness
- [ ] **Pace has benchmarks**: major climax in 7–10 days, minor climax in 3 days, then 1–2 transitional chapters
- [ ] **Reasonable progression pace**: neither too fast (loses anticipation) nor too slow (loses patience)
- [ ] **Unified theme**: a random chapter's emotion points toward the book's core
- [ ] **Title, synopsis, and opening form three doors**: filter layer by layer; avoid literary titles that cannot communicate the selling point
- [ ] **Chapter outline has a purpose word**: setup/climax/payoff/face-slapping/characterization/worldbuilding
- [ ] **Dual retention engine**: emotion (rapid identification) + hunger (questions planted through information gaps)
- [ ] **Extend strengths instead of patching weaknesses**: genre strengths match the creator's strengths
