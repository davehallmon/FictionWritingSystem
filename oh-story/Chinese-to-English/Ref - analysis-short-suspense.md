# Evaluation Standard for Suspense in Short-Form Source Text

> Observe how short-form fiction distributes questions, information, evidence, and answers. Describe only immediate, inter-section, and whole-story suspense found in the source; do not apply volume-level, complete-book, or serialization-hook models.

## Object of Suspense

Suspense is not merely “there is a secret” or “the atmosphere is mysterious.” The text must cause the reader to believe simultaneously in at least two possible outcomes while delaying one crucial answer.

Before analysis, state clearly:

```markdown
主问题：{全文最终必须回答的问题}
副问题：{服务主问题的证据、关系或动机问题；没有则写无}
读者先知道：{位置与信息}
角色先知道：{位置与信息}
最终答案：{位置与改变的判断}
```

## Three Scales of Observation

| Scale | Object of observation | Resolution test |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate | An unfinished objective within a conversation, action, or evidence-gathering scene | Does the scene produce a result, obstacle, or choice? |
| Between sections | An evidentiary anomaly, relationship question, or unfinished action left at a section ending | Does later text respond and change the next action? |
| Whole story | The central question and its answer about responsibility, identity, or emotion | Does the ending settle both fact and consequence? |

All three scales need not exist simultaneously. A short story may advance through one strong central question. When too many secondary questions cannot return to the central question, report expansion into side plots.

## Types of Information Gaps

| Type | Identification | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Reader ahead | The reader has seen danger or evidence that a character has not discovered | Does the lead create concern or anticipation without revealing the answer? |
| Character ahead | A character conceals a plan, evidence, or motive | Is the concealment fair, or does the narration deliberately lie? |
| Between characters | Different characters possess different evidence or interpretations | Does exchanging information change relationships and actions? |
| Shared misjudgment | The reader and protagonist reach the wrong interpretation from true evidence | Does the later answer preserve the old facts while correcting their interpretation? |

## Evidence-Release Chain

For every crucial piece of evidence, record:

| Location | New information | Interpretation at the time | Who obtains/verifies it | Action it causes | How it is reread later |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

An effective release changes at least one of the following: information, judgment of suspicion, relationship position, risk, or next action. Repeating a question, intensifying adjectives, or substituting testimony from another character does not pay interest on a delayed answer.

## Provisional Answers

A provisional answer is a temporary conclusion on which a character can take real action. Check:

- Which existing evidence supports it.
- Whether the character genuinely makes a choice based on it.
- Whether a later reveal overturns the interpretation or the fact.
- What cost or new situation follows from the mistaken action.

If the next sentence overturns a provisional answer immediately after it is spoken, the sequence is only consecutive revelation, not a layer of suspense.

## Does Delaying the Answer Pay Interest?

Every time the central question is delayed, record what the text delivers:

- New, verifiable evidence.
- A new interpretation of old evidence.
- A change in relationships or ownership of evidence.
- An active choice by the protagonist and its cost.
- A true answer to a local question.

If consecutive delays merely declare “there is a greater secret,” report circular suspense and identify the reader question that remains unchanged.

## Resolution Quality

| Check | Successful form | Failed form |
|---|---|---|
| Factual resolution | The central question receives a verifiable answer | A new character or new specification supplies the answer |
| Fairness | Earlier evidence remains true under the new interpretation | Early narration lies solely to misdirect |
| Consequences for action | The reveal changes choices, relationships, or responsibility | Nothing changes after the characters hear the explanation |
| Emotional resolution | The final judgment resolves the reader's earlier emotional investment | The case closes without addressing relationships or costs |
| Protagonist agency | The protagonist obtains, verifies, or chooses to use decisive evidence | The answer comes entirely from an antagonist's confession or outside rescue |

## Report Format

```markdown
主悬念：{问题、建立位置、最终回收}
副悬念：{与主问题的服务关系；无则写无}
信息差：{类型、双方知道什么、位置}
证据释放：{按位置列新信息、解释和行动变化}
阶段答案：{支持证据、触发行动、后续修正}
延迟利息：{每次推迟具体交付什么}
回收评价：{事实、公平性、行动、情绪、代理权}
证据不足：{无法从源文确认的项}
```

## Boundaries

- You may report where the source's answer actually appears, but do not judge it early or late against a recommended percentage.
- You may count actual hooks, but do not require a hook in every section or paragraph.
- Do not express suspense strength as a subjective tier; describe its force through reader questions, changes in evidence, and consequences for action.
- Do not provide added hooks, outline revisions, or continuation steps unless the user explicitly requests editorial recommendations.
