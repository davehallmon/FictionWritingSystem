# Emotional Design Methods: Three Moves, Push-Pull Rhythm, and Failure Modes

> Load when designing emotional sequences. First use the general long-form emotional engine below to plan the causal chain. Apply the three moves only as optional techniques in suitable scenes.

---

## Emotional Engine for Long-Form Story Units

Design a **positive engine** that creates and fulfills emotion for every plot unit, then conduct a safety review:

> **Core emotional proposition → carrier/unmet emotional need → obstacle or reason the need remains open → trigger in this cycle → protagonist's irreplaceable ignition/transformation action → changed meaning or visible fulfillment → genre/reader-contract fulfillment**

This causal chain applies to satisfaction, excitement, sweetness, anguish, fear, healing, and other core emotions. The carrier may be a person, relationship, objective, rule, or setting. A genuinely inapplicable link may be marked “none/immediate,” but causality must close. The framework does not require every genre to include prolonged obstruction, a final image, or restrained reaction, nor does it mandate a single mechanism. Misunderstanding, objects, reversals, and death are not compulsory. The “three moves for emotional anguish” below are **optional techniques** suited to some emotional genres, not hard requirements of the general engine.

### Order of Positive Engine and Negative Guardrails

1. **Design the positive engine first**: clarify why readers will invest and how this cycle changes the investment's meaning and delivers visible fulfillment.
2. **Then diagnose with negative guardrails for protagonist agency and growth**: use causal authority + resolution authority, ownership of expectation, and endgame reserves from `reader-contract-and-progression.md` to detect contract or progression failures. “Safe” only means no red line was crossed; it does not mean the story is compelling. Guardrails cannot replace the emotional engine.

### Five Plausibility Questions

1. Why this carrier?
2. Why now?
3. Why was it not resolved directly, or why did it remain suspended?
4. What gives the protagonist the ability or standing to intervene and transform it?
5. How do reality and setting constraints apply?

These questions catch coincidental plot tools, institutions that suddenly become stupid to move the plot, and explanatory patches added after the fact.

### Intensity Principle

Strong emotion comes from a genre-appropriate combination of concrete investment, obstruction or delay, changed meaning, credible reaction, and related mechanisms. Not every mechanism is required, and intensity does not come from piling up death, disease, and crying. Restrained reaction is an optional amplifier for certain emotions. Objects, dialogue, action, identity, rules, and relationships can all serve as optional anchors. Adjust anchor frequency to length and reader-memory needs; never require exactly three repetitions. Collective or thematic elevation is also optional and should expand individual fulfillment into the genre's core emotion only when the genre needs it.

## Three Moves for Emotional Anguish

### Move 1: Lay the Bond, First Third

**Goal**: use concrete objects, numbers, and details to make readers believe the relationship is real.

**Methods**:

- Create duration through exact numbers: “eight years together,” “five years in a coma,” “seven years.”
- Let an object carry emotion: a gold lock given by an older sister, ledger totals documenting debt repayment, a wooden horse carved by a father.
- Establish habit through repeated action: a bowl of porridge every day, visiting every two weeks, freezing awake every winter.

**Examples**:

- *Twenty Years Too Late*: twenty years of ledger entries totaling eighty thousand yuan give weight to a mother-daughter relationship.
- *I Prayed for You Before the Buddha*: five years in a coma, details of praying to gods and Buddhas, and a crush dating to middle school.
- *The Emperor's Younger Brother Bullied My Child*: concrete suffering across seven years as a hostage—kneeling in snow, being slapped, eating spoiled food.

**Core**: the more concrete the bond, the more painful the rupture. Do not use abstractions such as “they loved each other deeply.”

---

### Move 2: Tear the Bond, Middle to Late

**Goal**: create contrast so readers believe they hated the right person, then discover they were wrong, or the reverse.

**Methods**:

- **Contrast**: show warmth first, then destroy it with a cruel truth.
- **Displacement**: Character A believes they are protecting Character B but is actually causing harm.
- **Delayed truth**: reveal decisive information when readers least expect it.

**Examples**:

- *My Brother-in-Law Charged for Delivering Formula*: the mother appears weak and pitiable but has always manipulated the older sister for money. The gold lock is fake—gold-plated copper—creating the rupture.
- *The Emperor's Younger Brother Bullied My Child*: Lady Wang appears gentle, giving a hand warmer and preparing medicine, but actually drugs the former empress into madness and drives her to death with calculated words.
- *My Reborn Younger Brother*: the brother loves his girlfriend, who is a scammer; the pregnancy is false; he is trafficked into a telecom-fraud compound.

**Core**: the force of rupture depends on the depth of setup. Without a convincing bond, the rupture has no roots.

---

### Move 3: Dull After-Pain, Ending

**Goal**: penetrate readers' defenses with quiet details rather than loud grief.

**Methods**:

- Carry great emotion through everyday action: continue feeding porridge, fold the clothes, do not look back.
- Create aftertaste through objects: a broken gold lock, wooden horse, bloodstained ring.
- Use refusal to leave space: no explanation, no backward glance, no tears.

**Examples**:

- *The Emperor's Younger Brother Bullied My Child*: “He held the little wooden horse I had polished smooth… It fit his hand perfectly.”
- *My Brother-in-Law Charged for Delivering Formula*: “My phone became much quieter. The troubles that had weighed on me for so long became very, very small in an instant.”
- *Scoring My Future Daughter-in-Law*: “After the adoption papers were signed, she entered this unfamiliar home for the first time… I knew I had chosen correctly this time.”

**Core**: the best aftertaste does not depend on sobbing. It remains with readers long after they finish.

## Emotional Push-Pull Rhythm

### Basic Curve

```
温暖 → 残忍 → 善意 → 真相 → 原谅 → 来不及 → 释然 → 细节暴击
```

Not every story uses the full curve. Select a subset by genre:

| Genre | Typical rhythm | Core push-pull |
|------|----------|----------|
| Social/wish fulfillment | Oppression → endurance → eruption → humiliation of opponent | Fast emotional rebound and dense payoffs |
| Emotional/heartbreaking | Sweetness → fracture → attempted recovery → missed chance | Slow setup and lasting effect |
| Historical romance/revenge | Endurance → planning → exposure → retribution | Simple setting and direct violent beauty |
| Mystery/detective | Calm → unease → truth → shock | Information gaps create unease |
| Period/family | Misunderstanding → conflict → truth → reconciliation | Generational conflict and period texture |

### Push-Pull Principles

1. **Frequency of emotional turns is a genre- and length-based heuristic**: adjust it to genre, length, and the established fulfillment cadence; never require a turn every three to five sections.
2. **Every turn needs a triggering event**; emotion cannot change without cause.
3. **The final turn determines the ending's emotional aftertaste.**
4. **Wish fulfillment may rebound quickly; tragedy needs longer setup.**

## Emotional Strategies by Genre

### Social/Wish-Fulfillment Fiction

- Emotional rebound: fast, with retaliation two or three sections after humiliation.
- Payoff density: once every three to five sections.
- Antagonist arrogance: escalating from minor to extreme.
- Ending register: exhilarating and cathartic.
- Example: *Scoring My Future Daughter-in-Law*—countering the scoring system, the zero-score metal box, severing ties.

### Emotional/Heartbreaking Fiction

- Bond-detail density: high; establish a deep bond in the first third.
- Contrast: warm before cold, sweet before bitter.
- Aftertaste: quiet ending + object detail.
- Ending register: unresolved ache or acceptance.
- Example: *My Brother-in-Law Charged for Delivering Formula*—truth of the gold lock → false love → independence.

### Historical Romance/Revenge

- Keep setting and relationships clear; avoid needlessly complex worldbuilding.
- Make retaliation direct and clean.
- Reveal the greatest trump card in the final quarter.
- Ending register: satisfying retribution.
- Example: *The Emperor's Younger Brother Bullied My Child*—order twenty lashes → uncover the truth → depose the empress.

### Mystery/Detective

- Use information asymmetry: readers know what characters do not, or the reverse.
- Eliminate possibilities progressively until only the truth remains.
- Reveal what happened before revealing why.
- Ending register: disturbing in hindsight or sudden understanding.
- Example: *My Reborn Younger Brother*—rebirth + scammer identity + telecom-fraud compound.

### Period/Family

- Do not take sides in generational conflict; show both sides' pain.
- Build texture with era-specific objects and customs.
- Do not reconcile quickly; let both sides be fully hurt first.
- Ending register: warmth mixed with regret.
- Example: *My Brother-in-Law Charged for Delivering Formula*—parent-child relationship → truth of the gold lock → rupture.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Recognition | Correction |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Too flat** | Five or more consecutive sections without an emotional turn | Insert an unexpected event or new information |
| **Too rushed** | Major reversal lacks recognizable setup | Match setup to reversal weight and total length; do not mandate three sections |
| **False anguish** | Readers feel discomfort but not concern | Make the bond concrete |
| **Disconnected** | First and second halves feel like separate stories | Connect them through clues, objects, or theme |
| **Overextended ending** | Story keeps repeating explanations after the reversal | Resolve or cool down at a genre- and length-appropriate speed; do not impose 500 Chinese characters |
| **Character collapse** | Behavior at a decisive moment contradicts established characterization | Restore consistent behavioral logic |

## Quick Self-Check

- [ ] Does the first third contain concrete bond details rather than abstraction?
- [ ] Does the middle contain contrast or rupture—readers expect A and discover B?
- [ ] Does the ending use a quiet detail rather than extended sentiment?
- [ ] Is turn frequency adapted to genre, length, and established fulfillment cadence rather than a fixed density?
- [ ] Does antagonist behavior follow established logic?
- [ ] Are all six failure modes absent?
