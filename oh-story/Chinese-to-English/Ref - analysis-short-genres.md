# Genre Identification Scale for Short Source Stories

> Use only to identify and compare existing short source stories. It describes observable reader promises, conflict vehicles, and common failure modes. It does not provide writing steps and does not evaluate short fiction using long-form opening or volume-level models.

## How to Use

First assign one primary genre based on evidence from the complete text, then assign one secondary genre if necessary. A genre designation cannot be determined solely by a tag, setting, or individual plot beat.

Every genre judgment must answer:

1. What core emotion does the source story deliver consistently?
2. Which locatable plot events carry that promise?
3. What does the ending actually settle?
4. If genres are mixed, which owns the main plot and which merely supplies a device or atmosphere?

## Genre Routing

| Genre | Observable identification anchors | Core reader promise | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Groveling-after-breakup romance | Harm, departure, regret, rejection, or costly reconciliation forms a causal chain | Belated recognition and reversal of power in the relationship | An ordinary tragic romance contains suffering but no post-regret shift in agency |
| Rebirth revenge | Information from a previous life changes present choices, and old causality is settled anew | Correction, retribution, or repair of regret enabled by information asymmetry | A rebirth premise alone is not a mechanism if no key choice changes |
| Marriage/family social realism | Everyday interests, family ethics, property, or caregiving duties drive conflict | Injustice within familiar relationships is exposed and settled | Suspense may serve as a means of gathering evidence without being the primary genre |
| Scum-punishment/public reckoning | The protagonist obtains and chooses to release evidence, and the antagonist suffers visible consequences | Public retaliation after suppression and restoration of responsibility | Retribution from nowhere weakens the protagonist's agency |
| Suspense/mystery | Interpretations of evidence keep changing, and the truth changes action or judgments of responsibility | Fair information control and correction of understanding | Mysterious atmosphere without a verifiable evidence chain is insufficient for a suspense classification |
| Detail-clue revenge | Anomalies in objects, records, timing, or wording gradually form a chain of responsibility | Small anomalies expand into a provable truth | A clue that creates atmosphere but changes no judgment is not a structural pivot |
| Dead-narrator literature/spirit viewpoint | Death or absence changes information distribution, and the living understand the relationship anew when it is too late | Indebtedness, regret, and irreversible loss | A dead person's viewpoint used only as a narration format is not necessarily the primary genre |
| Sweet romance/relationship comedy | Relationship progress, contrast, or shared predicaments consistently create positive rewards | Security, intimacy, and payoff from light conflict | Do not force reversal or revenge metrics onto its event density |
| Folklore/rules horror | Explicit or implied rules constrain choices, and violations create traceable consequences | Testing rules, survival judgment, and pressure from the unknown | Rules that can be rewritten at any moment destroy fairness |
| Urban system/high-concept story | One anomalous mechanism changes how a real-world problem can be solved, with traceable rules, limits, and costs | Seeing how a new mechanism is used, misused, or abandoned | The appearance of a system does not justify imposing long-form progression or map-expansion structures |
| Xianxia/fantasy short story | A small set of supernatural rules serves one complete conflict and emotional settlement | Choice, cost, or identity reversal within unfamiliar rules | Numerous worldbuilding tiers do not make the structure complete |
| Historical/alternate-history short story | Historical circumstances or knowledge directly changes one key choice | A known turning point is reinterpreted or rewritten | Cataloged knowledge that never enters character causality is merely background information |
| Entertainment industry/workplace | Professional ability, organizational power, and public judgment participate in settling the conflict | Ability is validated, power is countered, or identity is reevaluated | Long chains of reactions are merely spectator noise if the main plot does not change |
| Fan fiction | A familiar world or character relationship is rewritten by one new variable | Reinterpretation produced by familiarity and deviation | Borrowing names without preserving the original relationship function is insufficient for a fan-fiction structure |

## Mixed-Genre Determination

Use the following evidence priority to distinguish primary from secondary genres:

| Priority | Question |
|---|---|
| Main-plot ownership | If this genre were removed, would the core goal and causal chain still hold? |
| Climax ownership | Which type of conflict does the final irreversible choice settle? |
| Emotional ownership | Does the reader ultimately receive exhilaration, pain, sweetness, fear, relief, or cognitive shock? |
| Means versus end | Are suspense, systems, rebirth, and similar elements what the story must settle, or tools that advance another main plot? |

If either of two genres can be removed without affecting the main plot, genre evidence is insufficient. Report “general short fiction + element tags” rather than forcing a classification.

## Report Format

```markdown
主类型：{类型}
副类型：{可选}
定位证据：{节次/原句/情节点}
主线所有权：{哪类冲突拥有高潮与结算}
符合常见模式：{观察到的功能，不写合格/不合格}
偏离或改造：{源文如何改变常见模式}
不确定项：{证据不足/待复核}
```

## Failure Checks

- Do not treat recommended proportions, a fixed number of sections, or a particular platform template as conditions for a genre to qualify.
- Do not invoke long-form progression and volume-level scales merely because labels such as “system,” “cultivation,” or “history” appear.
- Do not replace source-text evidence with “a required scene.” The absence of a common scene indicates deviation, not automatic failure.
- Do not state authorial intent as fact; report only what the text consistently delivers.
