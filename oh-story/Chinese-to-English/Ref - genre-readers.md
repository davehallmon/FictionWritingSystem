## Reader Psychology and Genre Fit

### Infer Psychology from Breakout Works

- Extract central plot elements from breakout works → define the reader persona → infer psychological needs → validate from two directions.
- Breakout works sustain one emotion throughout; *Battle Through the Heavens* carries “do not look down on the young and poor” from beginning to end. Weaker works scatter attention among unrelated emotions.
- Minor emotions such as progression, revenge, and treasure hunting must serve the primary emotion.
- Validate the inferred emotional need against target readers' real circumstances.
- Pay special attention to elements outsiders consider intolerable but core readers love.

### Genre → Persona → Deep Need

| Genre | Reader persona | Three-layer psychological need |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Apocalyptic rebirth | People who feel hopeless about class mobility | Reversed order and desire for power + insecurity and survival anxiety + windfall with maximum return for minimum effort |
| God-of-War live-in son-in-law | Middle-aged men under financial pressure | Pain of helplessness → anger at not being understood → shame → justification for endurance → explosive reversal |
| General web fiction | — | Deep need for recognition, understanding, and respect; surface “face-slapping” is only the shell |

### Finding Deep Needs

- Enter the audience's collective psychology: what do they love, hate, desire, and avoid?
- Different genres imply different personas; identify the reader before designing emotion.
- Before writing, ask what target readers lack most and want most in real life.
- Emotional depth determines the ceiling. Deeper needs create stronger resonance.

### Emotional Roots

Emotion grows from “unfairness” and “why.” Unequal treatment naturally feels unjust and generates emotion.

Urban example: a modest career success → father wants startup money → mother wants a wedding apartment for the younger brother → unfairness emerges naturally.

## Genre Longevity

- Judge longevity through current samples, not historical experience treated as present fact.
- Use the current scan/analyze workflow or user-specified samples to confirm active response on the target platform.
- Classify the stage: novelty / maturity / fatigue.
- During novelty, extract creative directions. During maturity, deliver stable genre expectations. During fatigue, provide a new entry point.
- Trend-following may reuse structural functions, never plot content.
- If the stage cannot be confirmed, treat it as mature: fulfill boundary expectations conservatively and use no more than three micro-innovations.

### Three Eras

| Era | Characteristics |
|------|------|
| Free-form | No fixed style; inspiration from traditional literature and comics |
| Gamified | Levels + stages + bosses + instances + equipment, designed to encourage consumption |
| Everyday-life tendency | Story over leveling, lived detail, contemporary relevance, anti-gamification; validate current applicability with platform samples |

## Cross-Platform Genre Adaptation

The same genre is written differently on different sites because audiences differ. Never apply samples from Platform A directly to Platform B. Calibrate expectations, pace, and red lines with target-platform samples.

### Core Platform Differences

| Platform | Core driver | Poor fit |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Ciweimao | Playful bits and characterization, rooted in anime fan fiction and gacha games | Nationwide-scale or high-powered martial-arts fiction usually fails |
| Qidian, excluding light novels | Pace and identification through conventional plot advancement | Pure joke-driven fiction is hard to sustain |
| Qidian Light Novels | Bits + characterization + pace | Requires sustained ability to generate playful material |
| Fanqie | Strong emotion, gimmicks, direct satisfaction | Genres requiring slow-burn setup |

### Reader Differences

| Platform | Reader characteristics |
|------|----------|
| Qidian | High selection cost; readers are more patient and tolerate slower pacing |
| New media, age 30+ | Prefers stories closer to reality, where life's setbacks are normal |

**Key**: identical execution produces radically different results across platforms. Adapt deliberately.

### Ciweimao Essentials

Validate samples from the target fandom first | Add high concepts within subculture | Let fandom acceptance determine logic | Reduce combat and write more daily life | Memes matter heavily and require calibration against recent rankings/comments

### Fanqie Title A/B Strategy

Prepare alternatives based on the cheat ability / first story unit / main plot / a conservative variant near the original title / a slightly exaggerated traffic-oriented variant. Filter through target-platform rules and user requirements.

## Genre Boundaries

### Established vs. Unbounded

| Type | Characteristics | Best for |
|------|------|----------|
| Established boundaries | Mature audience and known conventions | Stable delivery with sufficient sample validation |
| Unbounded/innovative | Uncertain audience and experimental execution | Higher risk; reduce length and number of innovations |

### Three Cores of Fantasy

- **Identification**: a cyberpunk fantasy empire can create far stronger identification; physical transcendence and visceral strikes are easier to inhabit than abstract magical exchanges.
- **Anticipation**: sustain it throughout and reader loss slows.
- **Alignment**: identification with the protagonist creates loyal readers who are difficult to lose. Minor flaws are acceptable; fundamental justice is not negotiable.

### Four Elements of Ambiguity in Urban Male-Audience Fiction

1. **Women**: in this convention, urban male-audience fiction requires female characters.
2. **Ease**: readers seek relaxation and pleasure, not relentless misery.
3. **Interaction**: interaction among characters plus a private “secret” interaction between reader and protagonist.
4. **Privacy**: conceal the protagonist's ability so reader and protagonist share a secret.

### Cheat-Ability Design

Tie it closely to the protagonist's life and profession. A medical manual for a doctor is credible; invisibility is forced. Connect it to the main plot, allow one skill to upgrade and produce varied effects, and avoid repeatedly adding new cheat abilities.

### Historical-Fiction Red Lines

- Core stance: an enlightened ruler's equality grounded in nationalism. Inconsistent positions create severe rupture.
- Historical web fiction is not academic history; the reader, not factual completeness, is the first consideration.
- Assess historical figures in line with contemporary authoritative consensus, especially evaluations of `教员` (“the Chairman”); do not cross established boundaries.
- Manage the payoff ceiling. If family or faction status rises too quickly, later escalation becomes unsustainable.

## Identification and Artificiality

### Identification

Identification overlaps strongly with satisfaction. When it is low, nothing the protagonist does feels rewarding. Greater identification increases satisfaction and desire to continue.

### Sources of Artificiality

Xianxia that does not feel immortal, wuxia that lacks chivalry, or clashing aesthetics such as laboratory research in an immortal world. Remove artificiality by making the world feel lived-in rather than made of paper.

### Why Fan Fiction Retains Readers Early

The worldbuilding is already solid and initial identification is high. Exception: comedy and meme fiction prioritizes entertainment over satisfaction, uses an observer stance, and does not require strong identification.

### Effective Rankings Scan

Ranking-scan rules: (1) run the current scan/analyze workflow for recent target-platform samples; (2) exclude leaders with built-in fan bases; and (3) analyze common opening structures in reproducible samples. Establish the core conflict within the first 300 Chinese characters; the first 10,000 determine competitiveness.

### Commercial Awareness

Non-core personal expression must remain under 5% and may not interrupt narrative pace. Commercialization does not mean deliberately antagonizing readers. Every personal statement must serve the core selling point and cannot exist independently of the main plot.

## Title Techniques

### Identity Method: About One-Third of Titles

| Category | Formula | Example |
|------|------|------|
| High-status identity | [immortal emperor/demon god/tycoon] parents + [contrasting behavior/goal] | “A Martial-God Dad and Tycoon Mom—What's Wrong with Coasting to Victory?” |
| Dramatic identity contrast | [low-status person] does [unexpected feat] for [high-status person] | “You Were Hired as a Page, and Took First Place in the Imperial Exam for Your Young Master?” |
| Suggestive identity relationship | Use a relationship to imply ambiguity | High risk but extremely effective at attracting traffic |

### Other Methods

| Method | Core | Platform |
|------|------|----------|
| Secret-identity reveal | Hidden identity emerges gradually | Use cautiously if recent target samples are saturated |
| Prestige naming | Highly evocative wording | Qidian |
| Antonym | Positive/negative contrast creates curiosity | General |
| Main plot + payoff | Central action + implied satisfaction | Qidian |

### Platform Differences

| Platform | Style | Key principle |
|------|------|----------|
| Fanqie | Gimmick first, traffic first | Title determines 80% of traffic ceiling |
| Qidian | Mild contrast + cheat ability + main plot + payoff | Content must fulfill the title |

## Synopsis and Marketing-Copy System

### Five Basic Types

| Type | Structure | Effect | Best use |
|------|------|------|------|
| Four-part success | Situation → conflict → action → success | Security and clear victory | Most universal |
| Three-part action | Situation → conflict → action, then cut | Suspense | Career-centered fiction |
| Two-part mystery | Situation → one reversal/mystery | Curiosity | Mystery, cerebral, niche |
| Four-part failure | Situation → conflict → action → failure | Pain and anger | Groveling pursuit |
| Three-part success | Situation → turn → success | Concise and light | Sweet/light fiction |

### Combination Strategy

- Success + mystery = security + curiosity.
- Action + success = anticipation then fulfillment.
- Failure + mystery = pain followed by suspense.

### Copy Essentials

- The heroine must have agency. Weak agency creates both attraction and retention risk.
- Readers need security; at minimum, imply that the protagonist will succeed.
- A mismatch between title, copy, and product drives readers away.
- Do not stack too many mysteries; excessive opacity reduces understanding and security.

## Quality Checklist

> Verify every item after positioning the concept. The result passes only when all do.

- [ ] **Clear persona**: one sentence identifies target readers and what they lack in real life.
- [ ] **Deep-need alignment**: the genre's core emotion meets the audience's need rather than indulging the writer.
- [ ] **Longevity assessment**: current samples confirm the genre's stage; historical popularity is not treated as present fact.
- [ ] **Platform adaptation**: execution is tailored to the target rather than copied from another site.
- [ ] **Boundary confirmation**: current material, knowledge, and length can support the genre.
- [ ] **Three-second title**: under target-platform conventions, it communicates the selling point or hook within three seconds.
- [ ] **Synopsis has security + hook**: the correct type provides reassurance while preserving suspense.
- [ ] **Identification without artificiality**: worldbuilding is coherent and aesthetics do not clash.
- [ ] **Title, synopsis, and content unify**: title promise = synopsis promise = prose delivery.
- [ ] **Cheat ability fits life**: the ability connects closely to the protagonist's work or life rather than being attached arbitrarily.
