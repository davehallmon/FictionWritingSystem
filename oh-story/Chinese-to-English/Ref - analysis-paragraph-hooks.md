# Source-Text Paragraph-Hook Analysis Scale
Types of short-form/paragraph-level hooks, combination techniques, prohibitions, escalating dialogue emotion, unjust harm, and spectator tiers. Use for designing micro-hooks within paragraphs.

---

## 11 Types of Paragraph-Level Hooks

| # | Name | Formula | Example | Effectiveness |
|---|------|------|------|--------|
| 1 | Information gap | Readers know key information that the character does not | “He accepted the coffee with a smile, unaware that she had just slipped a sleeping pill into it.” | ★5 |
| 2 | Countdown | Establish explicit time pressure | “Only three days remained in the divorce cooling-off period. In three days, whether he signed would no longer matter.” | ★4 |
| 3 | Reversal | Abruptly break reader expectations | “She opened the will. The beneficiary field was blank. Beneath the next page lay her mother's death certificate.” | ★5 |
| 4 | Hidden card | The protagonist withholds a move, and readers wait for the reveal | “My mother-in-law berated me for ten full minutes. I said nothing. My phone was still recording.” | ★4 |
| 5 | Public comeuppance | A character gloats while readers know humiliation is imminent | “【You? You think you can compete with me?】 She doubled over laughing. I said nothing. I simply took the paternity-test report from my bag.” | ★5 |
| 6 | Cost | Imply that the protagonist will lose something or pay dearly for a choice | “I knew what reporting him meant. If I reported him, I was finished too. I pressed Send anyway.” | ★4 |
| 7 | Vulnerable person/child | Introduce someone who needs protection to awaken protective feeling | “My daughter hid behind the door with her hands over her ears. She was only four and understood nothing. But she knew she must not make a sound.” | ★4 |
| 8 | Spirit observer | The protagonist is dead and narrates as an omniscient but powerless spirit | “My spirit slowly floated upward as I watched Mother's ashen face. I'm sorry, Mom. I couldn't remain standing.” | ★5 |
| 9 | Anomalous object | An object that should not be present triggers the larger plot | “The date on the condom had changed, and even the flavor was now mango—the flavor I used to be allergic to. I smiled and nodded.” | ★5 |
| 10 | Feigned submission | After rebirth, voluntarily surrender what the protagonist desperately protected in the previous life; the opponent thinks she has gone mad | “‘If you want it, you can have all of it.’ Whoever inherited that enormous fortune was headed for disaster; I had been searching for a way to survive.” | ★4 |
| 11 | Cold discovery | The first reaction to an anomaly is extreme calm; the calm itself creates suspense | “The keychain read ‘Yue'an Maternity Center VIP.’ We did not have children. I smiled and nodded. That same day, I went to the center and copied down the appointment.” | ★5 |

### Usage Notes
- Do not prolong an information gap beyond 3,000 Chinese characters; readers will become frustrated.
- Once a countdown is established, it must pay off at expiry; do not abandon it.
- A reversal requires setup. A reversal without setup is cheating.
- Do not hide a card too long; in short fiction, conceal it for no more than 30% of the work.
- Deliver comeuppance cleanly. One decisive sentence is more satisfying than a paragraph.
- A cost must be real. If the protagonist ultimately loses nothing, the cost hook fails.
- A vulnerable person cannot be merely a plot device; give them an action line of their own.
- Strictly honor the limitation of a spirit viewpoint: it may observe but cannot act.

## Hook Combinations

### Basic Combinations

| Combination | Effect | Suitable genres |
|------|------|----------|
| Information gap + hidden card | Readers know the protagonist is withholding a decisive move | Revenge, social realism |
| Countdown + cost | Urgent time pressure + every choice loses something | Suspense, marriage/romance |
| Reversal + comeuppance | Emotional roller coaster | All gratification-oriented short fiction |
| Vulnerable person + cost | Maximum empathy | Groveling romance, social realism, dead-narrator literature |
| Hidden card + comeuppance | Strongest gratification | Revenge, comeback |

### Advanced Combinations

| Combination | Effect |
|------|------|
| Anomalous object + cold discovery | Everyday suspense + intellectual dominance |
| Spirit observer + vulnerable person | Extreme tragedy + protective feeling |
| Feigned submission + hidden card | Triple information gap + accumulating gratification |
| Escalating betrayal + cold discovery | Layered escalation + sustained calm |

### Principles for Combining Hooks
1. Techniques serve expression; never use one merely for its own sake.
2. During plotting, consider multiple techniques to enrich the plot.
3. Techniques may be blended; discard any that do not help.
4. Do not derive a plot from one technique. Techniques are tools, not constraints.

## Hook Prohibitions

| Prohibition | Explanation |
|------|------|
| False suspense | The threat does not exist or resolves immediately; deceive readers once and they will stop trusting the story |
| Mechanical deus ex machina | End a chapter on a crisis, then solve it by coincidence at the start of the next |
| Excessive withholding | Reveal nothing across several consecutive chapters until readers lose patience |
| Low-stakes hook | Manufacture suspense around something inconsequential |
| Repeating one type | Use the same hook type for 3 or more consecutive chapters |

## Five Levels of Escalating Dialogue Emotion

In conflict, increasingly impolite dialogue carries increasingly strong emotion:

| Level | Type | Intensity |
|------|------|------|
| 1 | Objective statement of fact | Weakest |
| 2 | Objective statement + suggestion | Polite but forceful |
| 3 | Subjective accusation | Conflict begins to register |
| 4 | Subjective accusation + compulsory command | Strong emotion |
| 5 | Subjective accusation + self-elevation through PUA | Strongest |

The strongest tactic: hide behind “this is for your own good”; every sentence sounds concerned, yet every sentence conveys contempt, blame, or disgust.

## Unjust-Harm Hooks

Unjust harm = unfairness (the protagonist is innocent) + harm (physical + psychological)

- Psychological harm more readily moves readers because most readers have experienced resentment or unfair treatment.
- “People do not resent scarcity so much as unequal distribution”: reducing everyone's allowance is fair; downgrading only the protagonist's medicine is unfair.
- Any one form, executed fully, can build hatred toward the antagonist.

### Three Types of Harm

| Type | Motivation | Behavior | Attitude |
|------|------|------|------|
| Benefit transfer | The antagonist refuses to bear the cost | Plunder, command, or coerce the protagonist | Entitled and arrogant |
| Loss transfer | The antagonist shifts impending punishment onto the protagonist | Threaten, entice, set a trap, or force the protagonist to take the blame | Covert and sinister |
| Direct opposition | The protagonist disrupts the order that supports the antagonist | Frame, scheme against, or harm | Leaves no path to survival |

### The Right Way to Suppress the Protagonist
1. Avoid it where possible—replace “the protagonist genuinely suffers injustice” with “others dismiss the protagonist, who proves them wrong.”
2. Make rejection indirect—replace face-to-face ridicule with internal thought or discussion behind the protagonist's back.
3. Make the reason for rejection plausible—a reasonable judgment based on available information.
4. Diversify rejecting characters—different positions produce different forms of rejection.
5. Apply only slight suppression at the volume climax.

## Spectator-Quality Tiers

The impact of astonishment depends on both the number and quality of spectators:

| Tier | Subjects | Characteristics |
|------|------|------|
| Low quality | Extras, assistants, interns | Pure emotional reaction with no consequence |
| Medium quality | Knowledgeable practitioners, deputies | Can make professional comparisons and change their attitudes |
| High quality | Director-level figures, industry leaders | Reactions reveal information, advance relationships, and create anticipation |

### Principles for Expanding Spectator Tiers

| Dimension | Principle |
|------|------|
| Character types | Multiple types > one type; each produces a different reaction |
| Character count | Many > few; choose situations where several can appear |
| Character tiers | Multiple tiers > one tier; bystanders/peers/experts/antagonists react differently |
| Relationship depth | Acquaintances > strangers; astonishment from someone familiar is far more satisfying |

## Story-Breakdown Checklist

For every hook in a story breakdown, evaluate:

1. What type of hook is it?
2. In which sentence/at which character does it appear?
3. What is its intensity (1–10)?
4. Does it pay off at an appropriate point?
5. Is the distance between setup and payoff appropriate?
6. Is any hook left unresolved (abandoned)?
