# Story-Decomposition Examples (Three Complete Cases)

> Use these structural examples during story decomposition. Select the closest type through the decision router, then compare your decomposition against the example.

## Decision Routing

| Type of story being decomposed | Reference case | Focus |
|---------------|---------|--------|
| Contemporary tragic romance/reversal | Case 1: Blind Female Bodyguard × CEO | Feigned blindness + double meaning + diary reveal |
| Historical tragic romance/identity reversal | Case 2: Concubine-Born Daughter × General | Transferred token of love + rise from low status |
| Political intrigue/multiple relationships | Case 3: Grand Princess × Four Childhood Companions | Proactive planning + information-gap design |

## Case 1: Contemporary Tragic Romance (Blind Female Bodyguard × CEO)

**Introduction**
- Background: The heroine is a blind bodyguard who saved the hero; he promised to care for her for life.
- Pain point: She discovers his affair with the female rival but endures it out of gratitude.
- Reversal: She is only pretending to be blind and has seen every betrayal.
- Hooks: She can see, but nobody knows | She gives herself only seven days to leave.

**Setup:** She hears the hero arranging a meeting with the rival while she is in the kitchen -> carries out soup while pretending not to know -> a message from the rival appears on his phone, and she “happens” to see the screen -> pain point: he shows no caution in front of her.

**Development + Turn 1:** She discovers she is pregnant and plans to tell him -> catches him celebrating the rival's birthday -> sees that the rival's necklace is the token of love she once gave him -> escalation: the token has been passed to someone else.

**Development:** She silently returns home to pack -> he asks about her eyes -> she says, “Much better. I can see everything now” (a double meaning he misses).

**Development + Turn 2:** At his birthday banquet, the rival attends as his partner while the heroine stands in a corner as the “bodyguard” -> the heroine publicly announces the divorce and hands him the agreement -> payoff: the entire room is stunned.

**Resolution:** After she leaves, he finds the diary she left behind -> it records every betrayal she “saw” -> he collapses, but she has disappeared -> payoff plus suspense.

## Case 2: Historical Tragic Romance (Concubine-Born Daughter × General)

**Introduction**
- Background: The concubine-born daughter and the general have been secret lovers for three years; he promises to marry her after the war.
- Pain point: He returns victorious with a principal wife—her legitimate elder sister.
- Reversal: She discovers that she was only a “practice tool” for her sister.
- Hooks: Can she rise from bonded low status to the emperor's bed? | How deeply will the general regret his choice?

**Setup:** While embroidering in the rear courtyard, she hears that the general has returned -> joyfully prepares her wedding clothes -> hears her sister at the gate: “Husband, you have returned.”

**Development + Turn 1:** She is forced to serve tea to her sister -> overhears, “I only used her for practice; don't mind it” -> pain point: three years of love were only instrumental.

**Development:** Outwardly compliant, she secretly approaches a crown-prince banquet -> gains the prince's attention through her talent -> gathers strength within suffering.

**Development + Turn 2:** The general is shocked to learn that the prince has taken her into his household -> she publicly reveals that the general rose through her sister -> payoff: the identity reversal destroys his standing.

**Resolution:** After becoming the crown prince's secondary consort, she rises to principal consort -> the general is hunted on the battlefield with nobody coming to his aid -> before dying, he sees her portrait and finally regrets everything -> payoff: belated regret hurts more than death.

## Case 3: Political-Intrigue Princess (Grand Princess × Four Childhood Companions)

**Introduction**
- Background: The grand princess has four childhood-companion fiancés, but loves only the general who does not love her.
- Pain point: She chooses the man with the least power and gives up the general who has quietly protected her.
- Reversal: The man she chooses is responsible for harming her mother, the empress.
- Hooks: What follows when the princess gives up true love? | How will the abandoned man respond?

**Setup:** Marriage is decided by drawing lots, and the princess appears compliant -> she secretly rigs the draw so the weakest man is selected -> the reader knows she is protecting the general.

**Development + Turn 1:** The selected consort reveals his ambition -> she discovers his correspondence with an enemy state -> pain point: by trying to protect someone, the princess has nurtured a threat.

**Development:** The princess is confined while the general campaigns elsewhere, unaware -> she uses covert channels to send messages and counterattack -> gathers strength within suffering.

**Development + Turn 2:** The general returns to court and discovers her imprisonment -> she has already arranged the counterattack and is waiting for the right moment; the rescue is only cover -> payoff: the princess was never weak.

**Resolution:** The consort rebels and is suppressed; the princess personally appears on the city tower -> the general asks why she did not choose him years ago -> “Because only when you were away from me could you become the strongest blade” -> payoff plus resonance.

## Story-Decomposition Checklist

1. **Label emotion:** Mark each paragraph as pain, payoff, or gathering strength, then assess the distribution.
2. **Find hooks:** Identify at least two hooks in each story, including at least one information-gap hook.
3. **Check structure:** Confirm a complete setup-development-turn-resolution structure and credible turns.
4. **Inspect setup:** Confirm at least two setup clues before every reversal.
5. **Assess payoff strength:** Confirm that the largest payoff emerges from converging prior causes, choices, and evidence and leaves sufficient space for consequences.
