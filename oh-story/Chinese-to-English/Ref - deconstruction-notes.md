## Core Emotional Control

### Self-Check Rules

During deconstruction or review, mark whether the text drifts from the core hook and whether passages serve only the writer's desire for expression rather than reader expectations. When a passage drifts from the core hook, report it as a retention risk, not a subjective judgment.

### Cases of Core Emotional Collapse

| Problem | Lesson |
|------|------|
| The protagonist stands alone above the ages after everyone close to them dies | Readers identify with the protagonist, then feel frustrated that “invincibility yields nothing” |
| A map change kills every supporting character | Retention and continued-reading risk rises sharply; readers struggle to accept the erasure of everything they invested in |
| Everyone is sacrificed as a plot device | A strong opening declines into emotional collapse |

Correction: create tragedy through setbacks, not by killing important characters. During a map change, putting established characters in danger can give the protagonist a reason to act.

### Principles for Killing Characters

- Killing a character is a means, not an end, and demands exceptional craft.
- When a death is necessary, plant clues and leave room for emotional or narrative compensation.
- Do not kill an important character unless the project has sufficient setup, a compensation mechanism, and a later emotional-recovery plan.
- Calibrate tragic intensity against recent samples from the target platform. By default, do not let tragedy overwhelm the core sense of satisfaction or safety.

### Validating Strong-Emotion Samples

If recent target-platform samples frequently use highly emotional stories, record the trigger, release rhythm, gimmick placement, and retention evidence during deconstruction. Without recent samples, treat strong emotion as a candidate hypothesis rather than a hard trend.

## Genre Boundaries and the Core Hook

### Core Hook = Reader Need; Drift = Abandoning the Reader

| Type of drift | Consequence |
|----------|------|
| The central contrast disappears (urban high-powered martial arts → ordinary life) | Active readership drops immediately |
| The central mechanism stops evolving (a game story remains trapped in the beginner village) | The ceiling stays low |
| Genres contaminate one another (fantasy mixed with uncanny horror, or mystery veering into fantasy) | Neither audience is satisfied |
| Values drift late in the story | Audience filtering becomes too severe |

### Four Dimensions for Extending a Core Hook

Using an “early-arrival counterattack system” as an example: (1) number of missions, (2) reward difficulty, (3) levels/classes plus hidden triggers, and (4) rewards that trigger new dramatic missions. Using only the first two quickly exhausts dramatic potential; combining all four sustains it.

### Core-Hook Density Reference

*The Food That Granted Me Attributes* applies its core hook five times within eleven chapters—extremely high density.

## Book-Deconstruction Method

### Six Principles

Have a purpose / use a method / extract theory / set aside personal taste / keep strengths and avoid weaknesses / deconstruct books you like

### Process

Summarize in one sentence → identify structural units (setup, development, turn, resolution; progressive expansion) → isolate the framework and emotional chain → borrow the framework, not the content

### Conditional-Framework Extraction

Reduce a plot to conditions—for example, partial display of talent + competition between two powers + information asymmetry that reveals a perceptive judge. Replace characters, background, and cheat ability while preserving a similar emotional effect.

### Five-Step Abstraction

Summarize → divide into information clusters (emotional turning points) → classify as rise/fall/turn → extract a reusable conditional framework → fill it with new material

### Deconstructing Romance: The Double-Standard Method

Use the same action, such as offering a soda: the heroine refuses when a male supporting character offers it, then accepts when the male lead does. Three requirements: (1) nearly the same action occurs in close succession, (2) her attitude differs sharply, and (3) the male lead does not know.

## Structural Design and Pacing

### Tools for Emotional Push and Pull

| Technique | Method |
|------|------|
| Learn familiar emotional patterns | Choose a pattern → build characters → fill in plot |
| Restore broken anticipation | Introduce a new character or event to raise it again |
| Foundation of showing off and face-slapping | Status hierarchy + social stage + positive and negative propagation |
| Multifunctional supporting character | The protagonist's “white glove”: speaks on their behalf + gives absolute support + supplies comedy |

### Telescope Method for Building Anticipation

Use a countdown structure and repeatedly depict “discovery → observation → salvage → failure → impact.” Readers know the bullet will eventually strike the protagonist, creating enormous anticipation.

### Volume Pacing: *The Genius Club*

Each volume runs 200,000–300,000 Chinese characters and has a clear core and objective. Multiple threads tangle inside the volume, breaking linear setup-development-turn-resolution. The opening must cast a hook, and every volume ending plants a thread for the next.

### Adapting to the Era

Open with strong conflict → develop through human warmth and basic needs; use more delicate prose; create a powerful sense of cinematic cutting; resolve familiar formulas quickly within two or three chapters.

## Selling-Point Loop Design

### Perfect Loop: *Disaster Messenger*

Use an alternate identity to show off, humiliate opponents, and earn benefits → use the benefits to complete the main mission → receive rewards → strengthen the alternate identity → enter a new map and repeat. Key points: each cycle must differ; emotional progression sustains anticipation; the eventual reveal of the alternate identity returns all borrowed prestige to the protagonist.

### Information-Dense Loop: *Lying Low in the Primordial Saint Sect*

Divide the story by “reincarnations,” with each cycle forming a complete story. The structure can continue indefinitely as long as the power system remains coherent.

### Livestream Loop

Goal (certain expectation) → opposition (short, fast outcome) → stages (escalating missions) → positive feedback for entertaining stunts

## Practical Deconstruction Techniques

### Start from Anticipation

- Use advance-knowledge advantages when introducing the world, strengthening the protagonist, preparing to show off, contrasting against an expert, or introducing a new character.
- Use transitional plot for gains, romantic ambiguity, or small events. If there is no conflict, jump to one second before the conflict begins.

### Emotional Line Graph

Horizontal axis = chapter; vertical axis = positive/negative emotion. Controlling pace means controlling how quickly “dramatic information clusters” appear. An information cluster is a plot unit that creates an emotional turn.

### Paragraph-by-Paragraph Functional Annotation

Against the source text, mark each paragraph's narrative function—setup/turn/climax/transition. Track emotional direction, predicament design, and connections between planted clues, then output a reusable framework. Do not copy plot content or expression.

## Selecting Comparable Books

| Mistake | Reason |
|------|------|
| Deconstructing only one comparable book | Use at least five books from the same site, genre, and type |
| Deconstructing only the single top breakout | A leader may depend on an irreproducible author brand or historical traffic; prioritize reproducible samples on the same platform and in the same genre with stable data |
| Comparing across sites | Reader populations and writing conventions differ by site |

Process: collect broad samples in the genre → choose a direction → choose 5–10 reference books → designate the primary comparable book

## Abstract Deconstruction: Three Acts

| Act | Core |
|----|------|
| Act I—Setup | Hint at conflict → protagonist + cheat ability → startling incident → emotional push and pull → establish main plot |
| Act II—Confrontation | New world → stronger opponent → midpoint reversal → emotional push and pull, with a rise after every fall |
| Act III—Finale | Converge clues → push conflict to its limit → decisive choice that reveals the character arc → final battle + thematic elevation |

## Film Scene Analysis and Emotional Control

- A change in a character's attitude is the clearest expression of emotion.
- Intercut reactions during fights, but control their length or they will break the pace.
- Use emotion—lament, shock, private delight—to show the situation and comparative advantage.
- Every action passage should produce an emotional change.

## Multithreaded Narrative and Structural Loops

### Dimensions for Multithreaded Analysis

Event nodes (new character/conflict erupts) / emotional-chain nodes (one-sentence summary) / career plotline / romance plotline

Single-character romance template: attitude at first meeting → decisive turn → progressive attitude change (dislike → curiosity → attraction → surrender) → consummation trigger

### Layering a Loop

When repeating one framework, change the content each time: map, characters, conflict, and emotional detail. A coherent power system can sustain the loop indefinitely.

## Genre Practice

| Genre | Core points |
|------|----------|
| Historical fiction | Essentially resembles fan fiction; title uses “dynasty + opening event”; core hook is advance knowledge that astonishes famous figures and changes history |
| Military fiction | Selling points are emotion + immersion; central conflict is nation/faith/ideology |
| Farming/high-concept fiction | Dense minor payoffs through collecting, abilities, panels, and astonishment; panel settings must be detailed |
| Livestream fiction | Goal (certain expectation) + opposition (short and fast) + stages + payoff method: tell readers how the protagonist intends to show off, then exceed that promise |
| Two-world travel | The title itself is the selling point; core hook is quietly using World B's resources in World A. Mortal-immortality fiction uses a vast timescale and generational turnover among supporting characters |
| Rebirth fiction | Built-in anticipation; betrayal in the prior life → revenge in this life; repeat payoffs in different forms. Fanqie's standard is simple, direct labeling |

## Characterization

### Establish Character in Chapter 1

Use a small event to demonstrate personality, then establish a label. Use an urgent event to enter the plot quickly. A female character's first reaction should reveal her personality.

### Reinforce Character in Daily Life

Repeat behavior consistent with characterization. Functionalize supporting characters: the jealous type moves from smugness to jealousy; the admiring type moves from doubt to respect. Show their emotional changes.

### Opening Logic

Pulled into events → learn the world through interaction → fall into a predicament → define a goal → reveal the cheat ability → gain something through it
