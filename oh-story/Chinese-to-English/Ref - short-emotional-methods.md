## The Three-Strike Method for Emotional Pain

### First Strike: Build the Bond (First 1/3)

**Goal**: Use concrete objects, numbers, and details to establish the texture of a relationship so readers believe it is real.

**Methods**:
- Use exact numbers to establish a sense of time: “eight years together,” “five years in a coma,” “seven long years”
- Let specific objects carry emotion: a gold lock (a birthday gift from an older sister), figures in a ledger (a repayment record), a little wooden horse (carved by the prince's father)
- Use repeated actions to establish habits: one bowl of congee every day, a visit every two weeks, unable to sleep from the cold each winter

**Examples**:
- *Twenty Years Too Late*: uses twenty years of ledger figures (80,000 yuan) to establish the weight of a mother-daughter relationship
- *I Pray for You Before the Buddha*: five years in a coma, details of praying to gods and Buddha, and a crush dating back to junior high school
- *The Imperial Younger Brother Bullies the Young Son*: concrete suffering across seven years as a hostage prince (kneeling in snow, being slapped, eating spoiled rice)

**Core principle**: The more concrete the bond, the more painful the later rupture. Do not use abstractions such as “they loved each other very much.”

---

### Second Strike: Emotional Rupture (Middle to Late Sections)

**Goal**: Create contrast so readers believe they hated the right person → then discover they hated the wrong person (or vice versa).

**Methods**:
- **Contrast**: first show warmth, then shatter it with a cruel truth
- **Misalignment**: Character A believes they are protecting Character B but is actually causing harm
- **Delayed truth**: reveal critical information when readers least expect it

**Examples**:
- *My Brother-in-Law Demands Formula Money for Giving Me a Ride*: the mother appears weak and pitiable but has actually manipulated the older sister into demanding money all along → the gold lock is fake (gold-plated copper) → the contrast creates the rupture
- *The Imperial Younger Brother Bullies the Young Son*: Lady Wang appears gentle (gives a hand warmer, prepares medicinal decoctions) → but actually drugs the late empress into madness → then drives her to death with heart-piercing words
- *My Reborn Younger Brother*: the younger brother deeply loves his girlfriend → she is a fraudster → the pregnancy is fake → he is tricked into a telecom-fraud compound

**Core principle**: The force of the rupture depends on the depth of the setup. Without a strong bond, the rupture has no roots.

---

### Third Strike: Lingering Dull Ache (Ending)

**Goal**: Avoid loud sobbing and hysteria; use quiet details to pierce the reader's defenses.

**Methods**:
- Let ordinary actions carry enormous emotion: continue feeding congee, fold the clothes neatly, do not look back
- Use object details to create resonance: a broken gold lock, a little wooden horse, a bloodstained ring
- Use “not” to create negative space: do not explain, do not look back, do not cry

**Examples**:
- *The Imperial Younger Brother Bullies the Young Son*: “He held the little wooden horse I had polished smooth... It fit perfectly in his hand.”
- *My Brother-in-Law Demands Formula Money for Giving Me a Ride*: “My phone became much quieter. The troubles that had plagued me for so long suddenly became very, very small.”
- *Scoring My Prospective Daughter-in-Law*: “After the adoption paperwork was completed, she stepped into this unfamiliar home for the first time... I knew I had chosen correctly this time.”

**Core principle**: The best lingering impact does not rely on weeping. It relies on being unforgettable long after the reader finishes.

## Emotional Push-Pull Rhythm Design

### Basic Rhythm Curve

```
温暖 → 残忍 → 善意 → 真相 → 原谅 → 来不及 → 释然 → 细节暴击
```

Not every story uses the full curve. Choose a subset by genre:

| Genre | Typical rhythm | Core push-pull |
|------|----------|----------|
| Social realism / cathartic fiction | Oppression → endurance → eruption → comeuppance | Fast emotional rebound, high density of comeuppance |
| Emotional tragedy | Sweetness → rupture → attempt to repair → missed chance | Slow setup, powerful aftereffect |
| Historical romance / revenge | Forbearance → planning → exposure → retribution | Simple setup, violent aesthetics |
| Suspense / mystery | Calm → unease → truth → shock | Information gaps create unease |
| Period fiction / family | Misunderstanding → conflict → truth → reconciliation | Intergenerational conflict, period texture |

### Push-Pull Principles

1. **Emotion cannot remain relentlessly painful or relentlessly cathartic** (if several consecutive sections contain no change in emotional stance, expectation, or pressure, check for stagnation)
2. **Every turn requires a triggering event** (emotion cannot change without cause)
3. **The final turn determines the tone of the ending's aftereffect**
4. **Cathartic fiction permits a fast rebound; tragic fiction needs a longer setup**

## Emotional Strategy Differences by Genre

### Social Realism / Cathartic Fiction

- Emotional rebound speed: fast (after humiliation, first gather evidence or lay plans, then deliver comeuppance before readers have forgotten)
- Comeuppance rhythm: keep paying it off as the antagonist's arrogance escalates; do not insert it at fixed intervals
- Antagonist arrogance: escalating (minor arrogance the first time, major arrogance the last)
- Ending tone: satisfying, vindicating
- Example: *Scoring My Prospective Daughter-in-Law*—countering the scoring system, the zero in the metal box, severing the relationship

### Emotional Tragedy

- Density of bonding details: high (the first 1/3 must establish a deep bond)
- Contrast design: warm before cold, sweet before bitter
- Aftereffect technique: quiet ending + object detail
- Ending tone: unresolved regret, acceptance
- Example: *My Brother-in-Law Demands Formula Money for Giving Me a Ride*—truth of the gold lock → false love → independence

### Historical Romance / Revenge

- Simple-setting principle: keep character relationships clear; avoid a complex world system
- Violent-aesthetic approach: deliver comeuppance directly, without hesitation
- Trump-card timing: reveal the greatest trump card in the final 1/4
- Ending tone: deeply satisfying, karmic retribution
- Example: *The Imperial Younger Brother Bullies the Young Son*—an order to administer twenty strokes → uncover the truth → depose the empress

### Suspense / Mystery

- Information-gap design: readers know what the character does not, or vice versa
- Process-of-elimination structure: eliminate possibilities one by one until only the truth remains
- Motive-reveal rhythm: first reveal what was done, then why
- Ending tone: disturbing in retrospect, suddenly clear
- Example: *My Reborn Younger Brother*—rebirth premise + fraudster identity + telecom-fraud compound

### Period Fiction / Family

- Handling intergenerational conflict: do not take sides; show both sides' suffering
- Period-detail texture: establish texture through era-specific objects and customs
- Reconciliation rhythm: do not reconcile too quickly; first allow both sides to be fully hurt
- Ending tone: warmth tinged with regret
- Example: *My Brother-in-Law Demands Formula Money for Giving Me a Ride*—parent-child relationship → truth of the gold lock → rupture

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Identification method | Direction for correction |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Too flat** | 5+ consecutive sections without an emotional turn | Insert an unexpected event or new information |
| **Too rushed** | A major turn receives only one section of setup | Give the turn at least three sections of setup |
| **False suffering** | Readers do not ache for the character; they only feel uncomfortable watching | Check whether the bond was established concretely |
| **Disconnected** | The first and second halves feel like two different stories | Carry foreshadowing, objects, or themes throughout |
| **Botched ending** | The story keeps explaining for 2,000 Chinese characters after the reversal | Finish within 500 Chinese characters after the reversal |
| **Broken characterization** | A character's behavior at a critical moment contradicts their earlier characterization | Review the characterization and ensure behavioral logic remains consistent |

## Quick Self-Check

Use this checklist when designing emotion:

- [ ] Does the first 1/3 contain concrete bonding details rather than abstractions?
- [ ] Does the middle contain contrast/rupture (readers believe A → reality is B)?
- [ ] Does the ending contain a quiet detail rather than a long lyrical passage?
- [ ] Do emotional stance, expectation, and pressure keep changing, without several consecutive sections spinning in place?
- [ ] Does the antagonist's behavior follow their characterization logic?
- [ ] Are all six failure modes above absent?
