## Dual Tracks and Information Gaps

### Basic Dual-Track Approach

- Main track: Create emotion while the protagonist acts
- Secondary track: Create emotion while supporting characters investigate the protagonist’s identity or attitude
- Clearly establish the protagonist’s hidden identity, connections, and background; design the buffer zone
- Supporting characters repeatedly discovering the truth and becoming astonished creates a strong emotional track

### Using Information Gaps

- Information gaps are essential to parallel connections
- Protagonist knows + reader knows + supporting character does not → astonishment when the supporting character discovers it
- Information gaps can create layered payoffs
- When switching POV, use an information gap as the hook that renews reader interest

### Emotion-Driven Progression in *The Writer’s Journey*

- First let the protagonist follow the logical path—refusal of the call
- Use repeated emotional push-pull and the destruction of safety until no choice remains
- The protagonist ultimately chooses A, but everything remains logical and natural

## Stepwise Progression and Buffer Zones

### Buffer-Zone Concept

Interests, events, or emotions create distance between protagonist and supporting character. Closing that distance creates satisfaction. Designing the buffer zone requires clearly establishing the protagonist’s backstory.

### Divide Goals into Steps

- The protagonist always has a fundamental goal; divide it into several stages
- Apply the same method to current small goals, not only large ones
- Once the purpose is clear, action stops dragging

### Three Modes of Plot Advancement

1. The protagonist actively handles trouble that advances the goal
2. The protagonist actively pursues the goal and encounters trouble
3. Trouble comes to the protagonist, but solving it advances the goal

### Plan–Obstacle Structure

Plan 1 -> accident 1 -> plan 2 -> accident 2 -> climax

Every plan may be interrupted, creating fresh anticipation.

## Large-Structure Design

### The Large Structure Is a Deceptive Main Plot

Use the large structure as a gimmick that keeps readers suspended: Every time completion approaches, it remains just out of reach, so suspense never ends. Example—identity-exposure arc: The protagonist is repeatedly doubted and repeatedly disproves the doubt, yet full exposure always remains one step away.

### Four-Part Large Framework

| Stage | Content |
|------|------|
| Setup | Establish the world, introduce the protagonist, and provide the special advantage |
| Development | The protagonist uses the advantage to advance through many smaller plots |
| Turn | Circumstances change and introduce a greater enemy or crisis |
| Resolution | A major climax resolves the central conflict |

Concentrate most of the text in “development.”

### The More Small Plots Fill It, the Better

- More smaller plots before a large anticipation point stabilize returning readership
- Leave no gaps between smaller plots
- Parallel plotting is the best way to fill gaps

## Opening Design Essentials

- Title, synopsis, and opening must form one whole
- After the special advantage appears, the plot must connect strongly to it
- When identification is vague, create a social power structure readers understand
- Too many POV changes make the narrative choppy
- The opening scene needs action, information, and imagery
- Enter through a concrete action, introduce the special advantage, then enter the plot

## Storyboarding and Imagery

### Basic Storyboard Writing

- Camera 1: Protagonist’s POV—what the protagonist sees
- Camera 2: Target’s POV—how the protagonist reacts
- Do not use too many cameras at once
- Film mentally according to the information cluster’s key points

### Essence of Storyboarding

Storyboarding does not mechanically lengthen an information cluster. It makes the full block concrete and visual. Secure drama first, then consider storyboarding.

### Strengthening Imagery

- Plan only the chapter’s main information, not a required word count
- Scene-analysis rule: A gap exists between image and prose, so translate the visual into words

## RPG Structure and Reward Design

### Progression Fiction Is Fundamentally an RPG

The core is designing feedback points: Where does the reader receive satisfaction? Plot is relatively less important in progression fiction; the reading experience matters most.

### Weaving Reward Points

- Give readers a “reward” at every stage—not only the protagonist’s advancement, but also supporting-character reactions, environmental changes, and new information
- Rewards can be neither too dense nor too sparse
- Vary the reward form by mixing gains, recognition, revelations, and other types

### Ascension and Map-Change Loop

- A map change can have many causes: hide an identity while being hunted, find a sect as a disguise, or begin as an outer disciple concealing strength
- Every reset must bring new information and room to develop characterization
- Avoid simple repetition; rise in a spiral

## Story Configurations and Formula Use

### Three Layers of Abstraction from Archetypal Story to Formula

| Level | Explanation |
|------|------|
| Classic plot (concrete) | Cause, process, and result are all specific |
| Dramatic unit (dramatic abstraction) | Retains logical structure and can be re-skinned |
| Emotional module (emotional abstraction) | Direct expression of what readers want |

Example: Classic plot = “After Xiao Yan’s engagement is broken, he rises over three years and humiliates the Nalan clan”; dramatic unit = “A faction humiliates the protagonist → the protagonist lies low and grows stronger → the protagonist publicly counterattacks” (a broken engagement can become expulsion from a sect or defeat in a succession struggle); emotional module = “frustration → comeback.” Re-skinning preserves “frustration → comeback” but changes the broken engagement to being expelled from a school club.

### Traits of Dramatic Units

- Greater abstraction creates greater variability
- Several dramatic units can combine into a “story configuration”
- Drama wears out: Overusing similar structures makes them ineffective

### Traits of Emotional Modules

- Drama wears out; emotion does not
- When a dramatic unit wears out, strengthen or replace the emotional module to recombine it

### Correct vs. Incorrect Use of Formulas

| Approach | Result |
|------|------|
| Directly apply a classic plot | Similar story and broken characterization |
| Reconstruct by replacing the story configuration | Correct |
| Fine-tune by deepening characterization and adapting the plot | Correct; also makes motivation and story logic more plausible |

Long-form fiction at million-character scale must master reconstruction or inevitably become homogeneous.

## Relationship Between Structure and Character

### Character-Driven Structure

- Choose a plot structure for the character you created, rather than a character for the plot structure
- Synthesizing a new character first and then searching for a fitting plot structure easily causes collapse

### Structuring Romance and Career Arcs

- Career-arc key points: obstacle, crisis, display, discovery, acquisition, righteous act
- The romance arc needs explicit drama and structure
- It needs layers and stages, not a pile of attractive sequences
- Romance differs by genre: In combat fiction, write facing death together instead of forcing in urban dates
- Distinguish romance arcs for female leads with different characterizations to avoid a uniform feel

### Binding Career and Romance Arcs

- Fully binding help for the female lead to the career goal is efficient
- More generally: The male lead intentionally works on his career and helps the female lead along the way

### Strategic Retreat

- Strategic retreat also creates push-pull; if the emotional rises and falls work, sacrifice does not inherently become a reader turnoff
- Use it only when necessary and only after a large-scale display or act of help

## Framework and Faction-Hand Method

**Essence of a framework**: The proportions and arrangement of the main plot + subplots + daily life.

**Faction-hand method**: Like *Legends of the Three Kingdoms*, different factions play cards in turns; each card creates reactions and consequences that form the main plot when ordered.

**Three factions**: Protagonist faction / enemy faction / audience faction

**Three levels of linear plot**: See the basic, intermediate, and advanced versions of “Faction-Hand Method” in plot-special-topics.md for the complete card-playing structures; they are not repeated here.

**Avoid reader turnoffs**: When the protagonist suffers a setback, recover the emotion from another angle through deeper logic or an unexpected gain.

**Advanced framework**: Linear plots have a limited ceiling -> at the advanced level, use spiraling merged tracks. Alternate at least two main tracks, with each round rising above the last—for example, alternate investigation and progression so each solved case increases the protagonist’s power while the cases grow larger. This prevents a single track from reaching its ceiling.

## Popular-Book Framework Deconstruction

### Single Plot-Segment Template (10–40 Chapters Each)

1. **Cause**: Protagonist faces a new conflict or enters a new environment
2. **Development**: Use special advantage / meet new character / acquire new resource
3. **Setup**: Plant a thread for the next plot segment
4. **Climax**: Key scene for showing off and humiliating / competition / seizing treasure
5. **Turn**: Identity exposure / relationship change / truth revealed
6. **Ending / gain**: Inventory the spoils

### Arrangement Across Multiple Segments

- Vary combinations of three core elements—treasure + beauty + competition—and rotate them to avoid repetition
- Explicitly list gains after every segment
- Alternate setup and reward tracks

### Multi-Track Interweaving Rhythm

| Track | Frequency |
|------|------|
| Combat-power progression | At least one leap in every segment |
| Equipment gains | At least one key item or skill per segment |
| Romance | Deepen the relationship with at least one female character per segment |
| Reputation | At least one “astonished observer” scene per segment |

Advance the four tracks asynchronously and stagger their rhythms to avoid homogeneity.

### Volume Structure

- One volume contains about 100 chapters and ends by resolving the current conflict + opening a new map
- The final act of one volume is simultaneously the first act of the next, creating a seamless transition

## Nine Main-Plot Formulas

### Three Protagonist Essentials

| Element | Explanation |
|------|------|
| Goal | The fundamental force sustaining the protagonist’s progress; someone without a specific goal is not a strong protagonist |
| Personality | One dominant trait works best; developing plot from personality is a superior technique |
| Cultivation system | Copying poses no pressure; simply change the presentation |

### Superior Conflict Approaches

| Type | Explanation |
|------|------|
| Revenge | Avenge relatives or a sect; the key is passionate intensity |
| Ideological conflict | Two sides hold opposing ideas; the most natural |
| Sect grievance | Two powerful factions feud, trapping the protagonist in a fight |
| Fight for survival | The protagonist must contribute to the faction’s survival |
| Grievance among superiors | The master or superior has a grievance with the other side, implicating the protagonist |
| Guilt of possessing treasure | Acquire treasure -> news leaks -> pursuit begins |

### Inferior Conflicts (Avoid)

- A rich heir deliberately humiliates the protagonist -> repeat endlessly -> readers become frustrated
- Provocation without cause -> remains shallow

### Formula and Anti-Formula

- Mastering formulas is a prerequisite for innovation
- Formula = skeleton. Add flesh by changing setting, characters, and emotion; the template remains while content changes

## Formula-Template Repetition

### Universal Progression and Showing-Off Template

Fixed elements: male lead + male villain with high status + woman as reward + limited slot + time limit

1. Reinforce: Show the male villain’s status while everyone admires him
2. Conceal: The protagonist chooses the apparently worst option and is underestimated
3. Reveal: Erupt at the last moment and exceed every expectation

Key: Change the setting while retaining the formula. Changes to the special advantage, characters, and plot naturally create different stories.

### Three Levels of Formula Repetition

| Level | Explanation |
|------|------|
| Appeal repetition | Select the best successful template already in the market |
| Character repetition | Give the character one fixed behavioral trait and repeat it in different settings |
| Plot repetition | A formula loop naturally created by combining the core appeal with the character appeal |

### Avoiding Aesthetic Fatigue

- In strong formulas and writing, readers cannot see the formula even though it is present
- Highest level: Readers cannot detect the formula, but it has already drawn them deep inside

### Raising a Flag

Have a supporting character say a flag line: “There won’t be a monster in there, right?” or “Once I finish this mission, I can go home and marry.” If it works, repeat the technique.

## Fantasy Framework Deconstruction

### Overall Structural Pattern

Cycle three major elements: treasure + beauty + competition -> beauty + instance + competition -> instance + beauty + competition, varying among the three.

### Key Extraction Points

- Brisk pace and dense payoffs; every event has a clear climax and gain
- Memorable characterization for female characters
- Clearly layered stages for showing off
- Tightly arranged multiple tracks: combat growth + equipment gains + romance + reputation
- Every event ends with gains + setup for the next

## Alternating Dual-Track Framework

### Alternating Structure

Interweave the reality and instance tracks: The reality track develops the protagonist’s exceptional nature + reawakening spiritual energy; the instance track increases power + rewards.

### Three Suppressions, One Eruption, Three Reactions

- Suppression 1: Friendly faction -> believes the male lead is an expert
- Suppression 2: Enemy faction -> two layers of setup + disbelief forces the male lead to act
- Suppression 3: Neutral faction
- Eruption 1: Male lead acts and overwhelms
- Reactions 3: Show each faction’s astonishment

### Three Functions of a Ranking

1. Anticipation of the male lead rising in rank -> creates progression motivation
2. Introduce new opponents through the ranking -> anticipation of collision
3. Once the ranking exists -> later displays create an aftermath

### Trump-Card Management

Always keep at least three unused trump cards. Reveal only one each time while acquiring a new skill.

### Expanding Foreshadowing

Every unusual trait or talent can become foreshadowing that opens a new track.

### Guan Yu Slays Hua Xiong Before the Wine Cools

B defeats A -> C defeats B -> protagonist defeats C -> major boss appears. Progression-fiction substitutions: Replace “dialogue” with “combat trait” and “presiding over a meeting” with “taking the stage.”

## Structured Book Deconstruction

### Sequence Breakdown for Long-Form Fiction

A 3-million-Chinese-character book contains about 40–50 units; each unit runs 60,000–80,000 Chinese characters and contains 4–5 sequences; each sequence covers about four chapters. The full book contains roughly 200–300 sequences.

### Four-Chapter Structure for One Sequence

| Chapter | Content |
|----|------|
| Chapter 1 | First half builds identification through an everyday scene; second half creates an information gap by showing a dilemma the special advantage can solve |
| Chapter 2 | Strengthen anticipation through character interaction; end with the protagonist beginning the display |
| Chapter 3 | Fulfill anticipation and fully deliver the payoff |
| Chapter 4 | First half resolves the aftermath and demonstrates progress; second half opens the next goal |

### Core Principles

- Use the N+1 principle for identification: Familiar content helps readers accept new content
- Information gaps are the most common anticipation technique
- Lengthen / shorten / upgrade / reverse / add multiple rounds of push-pull—vary flexibly while preserving the basic structure
- Deconstruct books from a holistic viewpoint, raising your perspective to overlook the entire mountain range

## Essence of Story and Six-Act Structure

### Definition of Story

- Fact = static presentation
- Event = changing fact
- Story = the product of selecting, ordering, and causally connecting events

### Causal Chain

Causality creates meaning, and meaning is the soul of story. The same event sequence with different causal chains -> completely different themes.

- Essence of setup: Before Event B, plant its cause beside Event A
- Essence of foreshadowing: Plant the cause of a distant event early
- Essence of a hook: Plant the cause of the next segment at the current event’s end
- Close every other path for the protagonist -> they naturally enter the plot you designed

### Four Ways to Organize Events

| Method | Explanation |
|------|------|
| Selection | Remove events unrelated to the main plot |
| Chronological arrangement | Chronological / reverse chronological / flashback |
| Causal logic | Establish causality among events; used by 99% of web fiction |
| Thematic / associative logic | Organize by thematic association |

### Why Opening Techniques Work

List every event in the opening -> find the one with the most dramatic tension -> place it first.

### Essence of Five Acts

Five acts = a template that bends the causal chain, giving a straight chain rhythm, which creates emotion. See “Five-Act Structure” in outline-structure-theory.md for each act’s function + causal-chain state; it is not repeated here.

### Unified Theory of Three, Four, and Five Acts

The three-act, four-part, and five-act forms are fundamentally the same structure; freely add or remove parts according to conflict intensity.

### Core of a Turn

Purpose of a turn = increase conflict -> increase emotion. Focus on a qualitative change in the conflict that worsens the protagonist’s situation; it must be “unexpected yet inevitable in retrospect.”

### Act Six: Aftermath (A Web-Fiction Feature)

- Visible track (power) vs. hidden track (social perception): Every imbalance creates an opportunity to show off
- Showing off = the textual demonstration that the protagonist’s social status has risen

### Sources of Satisfaction

| Type | Question Answered |
|------|-----------|
| Progression | Can I grow stronger? |
| Overwhelming | Am I strong enough? |
| Showing off | Has my strength been recognized? |
| Revenge | Will injustice be corrected? |
| Escape from desperation | Can I get away? |
| Farming | How can the weak grow stronger? |
| Cautious survival | How can the weak survive? |

### Definition of Plot

Plot = the structure formed by making events causal and placing the causal chain inside a five-act form. Main plot = “main” (theme) + “line” (causal-chain structure). Subplot = “sub” (subtheme) + “line” (solution to the subtheme).

## Core Hooks and Refinement

### Definition of a Core Hook

A core hook is a fixed pattern of plot development that repeats in varied forms. Nearly every chart-topping book has one. It determines the full book’s experiential tone.

### Classic Core-Hook Examples

| Work | Loop Pattern |
|------|----------|
| *Lord of Mysteries* | Gray-fog space -> overinterpretation and imagined profundity -> exchange intelligence -> trade materials -> praise the Fool |
| *Release That Witch* | Witch is despised -> rescue -> modern knowledge astonishes -> magical training -> church pursues |
| Infinite-flow fiction | Lord God -> reincarnation -> transmigration -> complete mission -> receive reward -> exchange |
| Faction method (traditional) | Villain suppresses -> ally warns and reinforces -> neutral faction watches -> humiliation -> reaction |

### Refinement Method

Path from a one-sentence main plot to finished prose:

```
一句话主线 -> 粗略卷纲 -> 详细卷纲 -> 章纲 -> 正文
```

Each layer is a more concrete expansion of the one above. This naturally creates a multilevel structure without a separately designed framework.

Structural-choice rule: Structure is a tool that serves the story, not a mandatory mold. The refinement method itself creates structure.

## Conflict Structure and the No-Exit Principle

### Core Formula

Conflict + plot + suspense = an emotionally satisfying experience. Suspense is most important.

A sense of gain can replace a sense of danger: Change “do this or die” to “do this and gain something.”

### Three Types of Crisis (“Death”)

| Type | Example |
|------|------|
| Physical death | Ye Fan is captured to be refined into medicine |
| Identity / career death | Xiao Yan falls from genius and has his engagement broken |
| Psychological death | The opening of *Dao of the Bizarre Immortal*: “Are the hallucinations real?” |

Choose one primary type to run throughout; secondary types advance plot without overshadowing it.

### Opening = Begin with Trouble

Something unusual must happen to the protagonist at the opening. A protagonist worth following = who they are -> what trouble they encounter -> how they respond -> reader curiosity about that response.

### No-Exit Principle

Moving the protagonist from comfort to intensity requires a compelling reason—**readers must believe the protagonist has no choice but to enter**.

Anticipation = entry without exit = setup. Every anticipation point says, “Keep watching; this is what comes next.”

### Three Levels of No-Exit Setup

| Level | Technique |
|------|------|
| Basic | Establish mortal hostility between enemy and protagonist -> create oppression |
| Intermediate | Make the protagonist stronger than ever -> the enemy appears at exactly that moment -> the protagonist tests the new power |
| Better | Establish the enemy’s background and the earthquake that resolving them will cause -> layer several expectations |

Trend: Positive drivers such as gains and progression are more popular than traditional reinforcement of old grievances.

### Web Fiction = Multiple Troubles + Multiple Gates

Traditional literature uses one trouble and two gates. Web fiction repeats gates continuously and makes each doorway feel like entry without exit. Connect the gates through satisfaction from gains and progression.

## Mortal Stakes and Adhesive Design

### Four Adhesives

| Adhesive | Explanation |
|--------|------|
| Reason to kill | The villain has a powerful reason to kill the protagonist |
| Professional duty | A detective who takes a case must solve it; a sect disciple must complete an assignment |
| Moral responsibility | Relatives or friends are endangered, so the protagonist cannot stand aside |
| Physical location | A special location traps both sides, such as an instance or locked room |

If readers believe a character can leave the dilemma at any moment, all tension vanishes.

### Common Conflict Types

Revenge, disaster, resistance, romantic obstacles, ambition, identity conflict, environmental conflict, mortal danger, class conflict, and ideological conflict.

## Five Contradiction-Driven Plot Types

| Type | Core Logic |
|------|----------|
| Identity conflict | Use the external pressure inherent to an identity, naturally clarifying the goal |
| Environmental conflict | External pressure directly creates conflict; appropriate for large-scale novels |
| Mortal-crisis conflict | “Defeat the junior and the senior arrives,” preserving constant pressure in a classic advancement structure |
| Class conflict | Human desire escalates layer by layer, creating different demands at every stage |
| Ideological conflict | Idealism vs. reality; an advanced technique with an extremely high ceiling |

### Advanced: Sequence-Uniqueness Premise

- Acting method: Advancement carries risk and requires doing xxx -> gives the protagonist a reason to pursue subplots
- Sequence uniqueness: Your advancement harms entrenched interests -> every extraordinary person in the same sequence becomes an enemy -> a natural conflict line

## Deepening Suspense and Conflict

### Anticipation > Payoff

> Related: The authoritative payoff design system—six types and reverse-engineering method—is in “Payoff Design System” in [plot-emotion-system.md](plot-emotion-system.md). This section only compares why anticipation lasts longer than payoff.

- Satisfaction is the release of emotion and disappears when the release ends
- Reader tension, curiosity, concern, anger, and frustration last longer than “satisfaction”
- The most compelling part of romance is ambiguous push-pull; after the relationship is confirmed, it can become dull
- Lesson: Resolving a plot too perfectly harms long-term anticipation

### Suspense vs. Surprise

- Surprise = a bomb under the table suddenly explodes
- Suspense = the audience hears the timer ticking but does not know when the bomb will explode; suspense is much stronger
- End-of-chapter suspense = unresolved danger at the chapter’s end. Use strong suspense for high-pressure and advancement chapters; for low-pressure and transition chapters, a weak hook or stage goal is enough, according to chapter function—see “Chapter Function and Rhythmic Variation” in outline-structure-theory.md

### Time Lock

Requiring something to be completed within a fixed time significantly increases tension and pace. When unsure how to write an opening, try a time lock.

## Plot-Driven Fiction Standards and Advantages

### What Is Plot-Driven Fiction?

If a book remains exciting and “powerful” late in its run, with climaxes readers discuss enthusiastically, it is plot-driven. High-concept fiction often opens strongly and declines; plot-driven fiction becomes more exciting later.

### Qianjun Writing Guide: Five Safeguards Against Collapse

1. Goal never disappears: The protagonist always has a clear goal
2. Selling points never decrease: The core selling point cannot vanish midway
3. Protagonist’s social relationships never go blank: An interactive relationship network is mandatory
4. Higher status never disappears: The protagonist pursues and encounters people above them
5. Gains in status never stop: Maintain an ongoing sense of growth

### Qianjun Writing Guide: Three-Track Structure

- World track: Large-scale narrative at the worldbuilding level
- Plot track: Advancement through concrete events and conflicts
- Progression track: Continuous growth in the protagonist’s power or status
- Interweave all three; none can be omitted

### Qianjun Writing Guide: Five-Step Showing-Off Method

1. The protagonist is powerful, but others do not know it
2. Reinforce the difficulty of the dilemma
3. Supporting characters doubt the protagonist
4. The protagonist easily resolves it with the special advantage
5. Everyone is astonished

## Supplemental Structural and Plot Techniques

### Structural Levels of Emotional Activation

Character-emotion guidance principle: If every character guides emotion, none does. Select the focal characters.

Strong reversal structure: First guide readers into sustained dislike for a character, then reveal that character’s noble qualities during a pivotal event.

### Four Self-Checks

1. Is there a memorable payoff or characterization hook?
2. Does the story fully deliver what its genre promises—progression for progression fiction and push-pull for regretful-partner fiction?
3. Is the conflict direct and emotional enough?
4. Do the sentences flow?

One weak item is acceptable; failure across all four causes the book to flop. Direct conflict creates broad emotional resonance more easily than intricate construction.

## Quality-Control Checklist

After building a framework or writing a plot segment, use this checklist. Check every item and revise failures.

### Structure

- [ ] The protagonist always has a clear goal, both the current small goal and long-range major goal
- [ ] The current segment forms a complete cause → development → climax → gain loop
- [ ] The segment ending does two things: inventories gains + sets up the next segment
- [ ] The large structure uses a “deceptive main plot” to suspend readers, always approaching completion but falling just short
- [ ] Main plot and subplots advance on staggered rhythms rather than erupting or idling together

### Conflict and Suspense

- [ ] Conflict has a clear adhesive, so readers believe the protagonist cannot leave at any moment
- [ ] Every chapter ending gives readers a reason to continue. Use an end hook; a weak hook or stage goal is enough in low-pressure or transition chapters
- [ ] Anticipation > payoff: Setup length is at least as long as release
- [ ] When the protagonist suffers a setback, emotion recovers from another angle through deeper logic or an unexpected gain

### Satisfaction and Pace

- [ ] Progression fiction: Every stage contains a clear reward point—at least one advancement, item, recognition, or revelation
- [ ] Showing-off fiction: The rhythm uses “three suppressions, one eruption, three reactions” or the “five-step showing-off method”
- [ ] Romance arc: Contains layered push-pull rather than stacked sequences
- [ ] When repeating a formula, setting, characters, or emotion changes so readers cannot see the template
- [ ] No two consecutive segments use the same combination of core elements; rotate treasure + beauty + competition

### Characters

- [ ] Character behavior is driven by personality, not reverse-engineered from plot needs
- [ ] Supporting characters have their own positions and motivations instead of functioning only as tools
- [ ] Romance and career arcs bind or intersect rather than running independently
- [ ] Trump-card management: Keep 2–3 unrevealed cards available

### Global Five-Safeguard Check

- [ ] Goal does not disappear: The protagonist always has a clear goal
- [ ] Selling points do not decrease: The core selling point never vanishes midway
- [ ] Social relationships do not go blank: The protagonist has an interactive relationship network
- [ ] Higher status does not disappear: The protagonist pursues and meets people above them
- [ ] Growth does not stop: Status and power continue increasing
