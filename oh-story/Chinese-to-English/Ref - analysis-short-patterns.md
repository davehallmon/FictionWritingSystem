# Evaluation Standard for Structural Patterns in Short-Form Source Text

> Use this reference to describe how an existing short story organizes pressure, information, relationships, and payoffs. Patterns are comparison tools, not writing formulas; do not require fixed chapter positions, percentages, or clue counts.

## Identification Principles

First segment the source by actual function, then determine which pattern it most closely resembles. A valid pattern requires three kinds of evidence: a precondition, a turn that changes reader judgment or emotion, and consequences for action or relationships after the turn.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Observable functional chain | Evidence of validity | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build pressure—counterattack—settle accounts | Injustice becomes concrete → protagonist obtains conditions for action → consequences reach the responsible party | Pressure, active choice, and settlement belong to one causal chain | Outside help performs the counterattack while the protagonist watches |
| Anomaly—investigation—revised judgment | A small anomaly appears → multiple interpretations compete → new evidence changes judgment of responsibility | Every piece of evidence changes the next action | Clues add only atmosphere; testimony supplies the entire reveal |
| Provisional answer—reexamination—final resolution | A credible answer temporarily holds → it causes a new action → reexamination overturns the interpretation while preserving the facts | The later layer explains why the earlier layer was credible | Testimony reverses repeatedly without intermediate consequences for action |
| Ally—dependence—reevaluation | A relationship provides resources or safety → fractures appear within the help → protagonist reevaluates the relationship | Earlier behavior gains new meaning after the reveal | The ally abruptly changes sides at the ending |
| Prior-life information—different choice—new cost | Old information changes a present choice → the advantage creates an unfamiliar side effect → the new life receives resolution | Crucial causality in the current life genuinely differs | The story only retells the former life, while the current life follows the same path |
| Wrongdoing—corresponding countermeasure—visible consequence | Each injury establishes responsibility → the countermeasure uses relevant evidence or resources → consequences echo the wrongdoing | Retribution is not coincidence and follows from a character's choice | The story lists suffering and punishment without a causal bridge |
| Rule—verification—price | An abnormal rule is observed → the character tests or misjudges it → limitations and costs change the solution | Rule boundaries remain consistent | A newly invented ability solves the immediate problem |
| Ability experience—evidentiary progress—relinquished ability | A special ability solves a local problem → its insufficiency forces real-world cost → the final choice defines victory | The ability serves the central plot and cannot replace the final choice | Rewards escalate continually until the central short-form plot becomes an ability manual |
| Relationship approaches—meets resistance—is defined | Interaction establishes special importance → external or internal resistance tests the relationship → a choice defines it | Progress follows from shared experience and choices | Physical contact or misunderstanding mechanically escalates the relationship |
| Death/absence—rereading—aftermath | Absence creates an information gap → survivors reinterpret earlier behavior → an irreversible fact changes relational judgment | Earlier details support rereading, and the ending bears the loss | One final letter explains the entire truth |
| Public setting—changed ownership of evidence—judgment | A private conflict enters public space → the holder of evidence or interpretive authority changes → characters bear social or relational consequences | Public exposure changes power rather than merely providing spectators | Crowd reaction substitutes for proving the facts |
| Single objective—escalating cost—restrained resolution | The objective remains constant → each advance requires a harder choice → the ending settles only the central debt | Costs directly relate to the objective | A larger new story steals the climax late in the work |

## Structural Observation Table

```markdown
实际功能段：{按源文列出，不预设数量}
最接近模式：{名称}
前置条件证据：{位置}
关键转折证据：{位置}
行动/关系后果：{位置}
源文的偏离：{增加、删去或重排了什么功能}
偏离效果：{改善/削弱了哪种期待，附证据}
```

**English guide (non-executable):** Actual functional segments from the source without assuming a fixed count; closest pattern; evidence for prerequisites and key turns; action or relationship consequences; functions the source added, removed, or reordered; and evidence showing which expectation the departure strengthened or weakened.

## Comparing Common Patterns

- Describe structure through actual paragraphs and sections. You may report each source segment's actual percentage of the whole, but do not judge validity against recommended percentages.
- “Earlier” and “later” must be judged relative to whether the source provides adequate setup and leaves room for consequences after a reveal, not against a fixed percentage.
- Record clue count only as an observation. Fairness depends on whether clues can be traced back and whether they entered a character's judgment.
- One source may combine several patterns, but identify which climax and final settlement belong to the primary pattern.
- Deliver a reusable structure together with its failure conditions. Do not copy specific identities, objects, or dialogue from the source as a template.

## Prohibited Misuse

- Do not apply long-form models for volumes, maps, progression phases, or extended openings.
- Do not present “every chapter must,” fixed section positions, or fixed clue counts as quality conclusions about the source.
- Do not output a revised-outline plan unless the user explicitly requests editorial recommendations. By default, describe only observations, effects, and failure patterns.
