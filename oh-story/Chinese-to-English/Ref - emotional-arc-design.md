# Emotional Arc Operations Manual

> A quick-reference manual for choosing arcs, controlling pace, and shaping emotion while writing stories.

## Decision Guide

| Desired emotional effect | Arc | Key point |
|------------------|-----------|----------|
| Suffering followed by a comeback | V-shaped | Lowest point at 60–70%; healing rises rapidly |
| Sweetness followed by a devastating bad ending | Inverted V | Peak at 40–50%; fall faster than the rise |
| Unpredictable multiple reversals | W-shaped | Each peak higher than the last; maximum three fluctuations |
| Escalating rounds of humiliation/payoff | Progressive | No decline; climbs like stairs |
| Major eruption after endurance | Delayed gratification | Setup across the first 70–80%; concentrated payoff at the end |
| Ending overturns everything | Sharp turn | Sudden turn at 70–80%; earlier clues must make sense in retrospect |

Two arcs may be combined, such as delayed gratification + sharp turn, but never more than two.

## Six Emotional Arcs

### 1. V-Shaped Arc: Suffering → Healing

```
情绪
10 |          *
 9 |         / \
 7 |        /   \
 6 |       /     *  治愈高点
 1 |  *  虐谷底
   +------------------> 进度
     0%  40%  70%  100%
```

Key operations:

- Place the lowest point at 60–70%, then recover rapidly. The ending emotion must be higher than the opening.
- Make the low sufficiently deep and the turn sudden. Do not prolong recovery; turn after one or two scenes at the bottom.

| Genre | Suffering | Healing |
|------|----------|------------|
| Rebirth revenge | Abuse in the former life | Comeback after rebirth |
| Reunited lovers | Pain of separation | Reunion |
| Family tearjerker | Misunderstanding/regret | Reconciliation/truth revealed |
| Growth and healing | Life at the bottom | Standing again |

Word allocation for 12,000 Chinese characters: opening 10% → descent 30% → bottom 15% → turn 10% → healing rise 25% → high-point resolution 10%.

### 2. Inverted-V Arc: Satisfaction → Reversal → Low

```
情绪
10 |     *
 5 | *     \
 1 |        *  BE 落点
   +------------------> 进度
     0%  30%  60%  100%
```

Key operations:

- Place the peak at 40–50%, then fall rapidly after the reversal.
- The fall must be faster than the rise. Do not add sweetness to a bad ending.

| Genre | Satisfying portion | Fall |
|------|----------|------------|
| Tragic romance | Sweetness/happiness | Truth/separation |
| Workplace downfall | Promotion/success | Discovery that it was a trap |
| Friendship betrayal | Complete closeness | Hidden calculation |
| Collapse of trust | Unquestioning belief | Truth of deception |

Word allocation for 12,000 Chinese characters: start 10% → rise 35% → peak 10% → reversal 5% → fall 30% → low resolution 10%.

### 3. W-Shaped Arc: Multiple Fluctuations

```
情绪
10 |       *           *
 5 |  *         \ /
 4 |             *
   +--------------------> 进度
     0%  20%  40%  60%  80%  100%
```

Key operations:

- Use at least two peaks and one valley, usually three fluctuations.
- Every peak must exceed the previous one; place the final peak at 80–90%.
- Leave breathing room between fluctuations and use no more than three.

| Genre | Description |
|------|------|
| Multi-reversal mystery | Each apparent solution leads to another reversal |
| Groveling pursuit after divorce | Repeated push-pull; every apparent reconciliation brings a new problem |
| Business warfare | Each side reverses the advantage just when victory appears certain |

Word allocation for 15,000 Chinese characters and three fluctuations: opening + first peak 25% → first valley 10% → second peak 25% → second valley 10% → third peak 20% → resolution 10%.

### 4. Progressive Arc: Step-by-Step Escalation

```
情绪
10 |                     *
 7 |             * /
 5 |       * /
 3 | * /
   +--------------------> 进度
     0%  25%  50%  75%  100%
```

Key operations:

- Do not fall back; climb one stair at a time.
- Place the highest point at 85–95%. Offer only minor wins along the way without releasing the main payoff early.

| Genre | Escalation |
|------|----------|
| Face-slapping wish fulfillment | Defeat opponents one at a time, each more severely |
| Layered revelation | Each layer of truth is more shocking |
| Underdog comeback | Each reversal exceeds the last |

Word allocation for 10,000 Chinese characters: oppression 20% → first counterattack 20% → second 25% → third 25% → resolution 10%.

### 5. Delayed-Gratification Arc: Long Setup → Eruption

```
情绪
10 |                       *
 5 |              *--------|
 3 |       *------         |
 1 | *------                |
   +-------------------------> 进度
     0%  20%  40%  60%  80%  100%
```

Key operations:

- Use the first 70–80% for suppression/setup and the final 20–30% for concentrated release.
- Every setup passage needs information and tension; it cannot be dull.
- Vary the forms of suppression, make the release dense, and end quickly afterward.

| Genre | Suppression | Eruption |
|------|----------|------------|
| Revenge wish fulfillment | Endurance, feigned weakness, oppression | Sudden comeback and complete reckoning |
| Identity reveal | Hidden power/identity | True identity exposed; everyone shocked |
| Long-range scheme | Protagonist acts in secret | Every piece falls into place at once |

Word allocation for 12,000 Chinese characters: initial suppression 20% → deeper setup 25% → maximum pressure 15% → trigger 5% → concentrated eruption 25% → resolution 10%.

### 6. Sharp-Turn Arc: Sudden Turn at 70–80%

```
情绪
10 |                *  急转后的新高/低点
 8 |           *--/
 3 |      /
   +-------------------------> 进度
     0%   30%   70%  80%  100%
```

Key operations:

- Follow a normal arc for 70–80%, then suddenly change direction.
- The turn occupies only one or two passages. Earlier clues must become logical in retrospect.

| Genre | First portion | After the turn |
|------|--------|--------|
| Disturbing in hindsight | Warm everyday life | Horrific truth emerges |
| Misidentified identity | Viewpoint A | It was actually B |
| Motivation reversal | Appears to be love | Actually hatred/exploitation |

Word allocation for 10,000 Chinese characters: establish illusion 35% → strengthen illusion 30% → illusion peak 10% → sharp turn 5% → unfold truth 15% → resolve new emotion 5%.

## Four Ways to Increase Midpoint Pressure

1. **Public escalation**: move private harm into a family banquet, company meeting, livestream, or ceremony.
2. **Double betrayal**: add another layer after a known betrayal—financial betrayal, reputational betrayal, change of allegiance, or falsified evidence.
3. **Accelerating cost**: continually raise the cost of inaction through lost opportunity, public humiliation, or harm to others.
4. **Strategic silence**: the protagonist delays retaliation, and silence increases pressure through others' misreading and the antagonist's growing audacity.

## Genre-Lane Strategy

| Lane | Best payoff | Midpoint focus | Preferred ending |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| Marriage/groveling pursuit | Opening humiliation → public counterattack → bad partner regrets it → status reversal | Public/private contrast, escalating costs, at least one mini-payoff | Quiet superiority and social-status reversal |
| Family ethics | High-pressure realistic situation and rupture through family betrayal | Layered pressure, moral coercion, one decisive refusal | Clear boundaries and exit from the old structure |
| Urban comeback/workplace | Underestimated → demonstrate ability → reveal identity | Ability remains underestimated but continually shown; unexpected performance at a key event | Identity fully revealed and opponent's cost made visible |
| Mystery reversal | Anomalous fact → peel back layers → overturn understanding | Every revelation creates a new question; at least one “thought it was solved, but it wasn't” | One truth strike, with the final line reconstructing the whole story |

## Six Rules for Managing Anticipation

1. **Law of maximum anticipation**: tension peaks in the instant before payoff. Reader need is greatest when fulfillment is imminent but incomplete.
2. **Control information order**: the first planted information directs assumptions. Controlling order controls expectation.
3. **Escalate anticipation**: add details to the base expectation and create increasingly specific unmet needs. Every detail must align with the core hook.
4. **Never break anticipation**: do not end the current expectation before establishing the next. Plant the seed of the new expectation before fulfilling the old.
5. **Safety during downturns**: in downward emotional movement, blame belongs elsewhere and credit remains with the protagonist. Give readers security through a possible solution or potential gain.
6. **Escalating payoff contrast**: raise consecutive payoffs across reach (individual → group → society), depth of revelation (appearance → essence → overturning truth), or status gap (unknown → major figure → everyone shocked).

## Core Emotional Formula

Separate three emotional layers in every scene:

1. The character's emotion.
2. The emotion communicated by the text.
3. The emotion actually experienced by the reader.

All three may differ: a character cries, the text flirts, and the reader feels satisfaction.

Example: the heroine is publicly rejected and fights back tears. Character: humiliation. The text keeps a smile on her lips: endurance. Readers feel the pressure with her and await retaliation: stored energy before payoff.

---

## Before-Reaction → Event → After-Reaction

Use for tragic, heroic-sacrifice, or regretful scenes:

1. **Before-reaction**: let readers know the bad outcome in advance, then depict something beautiful.
2. **Event**: make the bad outcome occur.
3. **After-reaction**: the protagonist expresses genuine feeling and changes through anger, desperation, or renewed resolve.

Example: establish that someone will die tomorrow → give her a beautiful day of shopping and fireworks → she dies → the protagonist fights in fury.

---

## Small Against Great

Use for passionate underdog scenes:

1. **Before-reaction**: establish the weak side's suffering and intensify it through contrast between current weakness and former strength.
2. **Arrival**: the powerful figure comes: “I know what you've suffered. I'm here.”
3. **After-reaction**: the weak side is rescued.
4. **Morale surge**: the entire atmosphere transforms.

## Emotional Layers in Conflict and Death Stakes

### Choosing a Death Stake

| Stake | Suitable scene | Operation |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Physical death | Horror/uncanny fiction | If the protagonist becomes too powerful, shift to career death or the story collapses |
| Career death | Workplace competition or cultivation-sect struggle | Failure resets everything to zero |
| Psychological death | Relationship collapse or destruction of belief | Creates the deepest emotional impact; prioritize for romance |

Choose one primary stake to carry through the book; use the others to support plot.

### Adhesive That Sustains Tension

The opposing sides must be unable to leave easily. Four adhesives: a reason to kill, such as inevitable conflict over a unique succession; job duty; moral responsibility when family is endangered; and a physical enclosure such as a locked-room instance.

### Long-Range Maintenance

- Do not make Volume 1's ending too complete. Perfect resolution kills continued-reading motivation.
- Plant the next problem while resolving the current one.
- Emotional tension must grow later in the story.

## Emotion Driven by “This Should Not Be Happening”

- “This should not be happening” is the most fundamental emotional driver. False accusations, injustice, and bullying naturally create it.
- Core logic: satisfaction requires preceding dissatisfaction; fairness requires preceding unfairness; strength requires preceding weakness.
- An unresolved state is also a powerful driver.
- Strong concepts evoke regret and a desire for change.

---

## Emotional Force of Ideological Conflict

- Conflicting beliefs produce deeper resonance than competing interests.
- A few key scenes are enough; extensive treatment is unnecessary.
- Idealism against reality can elevate a supporting character into greatness.
- Agreement with beliefs creates agreement with characterization and reader investment.
- Interweave belief-driven characters through the main plot; their pursuit and sacrifice raise the story's emotional ceiling.

## Three Layers of Emotional Engagement

The layers are **long-term emotional investment**, **short-term event conflict**, and **scene depiction**. They can combine, but the first two are central.

### Three Steps for Long-Term Guidance

1. **Drop a resonance bomb**: identify the character's most relatable anchor—dream, hatred, or fear.
2. **Make readers witnesses**: show effort and struggle, with gains at each step.
3. **Make readers participants**: use enough setup and detail to create a shared sense of fate.

### Short-Term Emotional Guidance

Short-term emotion comes from self-contained event conflict. **Direct conflict creates resonance more readily than complicated plot.**

Two event types:

- **Plot-driving event**: makes readers identify with the character's circumstances.
- **Character-defining event**: makes readers identify with the character's thinking and personality.

Use a character-defining event at the opening. Readers care about events only after they care about the person.

### Identification Trap

Before readers identify with the protagonist, the protagonist's weakness creates sympathy, but an enemy's weakness does too. Do not make the opponent pitiable before identification is established.

### Emotional Prose Techniques

- **Match sentence length to emotion and pace**: use short sentences for tension and decision, longer ones for setup and emotional accumulation. Short sentences accelerate; they are not the default texture. Do not fragment the whole text.
- **Emotion words**: `骤然、猛然、刺眼` create urgency.
- **Action words**: `抽、划、留下` provide imagery and externalize emotion.
- **Modifiers**: use adjectives precisely and sparingly.
- **Advanced rule**: prefer behavior that implies emotion over direct description, but not absolutely. State necessary interiority directly rather than piling up functionless gestures. **Write sorrow through joy**: apparent happiness at the saddest moment can intensify emotion; someone cheerful while going to their death can feel sadder than someone crying.

## Emotional Design for Male-Audience Romance

### Two Core Needs

- **Emotional value**: an exceptional, attractive woman appreciates the reader-surrogate, filling an emotional absence.
- **Self-esteem value**: arriving with a campus beauty or female executive provokes others' envy.

### Write Romance Like Progression Fiction

- Create an “affection progress bar”: stranger → good person → friend → in love without realizing it → …
- Ambiguous push-pull is usually more attractive than becoming a couple.
- Put internal and external obstacles between them; overcoming each deepens affection.

### Asymmetric Progress

The two progress lines cannot move together:

- **Emotion faster than social relationship**: both are fully attached but too stubborn to confess.
- **Social relationship faster than emotion**: transmigration supplies a wife or fiancée, but affection begins at zero.

### Key Prohibitions and Techniques

- The male lead should not chase or grovel. Let his strengths attract the heroine.
- A career plot is essential, and the heroine should contribute positively.
- Show her care according to characterization: an insecure woman secretly slips him medicine, a tsundere makes lunch while denying effort, a direct woman gives a gift openly.
- **Contrast**: compare earlier and later attitudes; use double standards between others and the protagonist.
- **Information-gap anticipation**: dual online and real-world identities make readers anticipate the secret-identity reveal.

## Unified View of Emotional Engagement

> Use when a passage feels weak or reader feedback is flat.

### Ultimate Formula

**Calm → activation → release → satisfaction**

| Technique | Activation | Release | Result |
|------|------|------|------|
| Showing off/face-slapping | Antagonist boasts | Protagonist overwhelms | Satisfaction |
| Building hatred | Negative emotion | Villain punished | Satisfaction |
| Suppress then elevate | Pressure | Eruption | Satisfaction |
| Anticipation | Create a gap | Close the gap | Satisfaction |

### Practical Points

- Label each scene with the reader's current stage and whether activation or release comes next.
- Activation without release makes readers frustrated enough to leave.
- Release without activation feels weak.
- The strength of activation determines the strength of release: greater hatred makes retaliation more satisfying.

## Underlying Mechanism of Sustained Anticipation

> Use when a goal fails to matter to readers or the six rules produce weak results.

### Anticipation vs. Goal

- **Goal** = what the character intends to do, such as find treasure or defeat a demon king.
- **Anticipation** = what readers expect will happen after success.
- A goal without anticipation has no stakes for readers. Convert the goal into an expectation.

### Certain vs. Uncertain Anticipation

- **Certain**, common in wish fulfillment: readers know the protagonist will win and care about how; process creativity matters more than outcome suspense.
- **Uncertain**, common in mystery: readers do not know the result; outcome suspense matters more than process experience.
- Spoilers do not necessarily hurt wish fulfillment because anticipation rests in the process, not the outcome.

### Opening and Closing Loops

- **Open loop** = create a gap through a question or mystery.
- **Close loop** = bridge the gap with an answer or revelation.
- **Nested open loop** = close one expectation while opening another, preserving continuity.
- Rule: when closing one loop, another must already be running.

### The Relationship Gap

Romance without obstacles creates no anticipation and feels unearned. Establish a gap through status difference, family opposition, misunderstanding, or conflicting missions.

## Quality Checklist

### Arc Selection and Pace

- [ ] Does the arc match the desired emotional effect?
- [ ] Are critical turns at the correct percentages: V bottom 60–70%, inverted-V peak 40–50%, sharp turn 70–80%?
- [ ] Does word allocation match the selected arc?

### Activation and Release

- [ ] Is every scene clearly in activation or release?
- [ ] Is there activation without release, causing frustration?
- [ ] Is there release without activation, weakening satisfaction?
- [ ] Do consecutive payoffs escalate in reach, revelation depth, or status gap?

### Anticipation

- [ ] Has each goal become a reader expectation?
- [ ] When one loop closes, is another already open?
- [ ] Does the romance contain obstacles?

### Emotional Identification

- [ ] Does the opening use a character-defining event?
- [ ] Before identification forms, does the story avoid making opponents pitiable?
- [ ] During downturns, does the story preserve security by keeping blame elsewhere and credit with the protagonist?

### Long-Range Maintenance

- [ ] Does resolving one problem plant the next?
- [ ] Does emotional tension grow later in the story?
- [ ] Is one death stake selected to carry through the book?
- [ ] Does an adhesive keep opponents from escaping the conflict easily?
