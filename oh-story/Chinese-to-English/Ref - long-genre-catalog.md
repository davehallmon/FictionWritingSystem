# Quick Reference: Long-Form Genre Frameworks

> **Purpose**: Consult when selecting a long-form premise or designing an outline. Node percentages help evaluate the distribution of functions across a volume or entire book; they are not rigid chapter quotas. Route short-form structures to `story-short-write`.

---

## Genre-Selection Router

Use the following conditions to identify the appropriate framework quickly. Determine the broad category first, then select the specific variation.

| If the Story’s Core Is... | Use This Framework | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A man hurts a woman, regrets it after she leaves, but cannot win her back | **Wife-Chasing Redemption** | The heroine must not look back after leaving |
| The protagonist dies tragically in a previous life, is reborn, and uses an information advantage to take revenge | **Rebirth Revenge** | Antagonists require nested tiers |
| A partner cheats or schemes against the protagonist, who awakens and becomes independent | **Affair/Marriage Drama** | The discovery process must contain suspense |
| An unreasonable person or bully oppresses an honest person and receives deserved punishment | **Social Drama** | Depend on details rather than major events |
| The heroine is abused to death and the hero regrets it after she dies | **Posthumous-Regret Fiction** | The core is “too late” |
| An unusual encounter leads a powerful man to pursue and pamper the heroine | **CEO Romance/Sweet Romance** | Reader retention depends on the density of affectionate moments |
| An underdog rises through progressive challenges in an urban martial-arts setting | **Urban Martial Arts** | Money-driven progression and tiered maps |
| A supposedly worthless or fallen genius reaches the top of a cultivation or fantasy world | **Cultivation/Fantasy** | The power system must be clear |
| A unique advantage or inventive premise is the primary selling point | **High-Concept Fiction** | The central hook determines the market lane |
| A protagonist without talent survives in a cultivation world through caution and calculation | **Mortal-Path Cultivation** | Weighing costs and benefits is the core pattern |
| A protagonist travels or is reborn into a pivotal historical moment and changes destiny | **Historical/Alternate History** | The information advantage is the greatest special advantage |
| A protagonist displays talent in entertainment while observers react with shock | **Entertainment-Industry Fiction** | Reaction chains are the core technique |
| The user identifies a target IP, the platform permits it, and recent samples remain relevant | **Fan Fiction** | Extract only the structure of “familiar world + new variable + rewritten iconic scenes”; do not retain a fixed IP list |
| Players are pulled into rule-governed instances and must survive | **Rules Horror** (see Other Genres) | The special advantage provides a plausible cover |
| An immortal protagonist watches eras rise and fall | **Immortality Fiction** (see Other Genres) | The mortal period is usually the most compelling |
| Other genres—Western fantasy, new-media fiction, comedy, infinite-flow fiction, mystery, martial arts, or regret fiction | **Other Genres** | Each has its own priorities; use as needed |

## Wife-Chasing Redemption

### Long Form: Eight Nodes

**Premise**: The heroine is hurt → she leaves → the hero regrets it and tries to win her back, but she does not look back. Possible settings include wealthy families, workplaces, the entertainment industry, or historical nobility. A power imbalance is required.

**Characters**

| Character | Arc |
|------|------|
| Heroine | Endures and gives → loses all hope and leaves → succeeds independently → takes control |
| Hero | Cold and arrogant → later humbled and desperate with regret; readers should feel that he deserves his suffering while still pitying him |
| Second male lead (optional) | Waits gently and faithfully, creating a crisis for the hero and an emotional outlet for readers |

**Eight Nodes**

| Node | Content | Proportion | Emotion |
|------|------|------|------|
| 1. Opening hook | Show the heroine’s current suffering | 5% | Oppression |
| 2. Abuse intensifies | Coldness or betrayal reaches its peak | 10% | Grief and anger |
| 3. Heroine awakens | She decides to leave | 10% | Pain → resolve |
| 4. Departure and renewal | Independent life or career | 15% | Catharsis through liberation |
| 5. Hero’s regret | He recognizes her importance | 10% | Satisfaction |
| 6. Career/growth | She succeeds in a new field | 15% | Catharsis through reversal of fortune |
| 7. Repeated pursuit | He repeatedly asks her to return and is rejected | 20% | Catharsis through humiliation |
| 8. Ending | Open ending, reconciliation, or tragedy | 15% | Release |

## Rebirth Revenge

### Long Form: Eight Nodes

**Premise**: The protagonist dies tragically in a previous life → is reborn → uses an information advantage to take revenge one enemy at a time. Possible settings include domestic intrigue, palace intrigue, corporate warfare, or school. A complex relationship network is required.

**Characters**

| Character | Key Points |
|------|------|
| Heroine | Naive in her previous life → calculating after rebirth. Special advantage = memories of her previous life. Core conflict = whether she will become like her enemies |
| Antagonist tiers | Minor antagonist in Chapters 3–10 → middle-tier antagonist in Chapters 20–50 → major antagonist across the entire book; tiers are nested |
| Ally/hero | May have been misunderstood in the previous life; trust develops gradually and produces a crisis of confidence |

**Eight Nodes**

| Node | Content | Proportion | Emotion |
|------|------|------|------|
| 1. Conflict revealed | Reveal the truth of the previous life’s tragic death | 5% | Shock/anger |
| 2. Awareness after rebirth | Organize memories and establish goals | 5% | Anticipation |
| 3. Past and present intertwine | Contrast former blindness with present clarity | 15% | Catharsis through an overwhelming information advantage |
| 4. Layered revenge + new problems | Defeat minor antagonists while revealing a larger conspiracy | 25% | Alternating catharsis and suspense |
| 5. Multiple obstacles | Mid-tier counterattack + crisis of trust | 15% | Tension |
| 6. Decisive climactic strike | Final scheme and confrontation | 15% | Peak catharsis |
| 7. Antagonists’ downfall | Each receives retribution | 10% | Cathartic release |
| 8. New life | Choose a new direction | 10% | Healing/acceptance |

## Affair/Marriage Drama

### Long Form: Eight Nodes

**Premise**: The protagonist discovers a partner’s affair → awakens → becomes independent → punishes wrongdoing and rewards integrity. The setting is a modern city or household grounded in highly relatable daily life.

**Characters**: Heroine (homemaker or professional → independent) | Scoundrel husband (emotional or physical affair, asset concealment, domestic violence, or coercive control, escalating in severity) | Affair partner (optional conflict catalyst) | Support system (best friend, family, or attorney)

**Eight Nodes**

| Node | Proportion | Emotion |
|------|------|------|
| 1. Daily oppression/injustice | 10% | Frustration |
| 2. Affair or scheme discovered | 15% | Shock → anger |
| 3. Decision to leave | 10% | Pain → catharsis |
| 4. Career rebuilt | 20% | Growth-driven catharsis |
| 5. Scoundrel husband interferes | 10% | Tension |
| 6. Counterattack | 15% | Catharsis through humiliation |
| 7. Retribution | 10% | Cathartic release |
| 8. New life | 10% | Healing |

## Social Drama

### Long Form: Eight Nodes

**Premise**: An antagonist commits outrageously unreasonable acts → a decent person is oppressed → the victim fights back → wrongdoing receives its due. Possible settings include villages, small towns, neighborhoods, or extended families where the pressures of a close-knit community matter.

**Characters**: Protagonist (an honest person with firm boundaries) | Antagonist (an unreasonable person, bully, or terrible relative whose cruelty is inventive) | Onlookers (snobs, opportunists, and a small number of principled people)

**Eight Nodes**

| Node | Proportion | Emotion |
|------|------|------|
| 1. Unreasonable antagonist appears | 10% | Anger |
| 2. Oppression escalates | 15% | Frustration |
| 3. Pressure peaks | 10% | Extreme indignation |
| 4. First counterattack | 15% | Initial catharsis |
| 5. Counterattack from the antagonist | 10% | Tension |
| 6. Humiliation | 15% | Climactic catharsis |
| 7. Retribution | 15% | Cathartic release |
| 8. Goodness rewarded | 10% | Healing |

## Cultivation/Fantasy

### Long Form: Eight Nodes

**Premise**: The protagonist suffers a major blow → rises from the depths. The setting is a cultivation or fantasy world with a clearly defined hierarchy of power.

**Characters**: Protagonist (supposedly worthless or a fallen genius → reaches the summit; special advantage = unusual constitution, inheritance, or system) | Antagonist tiers (peers → elders → sect boss → cosmic order, rising by dimension) | Mentor (limited to a particular stage; neither too strong nor too weak)

**Eight Nodes**

| Node | Proportion | Emotion |
|------|------|------|
| 1. The blow arrives | 5% | Shock/grief and anger |
| 2. Conflict deepens | 10% | Oppression |
| 3. First counterattack | 15% | Initial catharsis |
| 4. Greatest crisis | 15% | Extreme tension |
| 5. Breakthrough/growth | 10% | Reversal-of-fortune catharsis |
| 6. Decisive strike | 15% | Climactic catharsis |
| 7. Retribution/reckoning | 15% | Cathartic release |
| 8. New journey | 15% | Anticipation |

**Critical dimensions**: Clear and stable power system | Distinctive special advantage with limitations that evolve alongside growth | Strategic combat rather than pure numerical comparisons | Maps revealed in tiers | Evenly paced advancement

## Posthumous-Regret Fiction

### Long Form: Eight Nodes

**Premise**: The heroine is abused to death → the hero regrets it → the truth emerges → retribution follows. Possible settings include wealthy families, historical nobility, or the entertainment industry, with the power imbalance maximized.

**Characters**: Heroine (patient and kind → death; greater influence after death than before it) | Hero (coldness → emptiness after her death → truth → unbearable pain → lifelong atonement) | Mastermind (optional; adds a reversal)

**Eight Nodes**

| Node | Proportion | Emotion |
|------|------|------|
| 1. Layered abuse | 20% | Extreme oppression |
| 2. Heroine dies | 5% | Extreme grief |
| 3. Regret begins | 10% | Oppression → unease |
| 4. Truth discovered | 20% | Heartbreak |
| 5. Mastermind exposed | 10% | Shock/anger |
| 6. Retribution | 15% | Catharsis + pain |
| 7. Causal reckoning | 10% | Release |
| 8. Lingering ending | 10% | Unresolved regret |

## Urban Martial Arts

### Long Form: Tiered-Map Structure

**Premise**: A poor student or martial artist at the bottom → advances through battles and earns money → enters higher tiers. The setting combines a modern city with a martial-arts system and may include school, martial-arts academies, martial-arts agencies, and military storylines.

**Characters**: Protagonist (money is the primary motivation; school → academy → martial-arts agency → military → interstellar) | Supporting tiers (classmates, mentors, romantic interest, and school bully) | Antagonist tiers (school bully → underground organization → demons → international forces → extraterrestrials)

**Tiered-Map Event Pool**

| Map | Available Events |
|------|----------|
| School | Gifted class, monthly exams, joint exams, college entrance exam, duel with the school bully, campus-beauty storyline, scholarships |
| Martial-arts academy | Recognition, inherited techniques, duels with senior disciples, municipal/provincial/national tournaments |
| Police/public security | Robbers, demons, instances, urban bosses |
| University | Upgraded versions of school events |
| Martial-arts agency/military | Upgraded versions of academy events |
| Interstellar space | Upgraded national events + world expansion |

**Key points**: Repeatedly connect progression to money | Use transitional characters when changing maps | Weak statistics before the first one hundred thousand Chinese characters are normal | First three chapters = disadvantaged position → special advantage → first demonstration

## CEO Romance/Sweet Romance

### Long Form: Five Stages

**Premise**: Unusual encounter → the hero pursues the heroine → the relationship deepens → external resistance appears → the couple overcomes it. The setting may involve wealthy families, business, or high society.

**Five Stages**

| Stage | Content | Emotion |
|------|------|------|
| 1. Meeting | The hero is attracted to one of the heroine’s traits | Novelty |
| 2. Pursuit | He uses status or wealth to create opportunities; her best friend is astonished | Sweetness/catharsis |
| 3. Commitment | The heroine is moved, and they become a couple | Satisfaction |
| 4. Resistance | Family, former girlfriend, or business rival | Tension |
| 5. Resolution | They overcome the obstacle together, deepening the relationship | Catharsis/healing |

**Characters**: Hero (powerful, devoted, and affectionate; using his power or wealth for the heroine is central to reader identification) | Heroine (cannot be decorative; she needs a dramatic quality that makes her irreplaceable to him) | Opposition (family elder, former girlfriend, or business rival)

**Key points**: Reader retention depends on the density of affectionate moments | Pampering must take forms unavailable to ordinary people | Resistance cannot be so strong that the story becomes abusive or so weak that it loses tension | The heroine needs an independent strength

## Fan Fiction

### Core Mechanism

Use an existing IP’s world and characters to tell a new story. The central selling point is: “What would happen if this protagonist existed in this world?”

### Boundaries

Do not maintain a fixed list of IP-based genres. Only when the user specifies a target IP, the platform permits it, and recent samples remain relevant should you extract the structural function of “familiar world + new protagonist variable + rewritten iconic scenes.”

### Writing Priorities

- Most readers are casual fans: More readers have not consumed the source material than have. Hardcore fidelity is not mandatory.
- Catharsis comes first: Do not sacrifice reader satisfaction solely to preserve the original work.
- Historical or setting details may be simplified, and moderate adaptation need not impair readability. Character names, however, must remain consistent with the source’s region and culture. Use official names or translations for established characters and follow the region’s naming conventions for new characters. Do not mix language traditions or insert modernized or politicized names that conflict with the setting.
- Advantage: The world is already established, reducing exposition. Risk: Devoted fans may challenge inaccuracies.
- Deeper approach: Add family, redemption, or the repair of old regrets beneath demonstrations of power. Reactions should include excitement, regret, or acceptance, not only “That’s so powerful.”

### “Only After Rebelling Did I Learn” Subgenre

The protagonist reaches the pinnacle of their own world, then discovers that they inhabit a particular IP universe. Title formula: “[After Achieving Something], I Learned This Was [an IP World].”

## High-Concept Fiction

### Core Mechanism

A unique special advantage or creative premise is the central selling point. Genre is only the shell; the real product is “this idea.”

### Development Process

Obtain an idea → assess its potential, meaning whether it creates room for imagination → choose a compatible genre → reshape it into an emotional gap → design the essential structure of the special advantage → build the selling point → main plot → framework → story units

### Essential Structures for Special Advantages

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|------|------|------|
| Conditional trigger | Satisfy X → receive Y | Looting bodies for treasure, daily check-in rewards |
| Multi-condition stages | Multiple conditions → staged advancement | Collecting fragments, completing achievements |
| Inverse mechanism | Lose X → receive a stronger Y | Growing stronger through suffering, becoming richer through debt |

### Sustaining a Long Story

After determining the central selling point, weave multiple patterns into a repeating cycle. Each story unit develops one pattern, while the main plot connects the units. Use a first-level structural cycle: repeat the underlying pattern while escalating the target, setting, or scale.

### Four Types of High-Concept Ideas

1. Special-advantage ideas: chat groups, systems, or a mentor living in a ring
2. Plot ideas: reversals or premises
3. Setting ideas: worlds or cultivation systems
4. Character ideas: identities or character concepts

**Avoid**: Directly copying everything will fail. The central hook determines the market lane and audience.

## Mortal-Path Cultivation

### Core Characteristics

The protagonist has no unusual talent or background and survives a brutal cultivation world through caution, intelligence, and careful evaluation of costs and benefits.

### Compared with Traditional Progression Fiction

| Dimension | Mortal-Path Cultivation | Traditional Progression |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Talent | Ordinary or poor | Genius or unusual constitution |
| Special advantage | Weak or nonexistent | Powerful system or inheritance |
| Behavior | Cautious calculation; escape comes first | Passionate frontal conflict and challenges above one’s level |
| Catharsis | Superior intelligence and preparation | Superior power |
| Pacing | Slow-building, detailed, and rich in foreshadowing | Fast-paced and dense with cathartic moments |

### Instance-Entry Formula

Minor supporting character appears → supporting character’s story → instance information revealed → protagonist decides to enter

### Critical Dimensions

The protagonist’s caution must be genuine; they cannot claim caution while behaving recklessly | Weighing costs and benefits is the central pattern | Supporting characters have independent interests | Foreshadowing and payoff must be precise

## Historical/Alternate History

### Long Form

**Premise**: The protagonist travels or is reborn into a pivotal historical moment and uses modern knowledge or an information advantage to change destiny.

**Subgenres**

| Type | Central Selling Point | Writing Priorities |
|------|----------|----------|
| Imperial-examination fiction | Modern knowledge overwhelms historical examinations | Poetry-based demonstrations of talent, advancement through officialdom, political strategy |
| Military history | Modern military knowledge transforms historical warfare | Training troops, reforming equipment, winning while outnumbered |
| Enterprise/settlement building | Developing an economy in an earlier era | Introducing technology, building a commercial empire, improving livelihoods |
| Cultural heritage | Appeal of traditional culture | List-based demonstrations of knowledge and cultural pride |

**Key points**: Most readers are casual enthusiasts; emotional satisfaction takes priority over total historical fidelity | Modern understanding and information provide the greatest special advantage | Alternate history can lower the barrier to entry | Cataloging achievements combined with historical resonance is powerful

## Entertainment-Industry Fiction

### Core Mechanism

The protagonist develops a career in entertainment. The central catharsis is “displayed talent + astonished observers.”

### Subgenres

| Type | Characteristics | Current Position |
|------|------|------|
| Chinese entertainment | Domestic entertainment industry | Mainstream, with the most polished works |
| Korean entertainment | Korean entertainment industry | Niche |
| Fictional entertainment | Invented entertainment industry | Often focuses on one heroine; systems provide direct rewards |

### Writing Priorities

Talent demonstrations are the primary status-display scenes. Show a reaction chain among the audience, judges, and rivals | May incorporate catalog-style fiction | Fictional celebrities avoid controversies involving real people and provide more freedom | Keep the special advantage somewhat ambiguous; later conflicts can undermine literal foreknowledge, so devices such as dreams can preserve internal logic

### Advanced Methods

Supporting-character situation comedy: Fill spaces between main-plot events with daily interactions among several characters in a recurring location | Empathetic contrast: Characters may hold elevated positions, but readers must not feel distanced from them. Every character needs an ordinary human quality.

## Other Genres

### Rules Horror

A form of infinite-flow fiction in which players are pulled into rule-governed instances and must survive. Combining national destiny with livestreaming offers the greatest return for the effort. Build an instance through: background story → rules as presentation → successful route + `dead end` → rhythm of others dying → protagonist’s demonstration → revelation → thematic elevation. Tomato Novel’s mainstream approach follows cathartic fiction; strategy and a special advantage provide a plausible surface logic.

### Immortality Fiction

The central selling point is the passage of time: The immortal protagonist watches eras and civilizations change. The mortal period is usually the strongest. The genre’s natural weakness appears after cultivation begins: The protagonist’s power grows, equally powerful figures emerge, and “waiting them out” loses significance, turning the story into conventional cultivation fiction. Possible solutions include preserving the advantage of time, using generational succession, creating tiered worlds, and fully developing the mortal period.

### Western Fantasy/Knight Fiction

Western fantasy is often an Eastern-fantasy core in a different shell. Knights naturally provide a progression ladder for the power system. Prefer D&D-style, Witcher-style, or territory-building fiction over Japanese-style fantasy. Begin from a low position, such as a stable hand or blacksmith’s apprentice, and emphasize determined effort.

### New-Media Fiction

Everything serves emotion. The primary emotional pairs are frustration → status demonstration and anger → satisfying release. A two-direction structure combines strength with suffering through contrast and conflict. Conflict must arise from real-world logic. The three essentials are memes, rhythm, and emotion. An extreme information advantage creates intense anticipation.

### Comedy

Two principles: Comedy must follow logic, producing surprise within established rules; and memes should be adapted through their underlying idea rather than copied. The author must find the story satisfying first. People close to the protagonist should support the protagonist unconditionally.

### Infinite-Flow Fiction

Use a game-instance structure, with one self-contained story every twenty to thirty chapters. Maintain a real-world main plot as a fallback connection between instances.

### Mystery

Suspense depends primarily on setup and atmosphere, not gore or violence. Carefully control the release of information.

### Martial-Arts Progression

The primary satisfaction—advancement and combat—is relatively narrow, but mature conventions make the genre difficult to mishandle. Closely modeling the architecture of a successful work’s world can lower the barrier to entry.

### Regret Fiction

Avoid stale wrappers such as broken engagements. Use innovative framing; the source of regret may shift from romance to a career choice or life decision.

## Long-Form Design Reminders

| Check | Explanation |
|--------|------|
| Genre identification | Which framework applies? Are frameworks being combined? |
| Pacing proportions | Do node functions appear in the appropriate volume or book stage? If they shift, does genre or character causality support the change? |
| Reversal position | Has the old understanding already supported meaningful action, and is there enough room after the reveal for consequences? |
| Hook density | Do high-pressure, progression, and low-pressure chapters create variation? Has the expectation chain broken? |
| Length efficiency | Are there unnecessary passages? What can be removed? |
| Emotional landing | What should readers feel after the final sentence? |

## Quality Checklist

After using this guide to position or outline a long-form story, verify every item:

| # | Check | Passing Standard |
|---|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Correct genre positioning** | The router confirms the framework without ambiguity |
| 2 | **Complete structural functions** | All functions assigned to the eight nodes or five stages are present. Actual proportions follow genre, volume function, and character causality rather than a rigid ±5% rule |
| 3 | **Complete emotional rhythm** | A complete emotional arc of oppression → eruption → release exists without gaps |
| 4 | **Clear character arc** | The protagonist has distinct starting and ending states; the antagonist has an independent motive and is not merely a plot device |
| 5 | **Unbroken expectation chain** | Chapter intensity matches function; the next layer of expectation is established before the old one is resolved, without assigning hooks by fixed word counts |
| 6 | **Internally consistent special advantage** | Rules are clear, use has limitations, and the mechanism remains stable later |
| 7 | **Effective information advantage** | The design includes information known to readers but not characters, or known to one character but not an opponent |
| 8 | **Techniques match the genre** | The corresponding genre’s technique entries have been reviewed with no omissions |
| 9 | **No wasted passages** | Every passage advances the plot or deepens characterization |
| 10 | **Ending emotion matches the genre** | The ending aligns with the genre’s core emotion: cathartic fiction delivers release or healing; tragic fiction leaves regret or resonance |
