# Long-Form Quality Coverage

> Use with `agent-quality.md` and load only for the `long` profile. Review long-form fiction at the chapter, story-unit, volume, and complete-book levels. Short-form percentages, per-section length targets, and dialogue-density rules do not apply.

## Opening Three Chapters

- [ ] Chapter 1 establishes the protagonist's circumstances, active objective, or an anomaly that cannot be ignored rather than merely presenting the world.
- [ ] Chapter 2 escalates resistance, cost, or informational complexity instead of repeating the same kind of setback from Chapter 1.
- [ ] Chapter 3 provides a provisional direction for investigation, an executable objective, or the first payoff while preserving a higher-order question.
- [ ] The protagonist makes an irreplaceable choice within the first three chapters; a mentor, institution, or coincidence does not take ownership of crucial causality or rewards.
- [ ] Every chapter ends with a reason to continue reading. A low-pressure chapter may end with a provisional objective, relationship change, or unfinished action; it does not require intense suspense.
- [ ] Within the first three chapters, the central mechanism establishes at least two verifiable hard rules and one unexplained exception. The exception creates a question without invalidating the rules just established.
- [ ] An executable provisional objective remains after Chapter 3. If a suspect, entry point, or clue carrier exits, leave a traceable object, location, witness, or chain of authorization.

## Serialization Rhythm

- [ ] The most recent chapter window shows visible progress. Determine the window's length from the current volume and the chapters' functions rather than a fixed number of short-form sections.
- [ ] High-pressure, advancement, low-pressure, relationship, and information-consolidation chapters create tension and release. Consecutive chapters do not sustain the same emotional motif and same hook at maximum intensity.
- [ ] Someone maintains short-, medium-, and long-term expectations. Establish the next layer of expectation before resolving the previous one.
- [ ] A chapter-level reversal changes the next action; a volume-level reversal changes the reader's understanding of the current mechanism; the complete-book truth is not spent prematurely.

## Reader Contract and Endgame Reserves

- [ ] Deliver the central appeal promised by the opening, with clear ownership of expectations and rewards.
- [ ] The protagonist retains causal and resolution authority. Supporting characters may execute local actions but must not silently take ownership of crucial choices or resolutions.
- [ ] Delayed expectations accrue interest instead of disappearing beneath process, explanation, or new worldbuilding.
- [ ] The current volume does not use an archenemy, identity, truth, or power ceiling meant to unlock only in a later phase.
- [ ] After a climax, permit brief low pressure and a visible reward rather than mechanically introducing a larger crisis immediately.

## Long-Form Reverse Checks

- After removing a “safe explanation,” does the emotion become stronger while the causality still holds?
- If a risky plot event remains, does it produce stronger expectation, cost, relationship change, or resource change?
- Does the chapter's function depend on a fixed “one climax per chapter” rule or a whole-story percentage? If so, return to the chapter's intended function and cross-chapter causality.

## Review Output Contract

- Every blocking and high-risk finding must include traceable evidence, the consequence of failure, and the smallest viable fix. Add one optional alternative only when the solution genuinely branches in different directions.
- The smallest viable fix must protect later narrative capacity: identify which expectation to preserve, which reveal to delay, and what specifically the next phase pursues, rather than merely making the current three chapters busier.
- Consolidate similar symptoms under one root cause. Prioritize problems that invalidate a phase objective, expose the mechanism's ceiling prematurely, or transfer the protagonist's causal authority elsewhere.
