## Contents

- [1. General Checks](#1-general-checks)

## 1. General Checks

### Chapter Structure

- [ ] Opening has a hook, not weather, scenery, or routine.
- [ ] Middle contains progress: fast-paced genres need a visible event; slow-burn genres may use a change in state.
- [ ] The situation changes; after the chapter, the world is different.
- [ ] Ending lands on change rather than summary.

### Opening: First 300–500 Chinese Characters

- [ ] Hook captures attention.
- [ ] Does not begin with weather, scenery, or routine.
- [ ] Protagonist appears quickly.
- [ ] Selling point or crisis is visible.

### Chapter Progress

- [ ] The chapter changes at least one of seven state types: goal, risk, information, relationship, resource, identity, or emotional position. Fast-paced Fanqie/wish-fulfillment fiction needs a visible event or payoff. Only slow-burn or emotional fiction may pass through a small, low-pressure change; not every chapter needs a climax.
- [ ] It is not filler. Ask what state change would be lost if the chapter were removed; compress only when there is no answer.
- [ ] A task bottleneck is useful only if becoming stuck produces one of those state changes.
- [ ] Strength of progression follows the book's established genre, fulfillment rhythm, and comparables rather than a fixed universal word count.

### Information Delivery

- [ ] No long expository block about the setting.
- [ ] Information travels with conflict through events.
- [ ] No more than three new concepts in one chapter.

### Scene Check

- [ ] Scene has an objective: what does the character want?
- [ ] Scene has an obstacle.
- [ ] Scene ends differently than it began.
- [ ] Characters do things instead of merely feeling things.
- [ ] No removable paragraphs.

### Chapter Ending

- [ ] Lands on change.
- [ ] Contains at least one crisis, decision, discovery, or reversal.
- [ ] Is not a summary.
- [ ] Pulls readers to the next page.

### Language

- [ ] No empty lyrical passages.
- [ ] No several consecutive paragraphs with the same emotion.
- [ ] Dialogue fits identity; different characters speak differently.
- [ ] Emotion lands through action rather than direct labels such as “he was sad.”
- [ ] No chapter/writing metadata outside the title line. Prose must not contain terms matched by `第[一二三四五六七八九十百千万两0-9]+章|上一章|上章|前一章|本章|这一章|前文|后文|伏笔|细纲|读者`. When connecting earlier events, use a perceptible event anchor or relative time. Exception: a character genuinely reads or discusses “Chapter X” in-world or genuinely speaks as an author/reader.

### Serial Continuity

- [ ] No forgotten promises or planted clues.
- [ ] No sudden mass of new setting information.
- [ ] Foreshadowing advances.
- [ ] Story engine remains active.
- [ ] Against the approved detailed outline, the chapter adds no independent event, character decision, relationship change, revelation, or subplot that affects later work. Incidental conversation, immediate reaction, and micro-connections serving an existing beat are allowed. A new meal, promise, choice, result, or future obligation is blocking.

### Filler Detection

Warning signs:

- Entire chapter's dialogue contains no new information.
- Same emotion occupies more than three paragraphs.
- More than 500 Chinese characters of scene description without plot progress.
- A character recalls prior events without a new perspective.
- More than two consecutive chapters lack conflict.
- Removable procedural detail or task bottlenecks are filler; compress or delete them.

## 2. Long-Form Checks

### Golden Three Chapters

- [ ] Hook within the first 500 Chinese characters of Chapter 1?
- [ ] Protagonist appears in Chapter 1?
- [ ] Chapter 1 contains an event rather than pure setup?
- [ ] Chapter 2 escalates the conflict?
- [ ] Chapter 3 provides a reason to continue?
- [ ] At least two payoffs across the first three chapters?
- [ ] No long worldbuilding exposition?
- [ ] Each chapter ends with a reason to continue? A weak hook or stage objective is enough for low-pressure/transitional chapters; strong suspense is not mandatory.

### Pace

- [ ] Clear progress across the latest five chapters?
- [ ] More than 5,000 Chinese characters between payoffs? Apply this to high-pressure/progression chapters; exclude low-pressure, relationship, and information-consolidation chapters.
- [ ] More than two consecutive chapters without conflict? Breathing chapters are normal. The red line is two consecutive chapters with pressure ≤1 or adjacent chapters converging emotionally.
- [ ] Does a volume contain high and low chapter roles rather than one constant intensity? Are low-pressure + transition chapters restrained to roughly 15% or less?

### Characters

- [ ] Protagonist behavior fits characterization?
- [ ] Supporting characters have presence?
- [ ] Antagonist's stature matches the current stage?

### Five-Dimension Scoring

Score each dimension from 0–100 and choose a revision strategy from the results.

### Dimension 1: Core Consistency

Check whether major conflict, action, and motivation remain consistent.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Motivation changes suddenly without setup | critical | Add a triggering event |
| Central conflict contradicts itself | high | Revise the conflict setting retrospectively |
| Key action contradicts personality | high | Adjust action or add explanation |
| Minor conflict forgotten | medium | Resolve or reduce it |

### Dimension 2: Surface Originality

Check whether syntax and wording are sufficiently original and avoid formulaic expression.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Copying source phrasing creates unnatural voice | medium | Rewrite only genuine AI-sounding phrasing; preserve normal original sentences |
| Heavy use of AI-signature words | high | Replace with concrete description |
| Same syntax repeats | medium | Vary expression |
| Excessively literary description through ornate diction, formal register, or chained similes | medium | Make it colloquial or action-based. Action-based does not mean strings of three-to-five-character fragments; revised narration should still favor flowing comma-linked sentences |

### Dimension 3: Format Consistency

Check whether paragraphs break naturally by dramatic unit/shot, subject and character-name rhythm feels natural, and opening/ending formats agree.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Mechanical character-count breaks or excessive subjects | medium | Reorganize by dramatic unit; name at paragraph opening, use pronouns/omission within, and repeat the name at key turns |
| Chapter length misses target | **high** | During writing/outline repair, return to the detailed outline and expand planned beats first. De-AI editing of existing prose must not add plot |
| Inconsistent dialogue/description format | low | Standardize format |

### Dimension 4: Readability

Check for verbosity, AI voice, vague summaries, and stock rhetoric.

| AI-voice feature | Recognition | Fix |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Empty summary | `他终于明白了`, `一切尽在不言中` | Delete and use action |
| Stock rhetoric | `命运仿佛在和他开玩笑` | Delete or use concrete description |
| Simile stacking | Repeated `像/好像/仿佛/如同`, with each image explained through comparison | Keep only the few with narrative function; convert the rest to action, object, sound, or consequence |
| Emotion label | `他感到一阵悲伤` | Express through behavior |
| Idle interiority | Internal monologue adds no information, repeats one emotion, or restates known facts | Compress only the idle portion; preserve monologue carrying information, decisions, or emotional turns regardless of sentence count |

### Dimension 5: Logical Coherence

Check flow between sentences/paragraphs and setting conflicts.

| Problem | Severity | Fix |
|------|--------|------|
| Contradictory setting | critical | Locate and unify it |
| Timeline error | high | Mark and correct timeline |
| Inconsistent character information | high | Compare against character profile |
| Broken causal chain | medium | Add a transition |

### Revision Strategy

| Primary problem | Strategy | Description |
|----------|------|------|
| Low core consistency | rewrite | Rewrite relevant passages around the central conflict |
| Excess length | compress | Remove content that does not advance plot |
| Meaningless bottleneck | compress | Preserve the resulting change and remove procedure |
| Strong AI voice | de_ai | Replace banned words and revise sentence patterns |
| Many minor issues | polish | Refine language details |

### Two-Way Review: Reader Contract and Endgame Reserves

See `reader-contract-and-progression.md`. First ask “Is it safe?” then “Is it compelling?” **Safe does not mean compelling**. Avoiding contract violations only means no red line was crossed; emotional fulfillment must still be strong.

- [ ] **Reader contract**: Does the chapter/volume fulfill the opening promise, or hand its central selling point to an institution, supporting character, or accident? Mark: contract-safe / needs reinforcement / contract breach.
- [ ] **Protagonist agency**: Judge causal authority + resolution authority, not page time. Use the four decisive-node questions to test decisions, irreplaceable choices, consequences, and central resolution. Supporting characters may perform local actions but cannot silently take ownership of anticipation.
- [ ] **Expectation debt**: Is the promise repaid, postponed with interest, or consumed by explanation, procedure, and setting?
- [ ] **Endgame reserves**: Does the unit unlock an endgame trump card too early—nemesis, truth, identity, or cheat ceiling? Does an advancement line approach its ceiling with no further step? If both answers are no, pass it. One battle yielding gains on several lines is good design, not excessive speed.
- [ ] **Ownership of fulfillment**: Is anticipation resolved by the promised owner? Voluntary concession is a red flag only when long-term net benefit is negative and control/causal authority also declines. Make greater control, future benefit, power, or information received through strategic concession visible.
- [ ] **Pacing continuation**: After climax/fulfillment, does the story allow brief low pressure and small visible gain/reward instead of mechanically adding immediate danger? Does each new element pay its “new-book debt” rather than evade old promises?
- [ ] **Genre-specific risk**: In promise-fulfillment or competence fantasy, does the protagonist repeatedly create disasters through stupidity or avoidable incompetence that others must clean up, damaging causal authority and trust in resolution?
- [ ] **Reverse review**: Would removing the “safety explanation” strengthen emotion? If a risky scene remains, does it create stronger anticipation, cost, or payoff?
- [ ] **Integrated line**: Do contract, agency, expectation debt, and endgame reserves support one another rather than merely achieving “safe”?

### Falsifiable Emotional Causality for a Story Unit

- [ ] Is the objective merely a label such as “passion” or “sadness,” without emotional causality from prior state → trigger → resulting state?
- [ ] Is the carrier only a tool character waiting for the protagonist to solve a problem?
- [ ] Does the climax depend on coincidence or a last-minute new rule?
- [ ] Does the protagonist's hard ability serve the core emotion rather than only self-validation?
- [ ] Does individual, relationship, or group fulfillment return to the genre contract? Use group fulfillment only when the genre requires it.
- [ ] Is “becoming safer” or “growing” mistaken for a climax without a change in emotional meaning?
- [ ] Do mechanisms remain optional rather than turning objects, misunderstanding, reversals, death, disease, crying, or group elevation into universal requirements?
