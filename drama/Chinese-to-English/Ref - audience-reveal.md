# What the Audience May Know at This Moment

Rear views, door gaps, off-screen voices, shallow depth of field, and occlusion determine when the audience learns someone's identity, sees evidence, or discovers that someone is behind a door.
Prevent two opposite errors at once: a beautiful composition hides information that should be communicated, or the camera prematurely reveals information that should remain withheld.

## One Fact, One Determination

For every fact with a timed reveal, document the following beneath the corresponding `SHOT-...`:

- **Source of the fact:** The scene ID, dialogue, action, or on-screen text from which it comes.
- **Permission for this shot:** Reveal it now or withhold it temporarily.
- **Visible or audible carrier:** What the audience can specifically see or hear.
- **Release point:** If withheld, the visible event after which it is revealed.
- **Protection method:** How occlusion, depth of field, off-screen sound, lighting, or composition protects it.
- **Dramatic reason:** Why this timing changes understanding or pressure.

Within one shot, “the arrival recognizes the protagonist” and “the arrival's identity” are separate facts. A familiar form of address may first come from off-screen while the arriving person's face remains in shadow. If both are collapsed into “reveal later,” downstream stages cannot handle voice and identity separately.

Withholding information has no inherent value. If the release point cannot be stated, the reveal has not been designed. During review, check both what the source permits the audience to learn at this moment and what this shot must make the audience understand.
