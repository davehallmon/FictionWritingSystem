# Story Engine

## I. How Pressure Appears and How the Protagonist Responds

Pressure always appears as **one specific number plus one specific deadline**: one million followers in five days, or three million points of topic engagement. The protagonist always responds the same way—he does not try to persuade anyone; he creates a work and lets it speak for him. This is his only instrument and also his only weakness: until the work is finished, he neither knows about nor can respond to anything happening outside.

## II. How the Opposition Counters and Why the Protagonist Cannot Withdraw

Opposition has three escalating layers:

| Layer | Opposing party | Countermeasure | Why the protagonist cannot withdraw |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prejudice among unfamiliar viewers | They decide before watching that an army account entering the arena is degrading itself | Withdrawal means mission failure, activating the device's terms |
| 2 | Institutional caution | Superiors fear an incident; their first response is to hide the person | His work bears the unit's name, so withdrawing would leave the unit stranded in place |
| 3 | People who profit from controversy | They attack credentials rather than facts and need no evidence | Silence will be read as admission, while the work he is creating remains unfinished |

The third layer is the hardest in the entire series: **the opponent need not be right, only faster**.

## III. What Each Round of Conflict Changes

| Round | What changes |
|---|---|
| EP001–002 | Resources: from nothing to one work, while incurring a debt (the device's terms) |
| EP003–004 | Information: the work overturns a group's judgment of him; one person remembers his name |
| EP005–009 | Power: he changes from a recruit acting on his own into someone with official endorsement, at the cost of losing control over the event's direction |
| EP010–011 | Exposure: he sees real firepower for the first time and, also for the first time, doubts whether his version is worthy |
| EP012–017 | Relationship and cost: someone shields him from pressure without his knowledge |
| EP018–024 | Understanding: he finally comprehends what he has actually filmed |

## IV. Premise-Device Contract (Creator Layer, Complete; In-Story Disclosure Tracked Separately)

Device type: **authorization-based** (an external rule grants capabilities).
The table below is the complete contract accepted by the creator. **Every term was accepted before it first took effect.** In-story disclosure may occur later than the contract and may be incomplete. This table supports subsequent reconciliation; it is not in-story information.

| Term | Category | Content | First effective | Rule reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEV-01 | Capability scope | Grants execution-layer abilities only: singing, directing, playing instruments, and finished accompaniment assets. It does not grant judgment about subject, platform, timing, or audience | EP001 | Reliable |
| DEV-02 | Trigger condition | Responds only when the host forms an explicit, specific need | EP001 | Reliable |
| DEV-03 | Failure condition | The device does not proactively suggest solutions or predict mission success or failure | EP001 | Reliable |
| DEV-04 | Cost of use | Everything the device provides is regarded by this world as his own talent. This misattribution cannot be corrected—if he denies it, the capability granted by the device fails during that performance | EP001 (cost first becomes visible in EP007) | Reliable |
| DEV-05 | Capability scope | It grants ability, not finished-work judgment: he may still create something nobody watches | EP002 | Reliable |
| DEV-06 | Settlement method | Mission rewards settle once, upon mission completion; they are not advanced | EP001 | Reliable |
| DEV-07 | Failure condition | Mission failure revokes all granted capabilities and forcibly unbinds the device | EP001 | Reliable |

**Defense against rule inflation**: This table was written in full and accepted before EP001. Any later term must first be added to this table and accepted by the creator before being disclosed in the story. Adding a rescue term because the writing is stuck counts as a defect even if procedurally compliant.

**DEV-04 is the core design of this project**: In the source novel, the device is purely beneficial and carries no cost. This project gives it a single cost—**he must accept a reputation that does not belong to him and cannot correct the misattribution**. This cost need not be disclosed in EP001, but it has been in force since EP001.

## V. Character Goals, Leverage, Red Lines, and Relationship Pressure

| Character | Goal | Leverage | Red line | Relationship pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jiang Chen | Turn this blank space into his own battlefield | All the experience of his previous life; execution abilities granted by the device | Rely on the work, not explanations | The more successful he becomes, the deeper the misattribution and the less able he is to correct it |
| Zhou Bosen | Stop the cultural troupe's publicity results from ranking last | Authority and connections as deputy troupe commander | Never let a soldier under his command take the blame for the unit | Tension between protecting Jiang Chen and answering to superiors |
| Li Lin | Ensure that those who never returned are remembered | A veteran's credibility among military enthusiasts | Never accept treating military affairs as entertainment | He vouches for a stranger who will never know |
| Zhong Jiajia | Produce reporting that people genuinely watch | The military newspaper's platform; her family background | Never use veterans as raw material | Part of her drive comes from somewhere she does not fully understand herself |
| Zhang Yaozu | Protect the unit's and his personal evaluation results | The troupe commander's final authority | No evident red line; follows the prevailing wind | Shifts responsibility when risk appears and claims credit when honor arrives |

## VI. What Ends This Engine

**The moment the misattribution is resolved.** The engine runs on “he takes something that is not his and creates a work worthy of it.” Once the world knows the source of his abilities, or he no longer needs the device, the engine stops. This project places that endpoint after Episode 24; it is not paid off this season.

## VII. Visual, Sound, Scene-Scale, and Content Boundaries

- **Visuals**: Semi-realistic generated imagery. Everyday spaces are small and worn; military spaces are large and severe. The contrast itself is thematic.
- **Sound**: The device's voice is the only sound in the entire series without spatial reverberation—it belongs to no room.
- **Scene scale**: Everyday scenes primarily use a single room and are heavily reused; large-scale scenes are concentrated in EP002, EP004, and EP010.
- **Content boundary**: Fictional unit designations and nonrealistic insignia; no identifiable marks of real units. Sacrifice and casualties are conveyed through old objects and narration, without graphic presentation.
